# Radial Modes of Hot Neutrino-Trapped Hybrid Stars 

Peter B. Rau a, Andrea Sabatucci b, Armen Sedrakian b,c 

> aColumbia Astrophysics Laboratory, Columbia University, New York, 10027, NY, USA
> bInstitute of Theoretical Physics, University of Wrocław, Wrocław, 50-204, Poland
> cFrankfurt Institute for Advanced Studies, Frankfurt am Main, 60438, Germany

Abstract 

We study the fundamental radial oscillation mode of hot hadronic and hybrid stars under conditions relevant to proto-neutron stars and neutron-star merger remnants. The stellar backgrounds are constructed from finite-temperature equations of state with a covariant density functional for hadronic matter and three-flavor 2SC quark matter at high density. We consider two isentropic configurations in both neutrino-transparent matter and with trapped electron neutrinos at fixed electron-lepton fraction. For trapped matter we compare a Gibbs-type construction with global electron-lepton-number conservation to a locally constrained Maxwell-type prescription; in both cases the fixed-entropy stellar equation of state contains an extended coexistence region and no sharp interface requiring conversion rate-dependent junction conditions. We find that the fundamental radial frequency follows the corresponding purely hadronic sequence up to the onset of deconfinement and then decreases relative to it. Neutrino trapping shifts this dynamical signature to larger stellar masses and central pressures, reflecting the delayed appearance of quark matter. The Maxwell-type prescription moves the onset of deconfinement to still higher central pressure and produces a more rapid decrease of the mode frequency near the stability limit. We quantify the deconfinement effect through the fractional frequency shift between hybrid and hadronic stars at equal gravitational mass. These results show that the fundamental radial mode provides a sensitive dynamical probe of the interplay among entropy, lepton trapping, phase-transition construction, and quark deconfinement in hot compact stars. 

Keywords: Compact stars, Asteroseismology, Nuclear equation of state 

1. Introduction 

Whether quark degrees of freedom are realized in the interi-ors of compact stars is a major unresolved issue in the study of strongly interacting matter at supranuclear density. Mature neu-tron stars predominantly constrain cold, neutrino-transparent matter close to weak equilibrium, with temperatures well be-low the characteristic Fermi scales. The conditions encoun-tered in proto-neutron stars formed after core collapse and in binary neutron-star merger remnants are markedly different (for reviews, see Lattimer and Prakash (2016); Foucart (2023); Se-drakian et al. (2023)). Shock and compression heating can raise the temperature to several tens of MeV, while the large neu-trino opacity delays deleptonization and the relaxation toward neutrino-free beta equilibrium Prakash et al. (1997); Pons et al. 

(1999); Espino et al. (2024); Pajkos and Most (2025). The ther-modynamic state must therefore be specified by thermal and compositional variables in addition to the baryon density, for example, by the entropy per baryon and the total lepton frac-tion in neutrino-opaque matter. Through their influence on the chemical potentials and phase-equilibrium conditions, finite en-tropy and trapped leptons can shift the onset of deconfinement, modify the extent of the coexistence region, and change the strength of the hadron–quark transition along astrophysically relevant trajectories. Radial oscillations provide a particularly direct measure of the dynamical response of a spherical stellar configuration. The lowest, nodeless radial mode determines stability against spher-ically symmetric perturbations, while its frequency is sensitive to the compressibility of the stellar interior. Radial pulsations of hot proto-neutron stars were studied in Gondek et al. (1997), where finite entropy and neutrino trapping were shown to mod-ify the mode spectrum. More recently, Sun et al. (2021) in-vestigated radial oscillations of cold hybrid stars containing a Gibbs-constructed hadron–quark mixed phase and found that the fundamental mode vanishes at the maximum-mass configu-ration, consistent with the usual turning-point stability criterion. The effects of entropy and fixed lepton fraction on the radial modes of isentropic proto-neutron stars without quark phase were subsequently studied in Sun et al. (2025), which showed that both thermal effects and neutrino trapping can substantially modify the mode frequencies and their evolution toward the cold neutron-star limit. In hybrid stars with a sharp first-order phase boundary, the radial-mode problem acquires an additional dependence on the conversion rate between the two phases. If phase conversion is slow or rapid compared with the oscillation period, different interface junction conditions apply and the stability boundary need not coincide in the two limits Haensel et al. (1989); Pereira 

et al. (2018); Gonçalves and Lazzari (2022); Rau and Sedrakian (2023); Rau and Salaben (2023). The present work studies radial modes of hot hybrid stars with an extended hadron–quark coexistence region, allowing arXiv:2610.07241v1 [astro-ph.HE] 5 Oct 2026 us to isolate how neutrino trapping shifts the dynamical sig-nature of deconfinement. For that purpose we use the finite-temperature hybrid equations of state developed in Sabatucci and Sedrakian (2026), in which the phase transition along the relevant isentropic trajectories is represented by an extended co-existence region rather than by a sharp density discontinuity. In neutrino-trapped matter, local electric neutrality and global con-servation of electron-lepton number lead to a Gibbs-type mixed phase whose pressure varies continuously with density. The same framework provides continuous isentropic hybrid equa-tions of state for the neutrino-transparent sequences considered below. Consequently, there is no isolated hadron–quark inter-face at which the slow- and rapid-conversion junction conditions of sharp-interface models must be imposed. Our purpose is to determine how this equilibrium mixed phase manifests itself in the fundamental radial mode of hot stars. We compare purely hadronic and hybrid configurations at fixed en-tropy per baryon, and with and without neutrino trapping. The central questions are whether the onset of deconfinement pro-duces an identifiable change in the radial-mode spectrum and how that change is displaced by neutrino trapping. We addition-ally introduce a simple dimensionless measure of the dynam-ical effect of deconfinement, namely the fractional difference between the fundamental frequencies of hybrid and hadronic configurations at the same gravitational mass. The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 summarizes the stellar equations of state and equilibrium backgrounds. Sec-tion 3 reviews the relativistic radial-mode equations and spec-ifies the treatment of the mixed phase. Our numerical results are presented in Sec. 4, and Sec. 5 summarizes the main con-clusions. 

2. Hot hadronic and hybrid-star backgrounds 

We employ the same finite-temperature equations of state as in Sabatucci and Sedrakian (2026) and therefore give only the information required for the oscillation analysis. The hadronic sector is described by the DDLS family of covariant density functionals. In the present work we use the reference DDLS50 parametrization, with symmetry-energy slope Lsym = 50 MeV and Qsat = 400 MeV Tsiopelas et al. (2024). The high-density phase is modeled within a three-flavor Nambu–Jona-Lasinio description with repulsive vector interactions and two-flavor color superconductivity (2SC). For trapped neutrinos, baryon number and electron-lepton number are conserved globally during phase coexistence, while each phase is locally electrically neutral. The quark volume fraction χ changes continuously through the mixed region, and the pressure is a continuous function of the density. As shown in Sabatucci and Sedrakian (2026), trapped neutrinos stabilize the hadronic phase against deconfinement and shift the onset of the mixed phase toward higher density. The main purpose of the present work is to determine how this thermodynamic delay is reflected in the radial-mode spectrum. In neutrino-transparent matter, where only baryon number remains as an independent globally conserved charge, the phase transition is described by a Maxwell-type construction. For trapped matter, we additionally construct a locally constrained Maxwell-type prescription for comparison with the globally constrained Gibbs-type construction. Such nonequilibrium effects are distinct from the sharp-interface slow/rapid conversion problem and are beyond the scope of the present calculation.

The radial perturbation equations are therefore inte-grated continuously through this region, without imposing a sharp-interface conversion condition. At masses below the onset of the mixed phase, the hadronic and hybrid calculations describe the same physical stellar core and their frequencies coincide, up to numerical accuracy. For the equilibrium mixed-phase re-sponse considered here, we find that the zero of the fundamental mode coincides, within numerical accuracy, with the maximum-mass turning point of the corresponding stellar sequence, in accordance with the Bardeen–Thorne–Meltzer (BTM) criterion Thorne (1966); Bardeen et al. This behavior should be contrasted with hybrid stars containing a genuine sharp first-order interface, for which nonequilibrium conversion physics can alter the junction conditions and permit stability beyond an ordinary turning point Rau and Sedrakian (2023); Rau and Salaben (2023). In the present equilibrium coexistence con-struction, no such sharp-interface degree of freedom is present. The relevant future nonequilibrium question is instead whether the phase fraction and composition can readjust on a timescale shorter than the radial oscillation period. The Maxwell-type sequences provide an additional consis-tency check. At fixed temperature, the underlying Maxwell construction contains the usual density discontinuity at the co-existence pressure. This discontinuity is absent, however, from the fixed-entropy stellar equation of state. Along an isentrope 5the system traverses the coexistence line while the phase fraction changes continuously from zero to unity; the temperature and pressure therefore vary across the coexistence interval, and both the baryon and energy densities remain continuous. The radial perturbation equations can consequently be integrated contin-uously through this region without imposing a sharp-interface conversion condition. Using the same equilibrium adiabatic re-sponse as for the stellar background, we find that the fundamen-tal mode again vanishes at the maximum-mass turning point, in accordance with the BTM criterion. Thus the slow/rapid-conversion ambiguity familiar from sharp-interface models is not operative for the isentropic Maxwell-type sequences con-sidered here. 

5. Discussion and conclusions 

We have calculated the fundamental radial oscillation mode of hot hadronic and 2SC hybrid stars for isentropic matter with S =

1 and 2, both in the neutrino-transparent regime and with trapped electron neutrinos at YLe = 0.4. The equations of state are those developed in Sabatucci and Sedrakian (2026), allowing the dynamical consequences of the same finite-temperature phase-transition physics to be studied without introducing an additional phenomenological stellar model. The first result is that deconfinement leaves a visible imprint on the fundamental radial mode even when the phase transition is represented by an extended coexistence region. The hybrid and hadronic mode frequencies coincide while the stellar center remains in the hadronic phase. Once the mixed phase appears, the hybrid frequency softens relative to the purely hadronic value, and the difference grows toward the high-mass end of the stellar sequence. The fractional frequency shift in Eq. (11) provides a direct quantitative measure of this effect. The second result is that neutrino trapping displaces the mode frequency softening in hybrid stars toward larger mass and cen-tral pressure. This behavior has a transparent thermodynamic origin. A fixed electron-lepton fraction increases the electron and proton abundances, stabilizing the hadronic phase against quark deconfinement and shifting the transition to higher den-sity Sabatucci and Sedrakian (2026). The radial spectrum there-fore carries a dynamical record of the same delayed transition: the hadronic and hybrid frequencies remain close over a larger portion of the stellar sequence before separating. Entropy changes the absolute mode frequencies substantially, but it does not remove this basic pattern. The comparison of 

S = 1 and S = 2 sequences shows that thermal structure and de-confinement affect the mode spectrum in distinguishable ways: entropy changes the overall frequency scale, whereas the ap-pearance of the mixed phase produces a differential softening relative to a hadronic star under the same thermodynamic condi-tions. This motivates the use of ∆ f (M) rather than the absolute value of f0 alone as a diagnostic of the phase transition. For trapped matter, the comparison between the globally con-strained Gibbs-type construction and the locally constrained Maxwell-type prescription shows that the treatment of lepton-number conservation also affects the location of the dynamical signature. The Maxwell-type prescription shifts the onset of quark-containing matter to higher central pressure and produces a more rapid decrease of the fundamental frequency near the stability limit. Nevertheless, because the stellar trajectories are isentropic, both constructions contain an extended coexistence interval and lead to continuous equilibrium equations of state. The present calculation also clarifies the relation to earlier studies of radial stability in sharp-interface hybrid stars. Be-cause the isentropic coexistence regions used here are continu-ous, no slow, rapid or intermediate (reactive)-conversion junc-tion condition is required. This eliminates the interface ambi-guity that can qualitatively alter radial stability when a density discontinuity is present Rau and Sedrakian (2023); Rau and Salaben (2023). It does not, however, imply that all conversion dynamics are irrelevant. If the phase fraction, weak composi-tion, or lepton content cannot follow a millisecond radial oscil-lation adiabatically, the effective adiabatic index of the mixed phase may differ from the equilibrium one used here. Extending the present mixed-phase calculation to a frozen-composition or finite-relaxation response is an interesting problem for future work. Radial modes of a nonrotating spherical star do not radiate gravitational waves. Rather, their significance is that they char-acterize the dynamical response and stability timescale of hot compact stars and expose a sensitivity to deconfinement. In realistic proto-neutron stars or merger remnants, rotation, non-spherical dynamics, and mode coupling can connect radial-like compression dynamics to observable fluid motion. Determining whether the phase-transition-induced shifts found here survive in such environments will require multidimensional calcula-tions. In summary, the fundamental radial mode provides a simple dynamical diagnostic of phase transition to hot quark matter, i.e., finite temperature/entropy deconfinement in proto-neutron stars and binary mergers. The appearance of a 2SC-containing mixed phase softens the mode relative to a purely hadronic star, while neutrino trapping delays the onset of this softening in mass and central pressure. The effect is naturally quantified by a fractional hadronic–hybrid frequency difference at fixed mass. These results establish a direct connection between the thermodynamic delay of deconfinement in lepton-rich matter and the radial dynamics of hot hybrid stars.
