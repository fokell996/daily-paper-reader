# Spin wandering of an accreting neutron star near torque balance: implications for gravitational wave searches 

A. Melatos 1,2

amelatos@unimelb.edu.au 

ABSTRACT 

Torque balance between gravitational radiation reaction and magnetocentrifugal accretion is one reason why accreting neutron stars rotate slower than centrifu-gal break-up. Random processes in the accretion disk, such as Rayleigh-Taylor instabilities and flicker noise, drive the spin frequency f∗ and gravitational wave frequency fgw ∝ f∗ to wander stochastically around their torque balance values. Here it is shown theoretically, in terms of an idealized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck model, that the standard deviation of fgw is given by σfgw (h0) ∝ fgw h0DβT 1/2, or equiva-lently σfgw (FX) ∝ f 1/2gw F 1/2X DβT 1/2, where h0 is the characteristic wave strain, FX

is the X-ray flux, D is the source distance, and β and T are the fractional ampli-tude and autocorrelation time-scale of the stochastic torque. One can use σfgw (h0)and σfgw (FX) to check the physical consistency of a detection candidate in a grav-itational wave search by comparing with σfgw observed. Spin wandering impacts search design, because it sets the maximum time interval max( Tcoh ), during which the signal may be treated as coherent. The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck calculation pre-dicts 10 −1 . max( Tcoh )/(1 day) . 10 3, with 3 × 10 −2 . βT 1/2/(1 s 1/2) . 4, for Rayleigh-Taylor instabilities and flicker noise. The predicted max( Tcoh ) is shorter typically than the year-long observation runs of audio-band interferometers op-erating today, emphasizing the utility of semi-coherent search algorithms, which divide the data into multiple coherent segments of duration max( Tcoh ).

That is, brighter X-ray sources are better targets, all else being equal. Torque balance is consistent with the observation, that accreting neutron stars rotate significantly slower than the cen-trifugal break-up limit for many plausible equations of state (Cook et al. 1994; Priymak et al. 2011), with f∗ . 1 kHz measured from X-ray pulsations and thermonuclear burst oscillations (Chakrabarty et al. 2003). Many semi-coherent algorithms are in use, e.g. based on cross-correlation methods, double Fourier transforms, or hidden Markov models (Dhurandhar et al. – 3 – 2008; Goetz & Riles 2011; Suvorova et al. 2016, 2017). Several semi-coherent searches using LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA data have targeted accreting neutron stars, e.g. the low-mass X-ray binaries Scorpius X −1 and XTE J1751 −305 (LIGO Scientific Collaboration et al. 2017a,b; Meadors et al. 2017; Ligo Scientific Collaboration et al. 2022c,a; Vargas & Melatos 2025) and 20 accreting millisecond X-ray pulsars (Middleton et al. 2020; Ligo Scientific Collaboration et al. 2022b). An important challenge when doing a semi-coherent search for an accreting neutron star is to set Tcoh optimally to match the true spin wandering time-scale, with Tcoh ≈ (2 σfgw )−1;see Figure 13 in Melatos et al. (2021) for example. If the source pulsates persistently in X-rays, one estimates σfgw empirically by measuring f∗ and assuming that the gravitational-wave-emitting quadrupole is locked approximately to the X-ray-emitting crust. Many sys-tems, however, including high-priority targets with relatively high FX like Scorpius X −1, do not pulsate in X-rays at all or do so intermittently. One is then obliged to estimate 

σfgw theoretically. For example, the published searches for Scorpius X −1 referenced in the previous paragraph assume 10 ≤ Tcoh /(1 day) ≤ 70 based on analytic calculations assuming a randomly kicked rotor (Sammut et al. 2014) or Monte Carlo simulations of torque fluctu-ations calibrated against the Rossi X-ray Timing Explorer (RXTE) light curve of Scorpius X−1 (Mukherjee et al. 2018). In this paper, we generalize previous theoretical calculations of σfgw by incorporating mean reversion caused by the gravitational radiation reaction and magnetocentrifugal ac-cretion torques. Specifically, we model the time-dependent spin of the neutron star, f∗(t), in terms of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, linearized about the torque balance equilibrium, which is driven by a stochastic torque of arbitrary physical origin with idealized white-noise statistics. The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck model has been applied successfully to RXTE timing data for 24 accretion-powered pulsars in the Small Magellanic Cloud to measure their mag-netic moments, radiative efficiencies, and magnetocentrifugal fastness parameters (Melatos et al. 2023; O’Leary et al. 2024; Christodoulou et al. 2025; O’Leary et al. 2025). It has also been applied to study torque reversals and retrograde accretion in 4U 1626 −67 by an-alyzing timing data from the Compton Gamma-Ray Observatory and Fermi Gamma-Ray Space Telescope (O’Leary et al. 2026). It builds on pioneering empirical work modeling spin wandering as a Wiener process (i.e. without mean reversion) by Mukherjee et al. (2018) and complements recent studies of the secular behavior of f∗(t) involving torque balance, magnetic mountains, and crustal breakage (Pagliaro et al. 2025). The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we formulate the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck model and calculate σfgw in terms of properties of the stochastic torque. In Section 3, we estimate σfgw approximately in two illustrative and representative astrophysical scenarios: accretion disk flicker noise (Lyubarskii 1997), and Rayleigh-Taylor instabilities at the disk-magnetosphere boundary – 4 – (Romanova et al. 2002, 2003; Romanova & Owocki 2015; Blinova et al. 2016). In Section 4, we calculate Tcoh and discuss the implications for semi-coherent gravitational wave searches targeting accreting neutron stars. 

2. Ornstein-Uhlenbeck model of spin wandering 

We start by formulating and solving the linear Ornstein-Uhlenbeck model for small-amplitude spin wandering around magnetorotational equilibrium, driven by an arbitrary stochastic torque with white-noise statistics. In Section 2.1, we write down a rotational equation of motion and justify the idealized form of the net torque astrophysically. In Section 2.2, we linearize the equation of motion and solve it for the stationary probability density function (PDF) of the angular velocity fluctuations. In Section 2.3, we convert the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck solution into a formula relating σfgw , the standard deviation of the gravitational wave signal frequency, and h0, the characteristic gravitational wave strain. The formula σfgw (h0) is useful in practice, when a semi-coherent gravitational wave search yields an above-threshold candidate, for which h0 is measureed. One can then test whether the implied value of σfgw is consistent with the amount of spin wandering actually observed in the search. In Section 2.4, we convert the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck solution into an equivalent formula relating σfgw and the X-ray flux FX (or equivalently the mass accretion rate ˙Ma). The formula σfgw (FX) is useful in practice, before a semi-coherent search is performed, because it predicts theoretically the optimal coherence time-scale Tcoh , as explained in Section 4.

3. Stochastic torque 

In general, it is hard to predict the noise amplitude σ from first principles. For example, no predictive theory exists to describe timing noise in an isolated radio pulsar, e.g. due to stochastic processes in its superfluid interior (Cordes & Downs 1985; Jones 1990; Melatos & Link 2014). In accreting systems, however, spin wandering is thought to be driven predom-inantly by fluctuations in the hydromagnetic accretion torque, making it possible to relate 

σ to Na(Ω eq ) and hence Ngw (Ω eq ) = −Na(Ω eq ) (torque balance). In Section 3.1, we relate σ

to the autocorrelation time-scale T of Na. In Section 3.2, we reexpress σfgw in (9) and (11) in terms of T instead of σ. We then evaluate T for two specific stochastic processes in the literature: Rayleigh-Taylor instabilities at the disk-magnetosphere boundary (Section 3.3), and disk flicker noise (Section 3.4).

3.3. Rayleigh-Taylor instabilities 

Global, three-dimensional, magnetohydrodynamic simulations of disk accretion onto a rapidly rotating, magnetized, compact object reveal, that the boundary between the accre-tion disk and magnetosphere is unstable (Romanova et al. 2002, 2003; Kulkarni & Romanova 2008, 2013; Blinova et al. 2016); see Lai (2014) and Romanova & Owocki (2015) for compre-hensive reviews. The unstable dynamics involve a complicated mixture of Kelvin-Helmholtz modes, driven by the shear between the Keplerian disk and corotating magnetosphere for 

ωa 6 = 1; and Rayleigh-Taylor modes, which occur because the magnetosphere acts as a lighter fluid supporting the heavier disk under gravity (Arons & Lea 1976). Nonlinear feedback loops regulate the instabilities, so that their dynamics are mean-reverting and approximately cyclic, – 12 – i.e. self-healing. 8 An instability grows at one or more locations on the disk-magnetosphere boundary, one or more transient magnetic channels open up which connect the disk to the star, “fingers” of disk material break through the magnetosphere and strike the stellar sur-face, then accretion replenishes the mass reservoir at the boundary, the magnetic channels shut, and equilibrium is restored temporarily (D’Angelo & Spruit 2010, 2012; D’Angelo 2017). With respect to Rayleigh-Taylor instabilities in particular, Blinova et al. (2016) iden-tified three distinct accretion regimes: stable (two stationary magnetospheric funnel flows and hotspots), chaotic unstable (multiple migrating funnels and hotspots), and ordered un-stable (two migrating funnels and hotspots), corresponding to ωa & 0.6, 0 .45 . ωa . 0.6, and 

ωa . 0.45 respectively. A Kalman filter analysis of RXTE pulse timing data for 24 accretion-powered pulsars in the Small Magellanic Cloud confirms observationally the existence of the regimes (O’Leary et al. 2025). Motivated thus, we focus on Rayleigh-Taylor instabilities in this section, while noting that other complicated mechanisms operate simultaneously, e.g. an outflowing wind (Matt & Pudritz 2005, 2008). Simulations like those referenced above demonstrate, that the accretion torque fluctuates by a factor of order unity about its mean value, i.e. one has β ∼ 1. 3.4. Disk flicker noise

5. Conclusion 

Accreting neutron stars are high-priority targets for continuous wave searches with audio-band, long-baseline, gravitational wave interferometers (Riles 2023; Wette 2023). Un-der the assumption that the gravitational radiation reaction torque balances the magneto-centrifugal accretion torque, higher priority is accorded to objects with higher X-ray flux FX,because the characteristic gravitational wave strain scales as h0 ∝ F 1/2X f −1/2

However, random processes in the accretion disk cause the signal frequency fgw ∝ f∗ to wander stochastically, especially when FX is rel-atively high (Watts et al. 2008). Spin wandering limits the maximum duration max( Tcoh ) of a coherent block in a semi-coherent continuous wave search, because the standard deviation of the wandering signal frequency, σfgw , must stay below the frequency bin width (2 Tcoh )−1

to track the signal successfully. In this paper, we calculate σfgw and hence max( Tcoh ) from first principles in the context of an idealized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck model of spin wandering. The model generalizes previous pioneering analyses (Sammut et al. 2014; Mukherjee et al. 2018; Pagliaro et al. 2025) by including mean reversion, as the system fluctuates around torque balance. It is shown (see Appendix B) that the radiation reaction torque dominates the mean reversion rate γ
