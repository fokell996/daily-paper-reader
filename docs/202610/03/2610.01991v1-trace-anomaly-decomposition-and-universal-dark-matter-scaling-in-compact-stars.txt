We investigate how dark matter (DM) admixture modifies the conformal properties and phase structure of dense neutron-star matter within a self-consistent single-fluid framework, with the global DM fraction Fχ = Nχ/N B fixing the local relation nχ = FχnB . We derive an exact decomposition of the total trace anomaly, ∆ tot , into microscopic contributions. For collider-motivated Higgs-portal benchmarks, explicit Higgs, vector-mediator, and contact-interaction contributions are negligible, while heavy nonrelativistic DM has an intrinsic trace anomaly close to the nonrelativistic limit, ∆χ ≃ 1/3. Consequently, the DM rest-mass energy fraction dominates the DM-induced modification of ∆ tot , producing a smooth upward shift of up to ∼ 0.1 for Fχ ∼ 0.2%. In the pressureless, comoving heavy-WIMP regime, we further identify a universal dark-sector scaling governed by the mass-loading parameter λ = Fχmχ/m N : numerical calculations with different ( Fχ, m χ) pairs at fixed λ exhibit overlapping trace-anomaly, sound-speed, and mass–radius responses for a given baryonic equation of state. In hybrid stars, DM leaves the coexistence pressure and chemical potential essentially unchanged, whereas first-order hadron–quark deconfinement produces sharp discontinuities in the squared sound speed c2 

> s

, ∆ tot , and ∆ tot − ∆B . The combined softening substantially reduces the maximum stellar mass, placing Fχ ∼ 0.2% in tension with the observed 2 M⊙ neutron stars. These results show that the trace anomaly and its density evolution provide a sensitive diagnostic for distinguishing smooth DM-induced modifications of dense matter from genuine first-order deconfinement. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

Neutron stars (NSs) are composed of ultra-dense nuclear matter [1], with typical masses of about 1 .4 M⊙ and radii of about 13 km, supported against gravitational collapse by the pressure of strongly interacting, degenerate matter [2, 3]. Recent observations indicate masses approaching 2 M⊙ and radii of about 12 km [4, 5]. At sufficiently high densities, additional degrees of freedom, including hyperons, kaons, and ∆ resonances, may emerge [6–8], while deconfinement to quark matter may lead to hybrid stars [9–12]. Although such exotic degrees of freedom have not been observationally established, their possible presence remains an open question, motivating theoretical studies constrained by multimessenger observations [13]. The study of dark matter (DM) in neutron stars (NSs) has attracted growing interest because of its unknown na-ture and largely unconstrained interactions with baryonic matter (BM) [14, 15]. The lack of a confirmed terrestrial signal [16, 17], despite strong cosmological [18, 19] and astrophysical [20–22] evidence, motivates NSs as comple-mentary probes. Two main frameworks are commonly employed: a two-fluid approach, where DM and BM obey separate EOSs and interact only gravitationally through the two-fluid TOV equations [23–25], and a single-fluid approach, where the two sectors are coupled through non-gravitational interactions [26–29]. In neutron dark decay, a related single-fluid scenario, chemical equilib-rium μn = μχ determines the DM abundance, which

can generate substantial DM fractions and soften the EOS, requiring additional repulsive interactions to satisfy massive-NS constraints [30–32]. In these scenarios, DM can alter stellar structure and increase compactness, po-tentially shifting the onset of exotic degrees of freedom, such as hyperons, to lower densities [33, 34]. The focus of this manuscript is to quantitatively deter-mine how DM modifies the conformal and phase structure of NSs within a self-consistent single-fluid framework, and to establish whether its thermodynamic signatures can be distinguished from those of genuine hadron–quark decon-finement. Previous work has shown that DM can lower the critical stellar mass for quark-matter formation in a two-fluid, gravitationally coupled framework [35], while its impact on conformality has been examined separately in neutron-dark-decay scenarios [36] and in interacting DM-admixed matter at fixed entropy per baryon [37]. These studies establish that DM can modify the high-density thermodynamic response, but the connection between explicit DM–BM interactions, the trace anomaly, and the signatures of deconfinement has not been systematically quantified. Here we address this gap using a self-consistent single-fluid framework based on the global DM fraction Fχ =

Nχ/N B , introduced in [38] and subsequently applied in [39], which, for a thermalized and comoving DM com-ponent, imposes the local closure nχ = FχnB . Within a Higgs-portal model supplemented by a massive vector mediator, we derive an exact energy-density-weighted de-composition of the normalized trace anomaly introduced in [40, 41], isolating the contributions of BM, DM, scalar, and vector-interaction sectors and quantifying how DM modifies the approach to conformality. We then extend the analysis to hybrid stars with a genuine first-order arXiv:2610.01991v1 [nucl-th] 1 Oct 2026 2hadron–quark transition, using two modified density-dependent quark-mass (MDDQM) model parametriza-tions, to determine whether DM alters the coexistence conditions or instead leaves the phase boundary essen-tially unchanged while modifying the thermodynamic and stellar signatures of deconfinement. We connect the mi-croscopic DM energy-pressure asymmetry to the trace anomaly and use it to discriminate continuously induced DM modifications from the discontinuous thermodynamic response of deconfinement. In the pressureless, comoving heavy-WIMP regime, we further identify a universal dark-sector scaling governed by the mass-loading parameter λ = Fχmχ/m N , and ver-ify that distinct ( Fχ, m χ) combinations numerically at fixed λ produce overlapping trace-anomaly, sound-speed, and mass–radius responses for a given BM EOS. This establishes that the leading DM-induced stellar response depends on the dark-sector parameters primarily through 

λ, while retaining the model dependence of the underly-ing BM EOS, and connects the microscopic DM energy– pressure asymmetry to a unified diagnostic of smooth DM-induced modifications and genuine first-order decon-finement. The paper is organized as follows. In section II, we in-troduce the theoretical framework, including the hadronic, DM, and quark-matter models, as well as the hybrid-star construction. In section III, we provide a general discus-sion of the trace-anomaly decomposition. In section III.1, we discuss the conformal dynamics, while in section III.2 we introduce the exact trace-anomaly decomposition. A quantitative assessment of the dark-sector contributions is presented in section III.3. In section IV, we introduce a universal scaling relation in the pressureless, comoving heavy-WIMP limit. We present our results and analysis in section V, followed by the main conclusions in section VI.

VI. CONCLUSION 

We demonstrate that a small DM admixture with NS matter can leave a distinct and quantifiable imprint on the conformal structure of the dense matter without triggering a phase transition of its own. The trace-anomaly decom-position, ∆ tot = P 

> a

wa∆a ≃ (1 −wχ)∆ B +wχ∆χ, isolates the underlying mechanism. For the collider-motivated Higgs-portal benchmarks considered, the explicit Higgs, vector-mediator, and contact-interaction contributions are negligible. Instead, the dominant effect arises from the DM rest-mass energy density weighting a nonrelativis-tic component with ∆ χ ≃ 1/3, reflecting the pressure-less scenario. Owing to the mass hierarchy mχ ≫ mN ,even a particle fraction Fχ = Nχ/N B ∼ 0.2% can pro-duce a large upward shift of ∆ tot by ∼ 0.1. This en-hancement is generic throughout the heavy-WIMP regime (mχ ≳ 5 GeV), whereas ∆ χ departs from 1 /3 only for sub-GeV DM, where relativistic effects become important. In the pressureless, comoving heavy-WIMP limit de-fined by the conditions Ptot ≃ PB and nχ = FχnB , these results further reduce the leading dark-sector dependence to the single parameter λ = Fχmχ/m N . Numerical calcu-lations, including the microscopic DM–BM interactions, with distinct ( Fχ, m χ) pairs at fixed λ show overlapping normalized trace-anomaly, sound-speed ratio, and mass– radius responses for a given BM EOS, demonstrating that the stellar response is insensitive to Fχ and mχ separately. This agreement supports the proposed universal scaling with λ, as the inclusion of the microscopic interactions does not produce any visible deviation from the scaling relations as shown in fig. 5 within the numerical accuracy of our calculations. The EOS dependence reflects the underlying BM dynamics rather than a breakdown of the dark-sector scaling. The central implication of the universal DM scaling framework is that NS observables constrain the dark-sector mass loading, λ = Fχmχ/m N ,rather than mχ and Fχ separately. Consequently, within the regime considered here, NS observations alone cannot disentangle mχ from Fχ. Doing so requires independent information on either mχ or Fχ, which complementary as-trophysical or particle-physics observations could provide. This provides a compact parameterization for incorpo-rating NS observations into searches for nonrelativistic DM and establishes λ as the natural quantity for future observational and theoretical studies. In hybrid stars, DM-induced modifications and genuine deconfinement exhibit qualitatively distinct signatures. While DM leaves the hadron–quark coexistence conditions, characterized by Pcr and μcr , essentially unchanged, a first-order phase transition produces sharp discontinuities in 

c2 

> s

, ∆ tot , and ∆ tot − ∆B . Thus, WIMP-like DM primarily modifies the energy-density weighting and global stellar structure rather than the phase-coexistence conditions. The combined effects of DM-induced softening and decon-finement substantially increase compactness and suppress the maximum stellar mass, placing Fχ = 0 .2% in increas-ing tension with the existence of ∼ 2 M⊙ NSs. Taken together, the results establish a clear distinction between dark-sector mass loading, which produces a continuous and λ-controlled modification of the stellar properties, and genuine first-order deconfinement, which introduces discontinuities in the thermodynamic observables.
