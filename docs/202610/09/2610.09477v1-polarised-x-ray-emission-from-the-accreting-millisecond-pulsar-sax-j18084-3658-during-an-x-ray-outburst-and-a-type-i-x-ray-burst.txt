# Polarised X-ray emission from the accreting millisecond pulsar SAX J1808.4–3658 during an X-ray outburst and a type-I X-ray burst 

Alessandro Papitto, Alessandro Di Marco, Maria Cristina Baglio, Daniele Baldi Tomei, Caterina Ballocco, Giulia Illiano, Riccardo La Placa, Christian Malacaria, et al. 

(Full author list and a ffi liations details can be found after the references) 

Received October 8, 2026 

ABSTRACT 

Modelling the X-ray pulse profiles of accreting millisecond pulsars (AMSPs) is a key technique to constrain the masses and radii of neutron stars. To this end, measuring the properties of polarised X-rays provides crucial information on the system geometry. We present the significant detection by IXPE of linearly polarised 2–8 keV X-rays during the decay of the 2025 outburst of the 2.5 ms AMSP SAX J1808.4–3658, with an average polarisation degree of 2 .9 ± 0.5% and a polarisation angle of 46 ◦ ± 5◦, broadly consistent with the direction observed in the optical band. This level of polarisation is similar to that found in the only other source of this class that has been targeted so far. By using a pulse-phase timing solution obtained with IXPE and NuSTAR , which confirms the long-term evolution of the pulsar spin and orbit, we did not find any significant pulse phase-resolved variability in the Stokes parameters. Interpreting the polarised X-rays as originating from hot spots on the NS surface indicates they are almost aligned with the spin axis. We also report an indication for linearly polarised emission with a degree of 16% ± 6% (2–6 keV) during the peak of a type-I thermonuclear X-ray burst, with an angle almost orthogonal to that of the persistent emission. If confirmed, this level would exceed expectations for X-rays reflected by a thin accretion disc. 

Key words. magnetic fields – polarisation – pulsars: individual: SAX J1808.4–3658 – stars: neutron – X-rays: binaries 

1. Introduction 

Modelling the X-ray pulse profiles of rotation-powered mil-lisecond pulsars (MSPs) has recently provided crucial con-straints on the masses and radii of neutron stars (NS; see, e.g. Mauviard et al. 2025; Miller et al. 2026, and references therein). Accreting millisecond pulsars (AMSPs) o ff er an in-triguing alternative for the scope, as they are orders of magnitude brighter during X-ray outbursts (e.g., Poutanen & Gierli´ nski 2003; Salmi et al. 2018; Dorsman et al. 2025). Compton scatter-ing by hot ( ∼50 keV) electrons in the accretion columns above hot-spots on the NS surface polarises X-ray photons emitted by the surface (Viironen & Poutanen 2004; Bobrikova et al. 2023). Modelling the spin phase-resolved polarised radiation provides crucial constraints on geometrical parameters such as the sys-tem inclination ( i) and the co-latitudes of the emitting spots ( θ1

and θ2), potentially allowing us to obtain similar constraints on the masses and radii of AMSPs to those obtained for rotation-powered MSPs (Salmi et al. 2021, 2025). IXPE recently detected X-rays with a polarisation degree (PD) of a few per cent from the AMSP SRGA J144459.2–604207, suggesting a nearly aligned rotator (Papitto et al. 2025, P25 in the following) and large spots (Dorsman et al. 2026b). Here we present an analysis of an IXPE 

observation of the prototype of AMSPs, SAX J1808.4–3658 (Wijnands & van der Klis 1998), during its 2025 outburst. 

2. Observations and data analysis 

On 2025 August 6, the XB-NEWS optical monitoring program of low-mass X-ray binaries with the Las Cumbres Observatory (Russell et al. 2019) first detected the onset of a new outburst of SAX J1808.4–3658 (Russell et al. 2025), later confirmed by follow-up X-ray observations (Wang et al. 2025; Ballocco et al. 2025a). 2.1. Phase-resolved variability 

We obtained a preliminary timing solution using NuSTAR obser-vations performed during the outburst rise and simultaneously with IXPE (see purple dots in Fig. 1 and Appendix C for details). We decomposed the coherent signal thus recovered in IXPE I 

data with two harmonic components with fractional amplitudes of 8 .9 ± 0.2% and 0 .9 ± 0.2% (see the top panel of Fig. 4). By modelling the evolution of the fundamental harmonic’s phase measured in 5 ks-long intervals (see Fig. C.1), we obtained the parameters listed in Table 1. Including a quadratic component ¨φ significantly improved the description of the phase evolution (χ2 = 165 .5 for 126 d.o.f.), yet timing noise suggests caution in interpreting it as accretion-driven spin-up (Hartman et al. 2008). We searched for burst oscillations in 4 s-long intervals of type-I burst seen by IXPE . We did not find any significant oscillation in 4 s-long intervals of the type-I burst seen by IXPE , with up-per limits on the amplitude at the known frequency ν during the burst peak in the range 10%–18% (3 σ c. l.; 16%–24% within 5 Hz from ν) much coarser than the values seen from the source in the past ( ≃ 4% Bult et al. 2019). The measured spin and orbital parameters confirm the long-term evolution of SAX J1808.4–3658 (see Appendix C for de-tails). Despite the presence of large residuals, modelling the evo-lution of the measured epoch of passage of the pulsar at the as-cending node (purple dot in the top panel of Fig. 3) suggests a 

≈12 s amplitude oscillation with a ≈22 yr period, similar to pre-vious observations (Illiano et al. 2023, I23 hereafter). Similarly, the spin frequency keeps a long-term spin-down at an average rate of ˙ ν = (−1.21 ± 0.09) × 10 −15 Hz s −1, superimposed on a yearly oscillation due to the di ff erence between the best-fitting position and that determined by Hartman et al. (2008) (see bot-tom panel of Fig. 3 and Appendix C). We used the parameters listed in Table 1 to measure the phase-resolved variability of the 2–6 keV Stokes parameters dur-ing the T1 interval (see Fig. 4). We found no significant variabil-ity in the normalised Stokes parameters Q/I and U/I, as data points had a 3.9 per cent probability of being drawn from a con-stant distribution. Yet, dips of the PA appear at pulse phases 0 .35 and 0 .5.

For a moderately optically thick medium, as inferred for SAX J1808.4–3658 ( τ ≃ 1.4), the X-ray polarisation produced by Compton scattering in a plane-parallel slab over the surface is expected to align with the slab axis (Sunyaev & Titarchuk 1985; Poutanen & Svensson 1996; Bobrikova et al. 2023). Con-sequently, the polarisation vector oscillates around the direction of the NS spin axis, which in turn is aligned with the disc /binary orbital axis as a consequence of the prolonged accretion history that brought an AMSP such as SAX J1808.4–3658 to its cur-rent spin period. The average X-ray PA during the outburst de-cay (PA T1 = 1/2 arctan ( U/Q) = 46 ◦ ± 5◦) is broadly aligned with the optical linear polarisation, whose PA varies on sub-hour timescales but clusters around a mean of ∼ 30 ◦ both in 2019 (Baglio et al. 2020) and in 2025 outbursts, including the VLT ob-servations simultaneous with the IXPE coverage (Baglio et al., in prep.). Such rapid variability of the PA points to a jet origin, although scattering in the disc atmosphere and /or emission from the disc–magnetosphere interaction region may contribute to the polarised emission during the reflaring stage and would also be directed along the disc /binary axis. The indication of linearly polarised X-ray emission, PD BP =

(16 ± 6)% at the 2.5 σ level, at the peak of a type-I X-ray burst is striking. For comparison, P25 obtained an upper limit of 8.5% (90% c.l.) on the PD from 52 stacked bursts from SRGA J144459.2–604207. If confirmed by future observations, the in-dication of enhanced polarisation during the burst could be at-tributed to the scattering or reflection of the incident emission, driven by a change in either the angular profile of the incident emission or the geometry of the scatterer /reflector relative to the persistent state. For nearly isotropic burst emission, the reflection of NS radiation o ff the accretion disc can produce PD ∼ 3–4% for i = 60 ◦–80 ◦ (Lapidus & Sunyaev 1985; Tomaru et al. 2026). Alternatively, scattering o ff a disc wind can produce enhanced polarisation (Nitindala et al. 2025). In both cases, the polarisa-tion is expected to be aligned with the system’s symmetry axis. The observed polarisation angle provides an additional clue regarding the origin of the burst polarisation suggested by IXPE 

data. The PA at the burst peak is roughly orthogonal to that of the persistent emission (Fig. 2), suggesting a burst polarisation ori-entation orthogonal to the disc axis. Such an orientation can arise from scattering in a vertically extended structure or an extended scattering region inflated by the pressure of the burst emission. For instance, scattering in an accretion flow can produce a PD of up to ∼ 60% at i = 60 ◦ in the single-scattering regime. If the scattered component contributes roughly 20% of the total flux, a level expected for a low optical depth that avoids depolarisation from multiple scatterings, the resulting net PD is ∼ 12%, consis-tent with the observed value. An even higher PD could naturally arise if the incident source is obscured, as in the case of Cyg X-3 (Veledina et al. 2024a,b). Interestingly, reflection features at 1 and 6 .7 keV alongside reflection fractions reaching 200% rel-ative to the continuum have been reported during type-I X-ray bursts from SAX J1808.4–3658 (in’t Zand et al. 2013; Bult et al. 2019; Mandal et al. 2026). 

IXPE observations of two AMSPs have revealed linear X-ray polarisation at the 2%–3% level, with no detectable phase-resolved variability. This suggests a configuration with the emit-ting spots closely aligned with the spin axis. Future IXPE cam-paigns will determine whether aligned rotators are a common feature among AMSPs. While such a geometry limits the preci-sion with which pulsar parameters can be inferred from polar-isation data (Salmi et al. 2025), it underscores the transforma-tive potential of large e ff ective area X-ray detectors of upcoming missions like eXTP for using AMSPs to constrain the NS equa-tion of state (Zhang et al. 2025; Li et al. 2025). Future X-ray po-larimetric observations will also clarify the feasibility of probing the impact of type-I X-ray bursts on accretion inflows.

Appendix A: Data analysis 

IXPE observed SAX J1808.4–3658 for 918 ks between 2025 Au-gust 19 (04:54 UTC) and September 8 (01:41 UTC) within the Target of Opportunity general observer program Id. 04007699 (PI: A. Papitto). We processed clean data obtained with 

ixpeevtrecon (a tool distributed within HEASoft 6.36) accord-ing to the guide by Di Marco (2026). We rejected instrumental particle background with IXPE-background 1 (Di Marco et al. 2023) employing improved cuts required after April 2025 DU2 anomaly; this procedure is now requested regardless of the source brightness to avoid introducing possible systematic ef-fects (Di Marco 2026). We checked the alignment of the three DU images and removed sporadic solar flares characterised by a background rate exceeding 0.4 s −1. We extracted source photons from a circular region with a 60 ′′ radius around the source position, whereas background photons were extracted from an annular region encompassing the source position with inner and outer radii of 150 ′′ and 250 ′′ , respectively. We used the contributed script ixpe_protractor 2 along with the IXPE 

CALDB v. 20260424 to get a model-independent measurement of the Stokes parameters ( I, Q, U) (see Fig. 2 and A.1). Exclud-ing the type-I X-ray burst, the 2–8 keV source count rate did not exceed 4 s −1. During intervals T1, T3 and the type I burst, the source is bright enough that we did not subtract any background in the polarimetric analysis. Background subtraction was instead performed in the polarimetric analysis of T2 data, as the source flux had decreased to a level comparable with the X-ray back-ground (Di Marco et al. 2023). We also used ixpestartx 3 with the NEFF weighted scheme (Di Marco et al. 2022) to produce an-cillary and modulation response files, selected the response ma-trix 20211209_alpha075 and binned the resulting spectra to have at least 30 counts per bin. Similar to P25, when perform-ing the phase-resolved analysis presented in Sect. 2.1, we used the pcube algorithm without acceptance corrections to measure the Stokes parameters (expected to be normally distributed) in counts per second. 

NuSTAR performed Target of Opportunity observa-tions of SAX J1808.4–3658 during the outburst rise (Obs. Id. 81101311002) and simultaneously with IXPE (Id. 81101346002; see purple dots in Fig. 1). The latter took place in the context of an XMM–Newton GO program (Id. 96132, PI: Papitto). Due to visibility limitations, XMM–Newton 

(Obs. Id. 0961320101) observed after the end of the IXPE 

coverage. A detailed analysis of the XMM–Newton and NuS-TAR observations will be reported in a separate publication (Malacaria et al., in prep.). Here, we reduced NuSTAR data using the calibration version indx20260630, with the fine-clock correction file version 230. We adopted circular regions with a radius of 140 ′′ around and away from the source position to extract source and background photons in the 3–79 keV band, respectively. 

Swift /XRT (Burrows et al. 2005) observed SAX J1808.4– 3658 throughout the outburst, starting on 2025 August 9 (Obs. Id. 19987 and 30034 Ballocco et al. 2025a). Observations were collected in both Photon Counting (PC) and Windowed Timing (WT) modes, switching between them according to the source flux levels. Appendix B: Type-I X-ray burst observed by 

IXPE 

Panel (a) in Fig. B.1 shows the light curve of the type-I X-ray burst observed by IXPE on MJD 60917.62751. Modelling the light curve with an exponential decay indicates a time-scale of 17 .5 ± 0.8 s, possibly due to ignition in an almost pure He en-vironment. The decay features two dips at ∼ 25 s and ∼ 50 s after the burst peak, which resemble the complex evolution seen during a bright burst observed by NICER in 2019 and ascribed to the local Eddington limit of hydrogen and helium layers of the NS envelope (Bult et al. 2019). However, the IXPE spectrum observed during a 15 s-long interval centred at the peak could be well fitted by an absorbed blackbody with a temperature of 

kT bb = 1.55 ±0.08 keV and a radius of (12 .0±0.7) d2.7 km, which implies a bolometric flux of (1 .2 ± 0.3) × 10 −7 erg cm −2 s−1, i. e. roughly half that reported for the photometric radius expansion burst observed in 2019 (Bult et al. 2020). Panels (b) and (c) in Fig. B.1 show the evolution of the blackbody temperature and radius, respectively, in intervals of length ranging between 5 and 20 s. The e ff ective radius decreases steadily during the burst de-cay, but the statistics do not allow us to study the evolution with the time resolution required to assess the possibility of a pho-tospheric radius expansion (see, e.g., Fig. 3 in Bult et al. 2019). Panel (d) and (e) in the same figure show the PD and PA observed during the burst decay, whereas Figure B.2 shows the energy de-pendence of the burst properties observed during the same 15 s long interval centred at the burst peak considered earlier to mea-sure the peak burst energetics.

Appendix C: Spin and orbital evolution 

We first performed an epoch-folding search on the NuSTAR time series (Obs. Id. 81101311002 and 81101346002), after applying barycentric corrections using the position reported by Bult et al. (2020) and the JPL DE405 ephemeris. Appendix D: Modelling of Stokes parameters phase-resolved variability 

To give a preliminary modelling of the pulse profiles of the Stokes parameters ( I, Q, U) of SAX J1808.4–3658 (see Fig. 4), we used the same approach as P25. We described the dependence of the Stokes parameters on the pulse phase using the analyti-cal relations of Poutanen & Beloborodov (2006) and Poutanen (2020). We assumed two point-like spots at the surface of a spherical NS spinning at 401 Hz, with a mass of 1 .4 M⊙ and an equatorial radius of 12 km, emitting radiation with a spectrum described by a power-law index Γ = 1.8, an anisotropy parame-ter h and energy-independent polarisation p (see Fig. 9 of P25 for a sketch of the geometry).
