Abstract 

In neutron-star and dense quantum chromodynamics (QCD) physics, it is important to understand how baryonic matter evolves into quark matter at high density. A continuous hadron–quark crossover is one of the most attractive candi-dates because it can reconcile the comparatively soft equation of state around nuclear density with the rapid sti ff ening required to support massive neutron stars. Recent neutron-star observations motivate equations of state that sti ff en rapidly across the intermediate-density regime, while quarkyonic-matter descriptions feature a shell-like baryon mo-mentum distribution there. In this paper, we review our recent work on the microscopic description and interpretation of these phenomena by drawing an analogy with the Bose–Einstein condensate (BEC) to Bardeen–Cooper–Schrie ff er (BCS) crossover in ultracold Fermi gases and two-color QCD. As pairing fluctuations play a crucial role in the BEC– BCS crossover, we discuss the role of its three-body counterpart, that is, tripling fluctuations associated with baryon formation in the hadron–quark crossover. In terms of a phase-shift representation of tripling fluctuations, the interplay between a three-body bound-state pole and the scattering continuum suppresses low-momentum baryonic occupa-tion, generates a baryonic momentum shell, and reduces the density susceptibility, thereby producing a peak in the speed of sound. We review the demonstration of this mechanism in a one-dimensional three-component Fermi gas, its relativistic extension, and its connection to phenomenological quarkyonic equations of state. 

Keywords: hadron–quark crossover, quarkyonic matter, tripling fluctuations, ultracold Fermi gases, speed of sound, neutron-star equation of state 

1. Introduction 

The composition of strongly interacting matter at baryon densities above the nuclear saturation density is one of the most important problems in nuclear astrophysics and in dense quantum chromodynamics (QCD) physics. Microscopic nuclear calculations constrain the equation of state at relatively low density, whereas quark matter descriptions are expected to become appropriate at asymptotically high density. Neutron stars probe the intermediate region in which neither description is manifestly complete. The existence of neutron stars with masses near two solar masses requires a su ffi ciently sti ff equation of state [1, 2, 3], while radius and tidal-deformability measurements constrain how this sti ff ening can occur [4, 5]. A continuous hadron–quark crossover (HQC) provides one possible explanation of these observations [6]. In such a scenario, the relevant degrees of freedom continuously change from baryons to quarks without phase transitions. Moreover, quarkyonic matter description [7] o ff ers a useful realization of the HQC scenario, where deeply occupied momentum states are described predominantly by quarks and baryonic excitations remain near the Fermi surface. Indeed, phenomenological quarkyonic models predict both rapid sti ff ening and a nonmonotonic speed of sound [8]. The quarkyonic matter model has been further elaborated within the variational approach [9]. So far, extensions of quarkyonic matter to various models such as relativistic mean-field model [10], parity-doublet model [11], and quark-meson coupling model [12], have also been discussed. From the viewpoint of lattice simulations, two-color QCD provides a particularly transparent connection between dense gauge theory and crossover physics [13]. Because the fundamental representation of S U (2) is pseudoreal, color-singlet diquarks can be bosonic baryons. Dense matter may therefore evolve from a Bose–Einstein condensate arXiv:2610.01117v1 [nucl-th] 1 Oct 2026 (BEC) of tightly bound diquarks to a Bardeen-Cooper-Schrie ff er (BCS) regime (i.e., BEC–BCS crossover [14, 15]) in which quarks pair near a Fermi surface [16, 17]. Indeed, lattice simulations find an enhanced sound speed across this crossover [18, 19], making this theory a useful setting for examining the connection between composite formation and a sti ff equation of state. The BEC–BCS crossover has been realized in ultracold Fermi gases [20, 21, 22]. Two-color QCD makes the crossover analogy concrete, but ordinary three-color baryons are three-fermion composites rather than pairs. This review takes the fluctuation theory of ultracold Fermi gases as a starting point for a microscopic account of these signatures. In the BEC–BCS crossover, pairing fluctuations describe how two-fermion bound states evolve into cor-relations near a Fermi surface. For three-color fermions the analogous channel is instead fermionic: three-body correlations can persist as Cooper triples at finite density [23, 24, 25]. The tripling-fluctuation approach relates the baryonic momentum shell and the enhanced sound speed to the interplay between a three-body bound-state pole and the scattering continuum [26, 27]. We review this mechanism in a one-dimensional model and compare it with phenomenological quarkyonic constructions. In the following, we set ℏ = kB = 1 and take unit volume.

8. Summary and outlook 

Tripling fluctuations o ff er a microscopic mechanism connecting three-fermion bound states to degenerate fermionic matter across a continuous three-body crossover. The phase-shift representation identifies the redistribution of spectral weight between a baryonic pole and the scattering continuum as the common origin of both a baryonic momentum shell and a peaked speed of sound. This perspective provides a bridge between ultracold-atom many-body theory and phenomenological models of quarkyonic matter. Its next challenge is to retain this mechanism in a quantitatively controlled equation of state incorporating the symmetries, composition, and interactions of dense QCD matter.
