ABSTRACT 

Context. Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are millisecond-duration radio transients of extragalactic origin whose physical origin remains uncertain. A small fraction of FRBs are known to repeat, and some of them are associated with persistent radio sources (PRSs), interpreted as synchrotron-emitting nebulae surrounding the FRB source. In the context of magnetar-based models, the rotation measure (RM) of an FRB is expected to correlate with the spectral luminosity of its associated PRS, providing a probe of the physical properties and evolution of the nebula. 

Aims. We investigate the relation between FRB RM and PRS spectral luminosity, constrain the characteristic size of the PRS nebulae, and use the intrinsic scatter of the relation to investigate evolutionary scenarios for FRB–PRS systems. 

Methods. We analyse a sample of 50 FRB sources with known RM, including both PRS detections and spectral luminosity upper limits, using a Bayesian MCMC framework that jointly accounts for detections and non-detections. We model the luminosity–RM relation as Lν ∝ ζeγ2

1. Introduction 

Fast radio bursts (FRBs; e.g., Cordes & Chatterjee 2019; Petro ff et al. 2022; Zhang 2023) are cosmological radio tran-sients of millisecond duration characterized by high radio luminosities and brightness temperatures, suggesting coher-ent emission from compact objects. Among the leading pro-genitor candidates, magnetars, i.e. neutron stars powered by strong internal magnetic fields (see, e.g., Rea & De Grandis 2026, for a recent review on the topic) are favored, and strongly supported by the 2020 detection of an FRB-like event from the Galactic magnetar SGR J1935 +2154 (CHIME /FRB Collaboration et al. 2020; Bochenek et al. 2020; Mereghetti & IBAS localization Team 2021), as well as from previous theoretical studies (e.g. Popov & Postnov 2013; Lyubarsky 2020; Beloborodov 2020; Sobacchi et al. 2022). A small fraction ( ∼ 2%) of the overall FRB population has been observed to repeat (Chime /Frb Collaboration et al. 2026), but nowadays it is still not clear whether the latter represent a di ff er-ent type of sources with respect to one-o ff s (e.g., Pleunis et al. 2021; James 2023; Sand et al. 2025). No prompt multiwavelength counterpart has been found for FRBs, despite substantial observational e ff orts toward their iden-tification (see Zhang 2024, for a review). Nevertheless, persistent radio sources (PRSs) co-located with a handful of precisely lo-calized FRBs have been reported (Marcote et al. 2017; Niu et al. 2022; Bruni et al. 2024, 2025; Ibik et al. 2024b; Moroianu et al. 

Article number, page 1 arXiv:2609.35733v2 [astro-ph.HE] 29 Sep 2026 A&A proofs: manuscript no. aa_example 

2026; Mfulwane et al. 2026). PRSs are compact (sub-parsec to parsec scales, e.g., Marcote et al. 2017) and luminous ( Lν ≃ 10 29 

erg s −1 Hz −1; Law et al. 2022), continuum radio sources, whose emission cannot be explained in terms of star-formation activity at the FRB site. To date, four repeating FRBs have been associ-ated with a PRS: FRB 20121102A (R1; Chatterjee et al. 2017; Marcote et al. 2017), FRB 20190520B (R1-twin; Niu et al. 2022; Bhandari et al. 2023), FRB 20190417A (Ibik et al. 2024b; Moroianu et al. 2026), and FRB 20240114A (Bruni et al. 2025; Bhusare et al. 2025). These persistent sources share common properties such as, e.g., their localization in dwarf and star-forming galaxies (see, e.g., Moroianu et al. 2026), flat or mildly inverted spectral indices (Moroianu et al. 2026; Bruno et al. 2026) and large values for the rotation measure (RM) of the asso-ciated FRBs (e.g. Michilli et al. 2018; Anna-Thomas et al. 2023; Moroianu et al. 2026). Furthermore, a number of PRS candidates have been re-ported, associated with both repeating FRBs, namely FRBs 20201124A (Bruni et al. 2024) and 20181030A (Ibik et al. 2024b), and apparently one-o ff sources (see Mfulwane et al. 2026). However, their physical sizes remain unconstrained at parsec scales and, in several cases, the FRB localization re-gions are too large to firmly establish a physical association with the candidate PRSs. In a companion work (hereafter Paper I; Pelliciari et al. 2026b), we have recently conducted a systematic search for PRS candidates using new 1 .26 GHz uGMRT obser-vations of 24 FRBs, combined with literature data. In another companion work (hereafter Paper II; Pelliciari et al. 2026a), we followed up the candidate PRS associated with FRB 20181030A with EVN and eMERLIN observations, providing evidence for its compactness on sub-parsec scales. Nowadays, the physical origin of PRSs remains debated. The most natural interpretation is synchrotron emission from a mag-netar wind nebula (MWN): a compact magnetized nebula in-flated by outflows from a young, active magnetar, powered ei-ther by the decay of its internal magnetic field or by spin-down (Margalit & Metzger 2018; Rahaman et al. 2025). Interestingly, a positive, linear correlation between the spe-cific luminosity ( Lν) of the PRS and the RM of the associ-ated FRB bursts is expected if the persistent radio emission and the Faraday rotation originate from the same magnetized neb-ula surrounding the FRB engine (Yang et al. 2020, 2022). This 

Lν − RM relation has been derived by Yang et al. (2020, 2022), who showed that it follows from this single assumption, regard-less of the nature of the central engine. Hereafter, we refer to this relation as the Yang-Li-Zhang (YLZ) relation, consistently with, e.g., Gao et al. (2025). This scaling is naturally expected in the MWN framework described in Rahaman et al. (2025): in a one-zone MWN framework, RM scales linearly with the source spec-tral luminosity, in agreement with Yang et al. (2020). Whether the hypernebula model (Sridhar & Metzger 2022; Sridhar et al. 2024) makes an analogous prediction is less straightforward. While in that framework both Lν and RM are produced by the same nebular plasma, the two quantities depend on a large number of coupled free parameters and evolve on di ff erent timescales, such that the sign and slope of any resulting Lν − RM relation depend critically on which parameters are held fixed and which are allowed to vary. The hypernebula model is therefore best regarded as consistent with – rather than predictive of – the observed relation. From an observational point of view, FRB-PRS systems agree well, although with some level of scatter (see Yang 2026), with the prediction of compact nebulae (see, e.g., Ibik et al. 2024b). On the other hand, FRBs showing low or negligible Faraday rotation are expected to have correspond-ingly faint or absent PRSs. This is consistent with the non-detection of PRSs in several well-monitored, active repeaters such as FRB 20180916B (Marcote et al. 2020), and 20200120E (Bhardwaj et al. 2021a) both of which show RM ∼ 0 rad m −2. Fi-nally, Yang (2026) recently showed that the scatter of the same relation can be used to constrain evolutionary models (i.e. the evolution with time of the nebula radius) for PRSs. The YLZ relation has so far been tested only on the hand-ful of confirmed PRS systems. A systematic, statistically mean-ingful test, spanning the full dynamic range of FRB RM val-ues, from highly Faraday-rotated sources to essentially unrotated ones, and including both repeaters and one-o ff events, is lacking. The present work exploit the FRB–PRS catalog presented in Pa-per I to carry out the first systematic analysis of the Lν − | RM |

relation across a large and well-defined sample of FRB sources. The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2 we outline the theoretical framework on which this work is based; in Section 3 we describe the properties of the sample of sources considered for the analysis and we present the constraints on the YLZ rela-tion in Section 4. Finally, we outline conclusions of this work in Section 5.

These studies provide a complete and rigorous model for a synchrotron emitting nebula which surrounds an FRB-emitting compact ob-ject (e.g. a magnetar). Here we only present the key ingredients of this framework that are relevant for the interpretation of our results, focusing in particular on the Lν–RM relation and its con-nection to the physical properties and temporal evolution of the emitting nebula.

2.2. Evolutionary models for PRSs 

The relativistic electrons responsible for the observed PRS emis-sion must be accelerated through specific physical mechanisms. Yang (2026) discuss three main astrophysical scenarios: (i) non-relativistic shocks propagating outward (forward shocks) or in-ward (reverse shocks) between a supernova remnant (SNR) and the external ISM, as well as forward shocks in a pulsar wind neb-ula (PWN) embedded in a SNR; (ii) re-acceleration of fossil (i.e. low-energy) electrons at the termination shock in a PWN /SNR system (so-called PWN bubble); and (iii) a bow shock formed through the interaction between the wind of a neutron star and the wind of its stellar companion in a binary system. For simplic-ity, we restrict the considered models only to isolated progeni-tor scenarios, namely models (i) and (ii). 4.1. Constraints on evolutionary models for PRSs 

We now exploit the scatter of the YLZ relation to constrain evo-lutionary models for PRSs (see Sect. 2.2). In particular we use the constraints obtained for the intrinsic scatter parameter, σint ,as a proxy for the composite parameter ˆ α|ϵ|, where ˆ α governs the temporal evolution of the nebular size, R ∝ t ˆα, while ϵ

parametrizes the generalized scaling of the Lν–RM relation with 

R, Lν ∝ Rϵ |RM |.Yang (2026) quantifies the scatter of the YLZ relation as the standard deviation of the residuals with respect to the best-fit model obtained by fitting only confirmed PRSs, with the addi-tion of the candidate PRS associated with FRB 20201124A. In our case, however, the presence of ULs drive the best-fit model significantly below the bulk of the detected data points. Conse-quently, in this case the residual-based estimate would not pro-vide a meaningful characterization of the true dispersion of the relation. If we consider the same dataset of Yang (2026), we ob-tain σint = 0.67 +0.75 

> −0.09

, which can be compared to σ∆ = 0.67 (Yang 2026). This indicates that σint is a good proxy for the true disper-sion of the YLZ relation. We then make use of Eq. 5 to convert the marginalized σint posterior distributions obtained in Sect. 4 into constraints for ˆ α|ϵ| in the various fitting scenarios considered (all vs. only repeaters and varying /fixed β). The resulting distributions for ˆ α|ϵ| are shown in Fig. 4, where they are compared with the theoretical expectations, namely M1–M7 models of Table 1. The corresponding median values for ˆ α|ϵ|, along with 1 σ CL uncertainties, are listed in Table 3 for all the considered scenarios. We find systematically larger values of ˆ α|ϵ| with respect to ˆα|ϵ| = 1.5 ± 0.7 reported by Yang (2026), favouring evolutionary scenarios in which the emitting region undergoes rapid expan-sion (i.e. larger ˆ α values) and /or e ffi cient energy injection into the relativistic electron population. Models predicting low values of ˆ α|ϵ| are generally disfavoured by our analysis. In particular, the Sedov–Taylor forward shock scenario in SNR /ISM environ-ments (M2, ˆ α|ϵ| ≃ 0.2), as well as PWN bubble models in both free-expansion and Sedov–Taylor phases (M5–M7, ˆ α|ϵ| ≲ 1), lie systematically below the bulk of the inferred distributions. While marginal consistency at the ∼ 2σ level cannot be excluded, these scenarios do not naturally reproduce the large scatter observed in the data. Conversely, forward shocks in SNR /ISM during the free-expansion phase (M1, ˆα|ϵ| ≃ 2.0) and forward shocks in PWN /SNR systems (M4, ˆ α|ϵ| ≃ 2.6) are broadly consistent with the peak of the obtained distributions. Even higher values, such as those predicted for reverse shocks in SNR /ISM environments (M3, ˆ α|ϵ| ≳ 3.5), are also compatible with the upper tails of the posteriors. However, adopting a steeper electron spectrum (s = 3) for the M3 scenario leads to a prediction of ˆ α|ϵ| ≃ 5(Yang 2026), which is in tension with our main constraints at the ∼ 2σ level. This value is only marginally compatible with the high ˆ α|ϵ| tail obtained in the specific case where the fit is restricted to repeaters and the slope is fixed to β = 1. However, an important caveat must be emphasized. In this work, detections and ULs are treated jointly within a single population of FRB-PRS systems, while in reality the observed sample may be heterogeneous. In particular, FRBs hosting con-firmed PRSs may represent a distinct subset with di ff erent physi-cal properties and evolutionary stages. As a consequence, analy-ses restricted to larger samples of securely identified PRSs could yield systematically di ff erent (and potentially lower) values of ˆα|ϵ|, more in line with previous estimates based on confirmed sources alone. For this reason, the constraints presented here should be regarded as indicative. While they provide evidence in favour of high- ˆ α|ϵ| scenarios, they also highlight the need for larger and more homogeneous samples of confirmed PRSs.

5. Summary and conclusions 

In a companion work (Paper I) we have presented an extended sample of FRB sources, comprising new 1 .26 GHz uGMRT ob-servations of a sample of 24 FRBs (13 are confirmed repeaters and 11 seemingly one-o ff sources) and literature data, obtaining a catalog of 75 FRBs for which a PRS search has been con-ducted. We already used it to study the occurrence of PRSs in the observed population of FRBs. In this work we exploited a subset of this catalog to deeply investigate the expected corre-lation between the FRBs RM and the radio spectral luminos-ity of the associated PRSs (YLZ relation; e.g., Yang et al. 2020; Bruni et al. 2024). In particular, we selected 50 individual FRB sources with known RM. We also provided constraints for evo-lutionary scenarios of FRB-PRS systems, following the frame-work delineated in Yang (2026). Being the majority of the PRS detections reported classified as candidates, we conducted our analysis under two scenarios: a conservative one, in which only confirmed PRSs are considered (4 repeaters and 0 one-o ff s), and an inclusive one, in which candidate PRSs are also included (9 repeaters and 3 one-o ff s). We constrained the principal parameters of the YLZ rela-tion using a Bayesian MCMC framework that accounts for both detections and spectral luminosity ULs. We considered a fitting relation of the form Lν ∝ ζeγ2 

> c

R2|RM |β, obtaining constraints on the size of the PRS nebula, R, and the RM scaling index β. Our analysis permitted to obtain R = 0.016 +0.115  

> −0.014

pc at 1 σ CL when considering the whole dataset and a varying β parameter. In a more canonical scenario in which β is kept fixed at β = 1, the size constraint approximately halves to R = 0.008 +0.058  

> −0.007

pc. Re-garding the dependence of the YLZ relation with the RM, our current dataset prefers mildly steeper β values than the canon-ical linear scaling ( β = 1). In particular, the steepest relation (β = 1.7 ± 0.4 at 1 σ CL) is obtained when we restrict the analy-sis only to repeaters, in the conservative scenario. Nevertheless, the constraints for β are consistent within 2 σ uncertainties with the canonical β = 1 scenario. We also considered the intrinsic scatter of the YLZ relation as a free parameter, given the large set of spectral luminosities upper limits and small number of PRS detections in our cata-log. We found that the YLZ relation shows a substantial intrin-sic scatter, significantly larger than previous estimates based on confirmed PRSs alone and we used the latter to investigate evo-lutionary models for PRSs following the prescriptions of Yang (2026). The inferred scatter translates into relatively large values of ˆ α|ϵ|, i.e. a composite parameter encapsulating both the tempo-ral dependence of R and the dependence of the Lν–RM relation on R, favouring models characterized by e ffi cient particle accel-eration and /or rapid nebular expansion. In particular, forward-shock scenarios in SNR /ISM or PWN /SNR systems are broadly consistent with the data, while models predicting low ˆ α|ϵ| (e.g. PWN bubble models or Sedov-Taylor shocks) are disfavoured. Future extensions of this analysis will benefit from the dis-covery and confirmation of additional PRSs, particularly at low RM, where the current sample provides shallow constraints on 

β. An interesting example is provided by the candidate PRS as-sociated with (Ibik et al. 2024b), recently shown to be compact on sub-parsec scales (Paper II), although its association with the FRB remains uncertain due to the still insu ffi cient localization precision. Such systems may reveal PRSs with physical proper-ties that di ff er substantially from those of the currently confirmed population. Systematic searches in large FRB samples, such as the recent CHIME catalogue (Chime /Frb Collaboration et al. 2026), will therefore be key to understand the properties of FRB-PRS systems.
