The phase structure of strongly interacting matter at high baryon density remains one of the major open problems in QCD, with potentially important consequences for the internal structure and magnetic properties of neutron stars. Among the possible phases that may emerge under such conditions, a quark spin-polarized (QSP) phase is of particular interest because of its potential im-pact on both the equation of state and spontaneous magnetization. We reexamine the spontaneous emergence of a QSP phase in cold and dense quark matter within the two-flavor Nambu–Jona-Lasinio model with tensor interactions, focusing on its sensitivity to regularization. We contrast the traditional regularization scheme (TRS), in which a sharp momentum cutoff is applied to vacuum and medium contributions, with the medium separation scheme (MSS), where algebraic subtractions isolate ultraviolet-divergent vacuum terms while finite medium-dependent phase-space contributions remain cutoff free. The two prescriptions lead to qualitatively different physics. In TRS, the QSP condensate eventually collapses at high chemical potential, producing nonmonotonic thermodynamic behavior and, for some couplings, violations of causality. In MSS, by contrast, the QSP condensate increases monotonically with density, the equation of state remains causal, and the associated spon-taneous magnetization grows toward high density. The persistence of spin polarization in this regime opens interesting possibilities for a microscopic contribution to the strong magnetic fields of compact stars, including magnetars. Using an exploratory hybrid-star construction, we further find that the QSP phase produces significant changes in the equation of state, stability trends, and mass-radius relations. While the stellar analysis is exploratory, the results demonstrate the potential impact of a persistent spin-polarized quark phase on neutron-star physics and highlight the importance of a consistent treatment of medium phase space. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

The properties of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) matter at high baryon density and low temperature re-main poorly understood. The main reason for this lack of understanding is the so-called sign problem, which severely limits the applicability of first-principles cal-culations using large-scale lattice Monte Carlo simula-tions [1, 2]. Understanding strongly interacting matter under such conditions is of significant astrophysical in-terest, as it is directly related to the internal structure of neutron stars and possibly other compact stellar objects. Several exotic phases have been proposed to emerge in this regime, including color-superconducting and ferro-magnetic phases, which may play a relevant role in these environments [3–6]. These possibilities have attracted considerable atten-tion from both the theoretical and observational com-munities. Observations of massive neutron stars, such as PSR J0740+6620 and PSR J1614–2230, as well as gravitational wave signals from neutron star mergers, have placed stringent constraints on the equation of state (EoS) at supranuclear densities [7–11]. existence of strongly magnetized neutron stars, known as magnetars, adds an additional level of complexity to the problem [12–16]. These stars exhibit surface magnetic field strengths on the order of 10 14 –10 15 G, and theoret-ical models indicate that the internal fields in their cores may attain even larger magnitudes. Nevertheless, the physical mechanism responsible for the generation and long-term maintenance of such extreme magnetic fields remains elusive. One proposed scenario [17–20], which is the subject of the present work, is that the magnetic field has a microscopic origin, potentially arising from spin polarization of quarks in dense quark matter. Given the aforementioned difficulties with lattice QCD, effective models of QCD have played an essen-tial role in exploring the behavior of quark matter un-der extreme conditions. Among these, the Nambu–Jona-Lasinio (NJL) model [21, 22] stands out due to its ability to capture essential nonperturbative features of QCD, es-pecially chiral symmetry breaking and its restoration at high densities or temperatures [23–25]. The NJL model, though it lacks confinement, serves as a valuable theoret-ical laboratory for studying dynamical mass generation and the formation of various condensates. In particu-lar, the spontaneous emergence of a spin-polarized phase of quark matter, commonly referred to as ferromagnetic quark matter, has been extensively explored in the liter-ature within this framework; for representative studies, see Refs. [26–32]. A recurrent finding in studies based on the NJL model is that, at sufficiently high quark densities, the spin-arXiv:2610.04160v1 [hep-ph] 3 Oct 2026 2polarized phase and the associated spontaneously gen-erated magnetic moment disappear, a result that is chal-lenging to interpret physically. In the present work, we reexamine this question in light of the regulation of ul-traviolet divergences in the model. As is well known, a critical component of any calculation within the NJL framework is the choice of an appropriate regularization scheme [25, 33, 34] owing to the intrinsic nonrenormal-izability of the model. Traditionally, a sharp cutoff or similar regulator is used with divergent momentum in-tegrals in the model, even when the integrands of those integrals involve medium-dependent contributions. Al-though this procedure is technically straightforward and is extensively employed in the literature, it might gener-ate nonphysical effects in terms that intertwine vacuum and medium contributions within ultraviolet-divergent integrals. Vacuum–medium mixing may induce spuri-ous dependencies of physical observables on temperature, chemical potential, or external background fields [35–40]. For example, the resulting equation of state may violate causality in certain density regimes, which is clearly phys-ically unacceptable. As argued in the following, the situation with the spin-polarized phase is similar to the color-superconducting gap in quark matter: the gap vanishes for chemical po-tentials comparable to the ultraviolet cutoff scale [35], in contradiction with QCD expectations [41], accord-ing to which the gap remains finite and increases with the chemical potential. Such spurious effects can, how-ever, be eliminated by using a procedure introduced in Ref. [35], later termed the medium separation scheme (MSS) in Ref. [36], which enables a systematic separa-tion of medium-dependent contributions from within di-vergent integrals. By untangling vacuum and medium effects, the MSS leads to physically consistent and ac-ceptable predictions. Although one might argue that in an effective low-energy theory like the NJL model, all momentum inte-grals should strictly be truncated at the cutoff scale Λ, even when mathematically finite, this view implicitly blurs the distinction in the role of Λ as an ultraviolet regulator of vacuum quantum fluctuations and that of a physical boundary on medium phase space. The view here is that the cutoff Λ is introduced to regularize diver-gent vacuum loops arising from point-like contact inter-actions; it parameterizes the scale of vacuum quantum fluctuations (such as chiral symmetry breaking) rather than serving as a wall restricting density-dependent states. When evaluating finite, medium-isolated con-tributions, such as those from quantum statistics via Fermi-Dirac distributions, they provide their own nat-ural, physical ultraviolet damping. Enforcing a sharp cutoff p ≤ Λ on medium-dependent integrals imposes an unphysical step-function boundary in momentum space, which truncates the phase space available to active states near the Fermi surface as the chemical potential ap-proaches Λ. This truncation is precisely what forces order parameters (such as the spin condensate F or color-superconducting gaps) to collapse at high densi-ties while simultaneously introducing boundary-driven anomalies that violate causality ( c2

Integrating the finite, medium-isolated parts over the full momentum space pre-vents these regulator-induced boundary effects without adding unphysical high-energy vacuum content, provided the chemical potential remains within the domain of va-lidity of the effective theory ( μq ≤ Λ). In this paper, we consider the SU(2) version of the NJL model augmented by a tensor interaction that can trigger a spin polarized phase. We perform a comparative analysis using both the traditional regularization scheme (TRS) and MSS frameworks, and we demonstrate the implications for the existence and properties of a spin-polarized phase in high density quark matter. We also examine how the MSS-regularized spin-polarized phase affects the equation of state and the mass-radius relation of compact stars. The present two-flavor treatment should be regarded as a controlled exploratory study of the regularization is-sue. At sufficiently high densities, strange quarks and ad-ditional pairing channels are expected to become relevant and may modify the equation of state, the spin-polarized phase, and the stellar mass-radius relation [4, 31, 40, 42– 44]. A realistic compact-star description should therefore include strangeness and other competing phases, which we leave for future work. We further emphasize that our analysis focuses on magnetization arising spontaneously, rather than being induced by an external spin potential that constrains the expectation value of a spin tensor; see, e.g., the review in Ref. [45] and references therein for a detailed discussion. References [46] and [47] imple-ment such a spin potential within the NJL model and the linear sigma model with quarks, respectively. III, we introduce the regularization schemes and outline the implementation of the MSS. In Sec. V, we summarize our findings and discuss possible extensions of this work.

> IV. NUMERICAL RESULTS

In this section, we present the numerical results and perform a comparative analysis of the outcomes ob-tained using the MSS (medium separation scheme) and the TRS (traditional regularization scheme). We em-ploy the same parameter values for GS and Λ as those adopted in Ref. [30], specifically GS = 5 .5 GeV −2 and Λ = 0 .63 GeV. As discussed in detail in Ref. [48], the value of the tensor coupling GT remains insufficiently constrained; therefore, we treat GT as a free parameter.  

> A. Dynamical quark mass and spin polarized phase

We determined the constituent quark mass M and the spin polarization F by solving the corresponding gap equations under imposed chemical equilibrium con-straints. In this study, we confine our analysis to values of the chemical potential that are compatible with in-terpreting the model as an effective, nonrenormalizable field theory characterized by an intrinsic ultraviolet cut-off scale Λ, beyond which its predictive capacity is in-trinsically constrained. Although there are ways to ex-tend the applicability of the model beyond Λ by making the couplings and cutoff T and μq dependent [49, 50], to avoid misinterpretations regarding the MSS, we accord-ingly work within the condition μq ≲ Λ. > V. CONCLUSIONS AND PERSPECTIVES

In this work, we investigated the effect of regularization on the appearance of a spin-polarized phase in high den-sity quark matter using the two-flavor NJL model with a tensor interaction under β equilibrium. We compared the traditional regularization scheme, in which the cut-off is applied directly to integrals containing both vacuum and medium-dependent quantities, with the medium sep-aration scheme, in which algebraic subtractions isolate the divergent vacuum contributions and leave the finite medium-dependent terms cutoff independent. Our results show that this distinction has significant physical consequences. In TRS, the QSP phase appears abruptly and disappears again at high chemical potential, leading to nonmonotonic thermodynamic behavior and, for some tensor couplings, acausal values of the speed of sound. In MSS, the QSP phase sets in at slightly higher chemical potential but grows monotonically with density. As a result, the thermodynamic observables are smoother, and the corresponding equation of state is softer than in TRS. The hybrid-star sequences obtained with MSS display different stability trends, and for the tensor couplings considered here, the masses are clearly affected by the QSP. We also found that the magneti-zation associated with the QSP phase is strongly regu-larization dependent. While the TRS result decreases at high density, the MSS result grows monotonically with baryon density. These findings indicate that predictions for the magnetic and stellar properties of spin-polarized quark matter within a NJL-type model are, not surpris-ingly, highly sensitive to the treatment of medium con-tributions. Extensions of the present analysis to include vector-channel interactions, color-superconducting pairing, and a potential interplay between chiral symmetry break-ing and spin polarization constitute natural and well-motivated next steps. Vector interactions are known to increase the stiffness of the quark-matter equation of state, thereby facilitating the support of more mas-sive hybrid-star configurations [44, 56–60]. Furthermore, the present calculation was restricted to two-flavor quark matter in order to isolate the effect of the regularization procedure on the QSP phase. This restriction is nontriv-ial: at densities relevant for massive compact stars, the appearance of strange quarks and additional pairing pat-terns may become energetically favored, with concomi-tant modifications to the equation of state, the onset of spin polarization, and the predicted stellar configurations [4, 31, 40, 42–44]. An additional direction is to assess the consequences of applying the MSS framework to the problem of rotation-induced magnetization [61]. Investi-gations along these lines are currently underway.
