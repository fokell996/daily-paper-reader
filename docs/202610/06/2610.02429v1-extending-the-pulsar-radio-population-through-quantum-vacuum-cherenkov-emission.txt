(Dated: October 5, 2026) The presence of some radio pulsars within and below the so-called “death valley” in the P –˙P diagram challenges the standard pair-cascade models based on curvature radiation (CR) and resonant inverse Compton scattering (RICS) in neutron star polar caps. We show that Cherenkov radiation from the quantum vacuum, a strong-field quantum electrodynamics (QED) phenomenon enabled by vacuum polarization, constitutes a distinct pair-creation channel within the vacuum gap model. Within this model, the acceleration length scale for Cherenkov photon emission and the subsequent photon mean free path are sufficiently short to ensure rapid conversion into e+e− pairs. This process acts as a complementary pair-creation channel, remaining viable even in regimes where classical mechanisms become heavily suppressed. This leads to a new set of death lines in the P – ˙P

diagram that partially overlap with and extend beyond the CR and RICS death valleys, enlarging the viable parameter space for radio-emitting neutron stars. The result provides a physical basis for coherent radio emission from long-period neutron stars previously considered inactive, and offers a new window for testing non-linear QED with next-generation radio facilities such as the Square Kilometre Array. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

The coherent pulsar radio emission is a defining charac-teristic of these objects, commonly explained by electron-positron pair-creation cascades in the polar cap of a neu-tron star (NS). This pair-creation mechanism is com-monly attributed to the interaction between high-energy photons and the pulsar’s magnetic field [1–3]. The condi-tion on the pulsar parameter space for pair creation de-fines the theoretical “death line”, commonly translated into a boundary in the P - ˙P diagram below which radio emission is expected to switch off. The region between the death line for pure dipolar and multipolar and highly-twisted fields forms a “death valley” where radio emission

Coherent radio emission produced close to the surface of pulsars is thought to stem from non-stationary pair creation across acceleration gaps in the Timokhin-Arons mechanism [5, 6]. The “pulsar death lines” [2] mark the threshold where primary particles no longer at-tain sufficient energy to produce enough pairs to screen ⃗E∥, leading to the cessation of radio emission. However, this classical paradigm has been challenged by the discovery of rotationally-powered radio pulsars located deep within or beyond the death valley, such arXiv:2610.02429v1 [astro-ph.HE] 1 Oct 2026 2as PSR J0250+5854 ( P = 23 .5 s) [7] and the ultra-long-period pulsar PSR J0901 −4046 ( P = 75 .9 s) [8]. For these sources, the available spin-down energy re-mains sufficient to power the observed radio luminosity, yet standard curvature radiation polar-cap mechanisms fail to operate at such slow rotation rates. While pair cascades might theoretically be sustained through Res-onant Inverse Compton Scattering (RICS), this mecha-nism stringently requires the presence of complex multi-polar surface magnetic fields (with local curvature radii 

ρc ∼ 10 5 cm) and high surface temperatures to enclose these sources within an active zone [9]. Concurrently, the recent discoveries of long-period radio transients (LPTs, e.g., [10, 11]) reveal an even more extreme population of slowly rotating compact objects, though these are gener-ally understood to be magnetically-powered systems or magnetic white dwarf binaries rather than rotationally-powered neutron stars [12–17]. Focusing on rotationally-powered pulsars in tension with theoretical death lines, a revised pair-creation framework grounded in first-principles QED is essential. Such a framework could ex-plain how coherent radio emission persists at slow rota-tion rates—without necessarily invoking highly localized magnetic anomalies—and ultimately determine the true boundary of radio-active neutron stars. In this work, we focus on the electrostatic structure of the vacuum gap (VG) model, assuming a strong binding of charges at the stellar surface that allows a substantial parallel electric field to develop. III. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 

Radiation produced by accelerated charges (such as synchrotron, curvature, and inverse Compton) and Cherenkov radiation, which are usually treated as dis-tinct emission processes, can be regarded as limiting regimes of a single underlying QED mechanism [38, 44]. Pulsars provide a unique environment for studying the interplay between these regimes, since both ultra-relativistic particle acceleration and strong-field vacuum polarization operate simultaneously. In the presence of intense magnetic fields, the quantum vacuum becomes polarized, modifying the photon dispersion, so enabling the QC onset in addition to classical radiation by accel-eration [36, 40, 45–49]. The three pair-creation channels considered here op-erate on distinct characteristic length scales within the vacuum gap. CR photons are emitted on a short scale but must propagate a significant distance before the per-pendicular component of the magnetic field is sufficient to trigger decay into pairs. Regarding the multi-wavelength implications, the QC mechanism reshapes the vacuum gap structure, leading to distinct observational predictions. First, the modi-fied gap height interferes with the back-streaming parti-cle flux, potentially maintaining polar cap temperatures detectable in the thermal X-ray band even for long-period pulsars near the traditional death line [50]. Second, high-energy secondary cascades are structurally altered: since primary QC photons are emitted strongly peaked at a characteristic quantum parameter χChe

This implies a distinct, sharply peaked (nearly monoenergetic) signature in the hard X-ray to soft gamma-ray band prior to cascade repro-cessing. These distinct gamma-ray signatures could be probed by next-generation observatories such as CTAO [51]. Finally, a critical aspect of extending the radio population to ultra-long periods is detectability. Pul-sars operating near the QC boundary exhibit inherently low spin-down luminosities. However, for a hypothetical pulsar just above the QC death line (e.g., P = 10 s, ˙P = 10 −14 s s −1), the available spin-down energy is 

Lsd ∼ 4×10 29 erg s −1. Assuming a typical radio emission efficiency of ηrad ∼ 10 −4, the expected radio luminosity is Lrad ∼ 4 × 10 25 erg s −1, which translates to a flux den-sity on the order of ∼ 10–100 μJy for a source at 1 kpc. While faint, this predicted flux provides a clear, falsifi-able target that falls well within the sensitivity limits of next-generation radio facilities such as the Square Kilo-metre Array (SKA), which is projected to reach sub-μJy sensitivities in deep integrations, and the next-generation Very Large Array (ngVLA). In summary, Cherenkov radiation from the quantum vacuum constitutes a fundamental pair-creation channel in NS polar caps, extending the paradigm of pulsar ra-dio emission. Our modeling of the electrodynamic gap shows that Cherenkov photons are efficiently produced at low altitudes, with mean free paths that are signifi-cantly shorter than the gap height ( ℓph ≪ ℓacc ∼ hgap ). This ensures rapid conversion into electron-positron pairs even in regimes where CR and/or RICS are suppressed. Consequently, our analysis reveals a new, Cherenkov-enabled death line that extends the viable parameter space for radio-emitting pulsars. This framework con-firms that sustained radio emission remains physically possible for some pulsars within/below the CR and/or RICS death valleys. Although the nature of individ-ual long-period transients such as GLEAM-X J1627– 5235 and GPM J1839–10 remains under investigation, our work demonstrates that NSs can, in principle, sus-tain radio emission in this extreme domain. This re-sult, together with the possibility that some long-period transients could be magnetars that give rise to fast ra-dio bursts (FRBs) [52], suggests the interesting possi-bility of a unified picture connecting long-period tran-sients, magnetars, and FRBs [17]. More broadly, our results confirm NSs as natural laboratories for strong-field QED, where vacuum polarization fundamentally re-shapes magnetospheric physics. The predicted popula-tion of Cherenkov-powered pulsars represents a clear tar-get for next-generation radio surveys, offering a unique opportunity to test non-linear QED in the astrophysical wild.
