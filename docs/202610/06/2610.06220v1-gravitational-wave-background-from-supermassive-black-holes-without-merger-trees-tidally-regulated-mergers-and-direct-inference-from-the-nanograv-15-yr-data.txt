(Dated: October 6, 2026) We present a semi-analytic framework that computes the gravitational-wave background (GWB) from supermassive black hole (SMBH) binaries by direct integration of an N -body-recalibrated extended Press–Schechter accretion kernel, bypassing Monte Carlo merger trees entirely. The calcu-lation is resolution-free and fast enough to be sampled directly against pulsar-timing data with no emulator or precomputed model bank. Between halo accretion and black-hole binary formation, we couple the satellite’s simulation-calibrated tidal mass loss continuously to Chandrasekhar dynamical friction. Satellites below a sharp, nearly redshift-independent mass-ratio threshold, ξ ≃ 0.04, are stripped faster than they sink and stall: their drag collapses onto the stellar core, and at z = 0, 21% of accreted satellites still owe more than a Hubble time of sinking. Counting also those still in transit, 38% are unmerged by z = 0. This lowers the GWB amplitude by 11.2% relative to fixed-mass merger-time formulas, and gives a physical origin for wandering SMBHs whose predicted mass budget matches cosmological simulations, although their number in a Milky-Way-mass halo does not. Combining this population model with per-binary stellar-hardening turnovers and eccentric spectra, we sample the five-parameter posterior directly against the NANOGrav 15 yr Hellings– Downs-correlated free spectrum, excluding the 15 .8 nHz bin, whose excess power a subsequent chromatic-noise reanalysis attributes to pulsar noise. I. INTRODUCTION 

Pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) have reported evidence for a stochastic gravitational-wave background (GWB) at nanohertz (nHz) frequencies, with the Hellings–Downs [1] angular correlation expected of gravitational waves: the NANOGrav 15 yr data set [2], the EPTA/InPTA second data release [3], the Parkes PTA [4], the Chinese PTA [5], and the MeerKAT PTA [6, 7]. The leading astrophysical inter-pretation is the superposition of inspiraling supermas-sive black hole (SMBH) binaries formed in galaxy merg-ers, anticipated since the earliest PTA forecasts [10–14], although early-Universe sources remain statistically vi-able interpretations of the same data [15]. Extracting black-hole and galaxy-evolution physics from this sig-nal requires a population model connecting halo merg-ers, through galaxy pairing and binary formation, to the sub-pc hardening where nHz waves are emitted. Most existing predictions build this chain on merger trees: Monte Carlo realizations of extended Press– Schechter (EPS) branching [16–18], halo catalogs from 

N -body simulations, or hydrodynamic volumes [13, 19– 21]. Trees are flexible but carry two structural costs. First, a mass resolution must be chosen, and the shot noise of discrete branchings propagates into the predicted spectrum. Pushing the resolution down to the progeni-tors that matter for seed-black-hole populations is ex-pensive. Second, and more consequentially for infer-ence, a tree-based pipeline is too slow to be called in-side a sampler. Posterior exploration must instead go through precomputed model banks or emulators. The NANOGrav astrophysical interpretation [14] samples a Gaussian-process emulator trained on a holodeck li-brary. At the opposite extreme, fully analytic EPS-based calculations [22, 23] evaluate the GWB as a direct inte-gral over the Lacey–Cole merger kernel [18] (hereafter LC93). This route is fast and resolution-free, but it is built on the raw EPS rate, whose known O(1) inaccu-racies against N -body merger rates [24] then propagate into the signal, and it reduces the halo-merger-to-binary delay to a constant merger probability. This paper develops a middle path with three ingredi-ents. 

(i) A tree-free, N -body-calibrated merger integral. We use the subhalo accretion-rate distribution of Ref. [25] arXiv:2610.06220v1 [astro-ph.CO] 5 Oct 2026 2(hereafter Y11), the EPS branching statistics recali-brated against simulations, as implemented in the semi-analytic subhalo code sashimi 1 [26–29], and treat it as a continuous merger kernel. Multiplied by the Sheth– Tormen mass function [30] and integrated over host mass, accretion redshift, and satellite mass, this kernel counts every branch–branch crossing of the ensemble of merger trees exactly once, with no realization noise and no reso-lution floor (Sec. II). Against the Fakhouri–Ma–Boylan-Kolchin (F10) N -body rate [24], the calibrated kernel reproduces the GWB amplitude to 3.4%, whereas the raw LC93 kernel used in Refs. [22, 23] deviates by 14% (Sec. II C). The calibration buys a factor of 4 in kernel systematics at zero runtime cost. 

(ii) Merger delays regulated by subhalo tidal evolution. 

Between halo accretion and black-hole binary formation, the satellite must sink to the halo center by dynamical friction while tides strip its mass. The two compete, be-cause the friction force scales with the satellite’s surviving bound mass. Almost all PTA population models evaluate this stage at fixed (infall) satellite mass [14, 19, 31–33], or impose no delay at all [22, 34]. Reference [20] lets strip-ping set the starting radius of a subsequent fixed-mass phase, but the sinking mass is frozen once that hand-off occurs. Integrating the orbit while the satellite is stripped is, by contrast, routine in satellite dynamics, where the two are solved together against simulation-calibrated recipes [35, 36]. We bring that treatment into the nHz-background calculation, coupling sashimi ’s stripping law [26] continuously to Chandrasekhar dynam-ical friction [37] throughout the orbital decay (Sec. III), and ask what it changes for the background and for the black holes left behind. The competition then acquires a sharp, nearly redshift-independent critical mass ratio. Satellites with ξ ≲ 0.04 (where ξ is the satellite-to-host mass ratio) are stripped faster than they sink, their drag collapses onto the sur-viving stellar core, and the orbital decay all but halts: at z = 0, 21% of accreted satellites still owe more than a Hubble time of sinking. A fixed-mass timescale leaves such satellites behind too, but selects them by epoch as much as by mass ratio, and the two therefore disagree about which black holes are left wandering (Sec. III D). The amplitude effect is a reduction of 11.2%, well inside the current amplitude uncertainty, which is why fixed-mass formulas have served PTA predictions adequately. The prize lies elsewhere: the same calibrated dynam-ics predict, with no extra freedom, the black holes that have not arrived at their host’s center by z = 0, a pop-ulation tracked in cosmological simulations and in semi-analytic models [38–41]. Their wandering mass fraction in galaxy-scale halos is 10% under the tidally regulated delay, matching without tuning the ∼10% seen in cos-mological hydrodynamic simulations, whereas the fixed-mass delay strands only 5.6% (Sec. VI B). This census is

(iii) The whole chain inside the sampler. Because the integral framework is deterministic and nearly all of it is independent of the black-hole parameters, a full like-lihood evaluation (population, delays, per-binary envi-ronmental spectral turnover, and eccentric harmonics) costs milliseconds after caching (Sec. We there-fore sample the five-parameter posterior directly against the NANOGrav 15 yr Hellings–Downs free-spectrum data [2, 43], calling the exact model inside the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampler with no emulator, no interpolation in parameter space, and no model bank (Sec. V). Emulator-free inference is itself established, and the way it is achieved is instructive. One route parametrizes the merger rate directly from observed galaxy statistics, bypassing halos entirely [33, 44, 45]. The other keeps halos but takes the raw analytic EPS rate and collapses the whole halo-merger-to-coalescence stage into a single constant efficiency [22, 46]. A third parametrizes an EPS black-hole population and confronts pulsar-timing with high-redshift data in one Bayesian fit, again carrying the delay as a fitted parameter [47]. Each buys its speed by simplifying the stage this pa-per is about. The calculation here keeps the halo ker-nel, calibrated against N -body rates, and integrates the delay from the stripping and sinking equations, while staying cheap enough to be the sampled model itself. > II. MERGER RATES WITHOUT MERGER TREES A. The master integral

A Monte Carlo merger tree encodes two distinct pieces of information: the assembly history of individual ha-los, and the statistical rate at which branches of given masses join. For a population-level observable such as the GWB, only the latter is needed, and EPS theory provides it in closed form. > C. Kernel systematics: what the calibration buys

The EPS merger rate has a known conceptual ambi-guity (it is asymmetric under exchanging progenitors) and O(1) normalization offsets against simulations. To quantify what the Y11 recalibration buys, we repeat the entire calculation (Fig. 1) with the kernel Na in Eq. (2) swapped for (i) the raw LC93 rate [18] [their Eq. (2.18), reduced to the per-host form in Appendix A], the kernel underlying the tree-free calculations of Refs. [22, 23], and (ii) the F10 fit to the Millennium simulations [24], which we treat as the N -body reference. Figure 1 shows the rates at a representative host mass and the GWB ampli-tudes obtained with each kernel under otherwise identical assumptions. The Y11 kernel tracks the N -body refer-ence to within ±(20–50)% pointwise over the mass ratios and redshifts that matter, and to 3.4% in the amplitude (the pointwise residuals average out in the triple inte-gral), while the raw EPS kernel is biased high by 14% in amplitude. Since hc ∝ √rate at fixed masses, kernel systematics at this level are subdominant to the delay and black-hole-mass uncertainties that we treat as free parameters below. We use F10 as the N -body reference but not as the fiducial kernel, for two reasons. The first is internal consistency: Y11 is the accretion distribution on which 

sashimi ’s evolved subhalo population is built, and the tidal stripping law that regulates our delays (Sec. III A) is calibrated within that same framework. Pairing F10 rates with sashimi stripping would splice together two independent calibrations. The second is portability: F10 is a fitting formula tied to the Millennium simulations and their cosmology, valid over the mass ratios and red-shifts sampled there, whereas Y11 is analytic in σ(M )and therefore adopts our cosmology self-consistently and extends smoothly to the low masses and high redshifts of the seed regime, with no resolution floor. The 3.4% amplitude agreement between the two shows that this choice is not a limiting systematic in any case. The kernel counts accretion onto the main branch. Satellite–satellite sinking, which N -body measurements find can rival the central–satellite channel at low mass thresholds [53], is not included, and alternative routes to the same rate are set out in Refs. [54, 55].

C. The residual hardening delay 

Reaching the halo center assembles the galaxy pair. The two black holes must still travel from kpc separa-tions to the sub-pc orbits where nHz waves are emitted, by dynamical friction inside the merged remnant [58] and through the final-parsec regime [59, 60]. We lump that journey into a single residual delay τhard , common to the population and sampled in the inference (Table I). A pair accreted at za therefore coalesces at the redshift 

zm reached by advancing za first by the sinking time of Sec. III A, then by τhard .A single timescale does not exhaust this stage. The same hardening phase also reshapes the emitted spec-trum, through the time each binary spends per unit frequency, and that aspect is modeled separately in Sec. IV B. The two are complementary descriptions of one stage rather than a double count: τhard moves the coalescence epoch , and hence which pairs arrive by z = 0, while the loss-cone model moves where in frequency the emitted power lands.

C. Eccentricity 

Eccentric binaries radiate in a comb of harmonics of the orbital frequency [69] and circularize as they shrink [65]. Both effects move power from low to high frequencies and flatten the spectrum in the PTA band [46, 70, 71]. We assign each binary an eccentricity et at its own transition frequency ft, the same per-binary ft of Eq. (9), hold it constant through the stellar-hardening phase, and evolve it along the Peters track above ft. A single et is there-fore shared by the population, but it is imposed at each binary’s own ft rather than at a common frequency, and above ft, it is not held fixed: the binary circularizes along the Peters track. et is thus the eccentricity at the end of the stellar-hardening phase, where gravitational-wave emission takes over. > B. Parameters, priors, and sampling

The model has five parameters, listed with their pri-ors in Table I. The first two set the black-hole masses (Sec. II B). The delay τhard is the residual hardening time introduced in Sec. III C: it advances the coalescence epoch of every pair that survives dynamical friction and removes those that fail to coalesce by z = 0, thereby lowering the amplitude, while et and ρ× reshape the low-frequency spectrum through the per-binary turnover (Secs. IV B and IV C). Everything else (the merger ker-nel, mass histories, stripping law, and the sinking rate) is fixed by its own calibration, not fit to the PTA data. The population weights, base black-hole masses, and galaxy-merger epochs are independent of θ and are therefore computed once and cached. A full likelihood evaluation then costs 43 ms on a single core of an Apple M1 Max, the machine on which all chains in this paper were run. We sample with an affine-invariant ensemble [75, 76] of 64 walkers for 12000 steps, discarding five autocorre-lation times as burn-in. Acceptance fractions are ≃ 0.3, and effective sample sizes are ∼2.3 × 10 3 for the prior-edge-truncated et and 3 .4–4 .2 × 10 3 for the remaining parameters, sufficient for the percentile-level statements quoted below. The robustness refits of Sec. VII A use 32 walkers for 6000 steps and exclude the same frequency bin as the fiducial. Identical runs are performed for the both BK08 and tidal delay models, the latter with the stall physics active.

Those measurements condition on galaxy mergers that completed, and simu-lations that reposition black holes to galaxy centers at every step [86] exclude the stalled population from the denominator by construction. A related constraint runs the other way, since matching the background amplitude has also been argued to require central black holes in nearly all galaxies above 10 9 M⊙ [87]; our stalled satel-lites sit below that stellar mass, and the two statements are therefore compatible. These systems should appear as long-lived kpc-scale offset nuclei and wandering black holes [38–40, 61], with abundances and host-mass distri-butions that the framework predicts deterministically: a parameter-free, falsifiable prediction of the calibrated de-lay dynamics, independent of the GWB fit, rather than a free knob. Wandering black holes have been tracked before [38– 41]. Our prediction can be confronted with the 12 wandering-black-hole demographics of cosmological hy-drodynamic simulations (Fig. 7). Assigning each stalled satellite its infall black-hole mass and each host its z = 0 nuclear mass from the same scaling relation, the frame-work predicts that wandering black holes hold a fraction 10% of the total black-hole mass in galaxy-scale halos (M200 c ≲ 10 13 M⊙), rising to 21% once cluster hosts are included. Romulus finds ∼10% of the local budget wan-dering [39], which our 10% matches with no tuning. That measurement counts seed-mass black holes, which we do not paint, and our figure is therefore a lower limit on the same quantity. Reference [39] further reports that the number of wanderers grows roughly linearly with halo mass, and the like-for-like comparison is our number slope, Nwander ∝ M 1.0200 c over 10 12 –10 14 M⊙. The wander-ing mass grows a little faster, ∝ M 1.2200 c, which is why the mass share in Fig. 7 climbs with host mass instead of staying flat. This agreement does not come from the GWB fit: none of the five sampled parameters enters the stall determina-tion, and the wandering mass fraction is exactly indepen-dent of ∆ KH (a uniform shift of the mass normalization cancels in the ratio). The comparison, therefore, tests the framework’s delay physics independently of the inference. It also mildly favors the tidal coupling. Every total in Ta-ble II is smaller under the fixed-mass delay: 26% against 38% by satellite number, and, in the mass budget, 5.6% against 10% at galaxy scale ( M200 c < 10 13 M⊙, the aper-ture of the Romulus comparison above) and 20% against 29% in a 10 13 M⊙ host. The fixed-mass delay lengthens the sinking time less for the low-mass-ratio satellites that dominate the wandering population. The separation is modest rather than qualitative, which is why its discrim-inating power has to be quantified. The model further predicts that cluster black-hole mass becomes wandering-dominated (62% at 10 14 M⊙), the crossover to a 10% budget occurring near M200 c ≃ 2×10 12 M⊙. The two de-lays diverge most, in relative terms, at galaxy and group scales (Fig. 7). A wandering-black-hole or offset-AGN census in that regime is therefore the sharpest near-term discriminant between them. Occupation measured in astrid now resolves this com-parison by seed model [88, 89], and a volumetric wan-dering density anchored to off-nuclear transients is avail-able [90]. Two biases act on the mass shares above in opposite directions, and we do not attempt to net them: not painting seed-mass black holes makes our share an underestimate, as noted above, while assigning wander-ers the same scaling relation as central black holes makes it an overestimate, since wanderers descend from stripped satellites and are undermassive for their hosts [86]. > VII. DISCUSSION A. Systematics budget

Table III collects the known systematics of the frame-work, each quantified by direct numerical experiment (Appendix B). The dominant modeling choices (the merger kernel and the growth of the primary black hole between infall and coalescence) shift the amplitude at the few-percent to ∼10% level, comfortably inside the current measurement uncertainty but relevant for the next data releases. At the massive end this is a ≲ 0.1 dex effect degenerate with ∆ KH . A morphology-dependent slope at the high-mass end [100] and the cross-matching of black-hole catalogs [101] act on the shape of the calibration rather than on its normalization and are not captured by the three swaps below. Feedback prescriptions move pre-dicted amplitudes by factors of a few [102]; fitting ∆ KH 

rather than importing a mass function insulates us from that. (ii) The mean main-branch history replaces the dis-tribution of histories. Assembly variance enters the signal only through the weak host-mass dependence of the strip-ping coefficients and the mass function, and ±10% shifts 14 of the assembly history move population-level rates at the tens-of-percent level only in the LISA-band extrap-olation, not in the PTA band. (iii) The Y11 kernel is calibrated on the same simulation suites as the mass his-tories. Kernel and history systematics are, therefore, not fully independent, and the F10 cross-check bounds their combined effect. (iv) Both the sinking march and the nu-clear density assignment idealize their hosts as singular isothermal spheres. A cored or NFW host would mainly rescale the sinking rate, and profile-dependent residuals in the stall boundary are not quantified here. The singu-lar isothermal sphere is cuspy and has no plateau in its distribution function, and the core stalling and buoyancy identified for cored hosts [103] are therefore absent by construction, while triaxiality leaves population medians essentially unchanged [104]. What we claim is accord-ingly the shape of the boundary, the tilt s, rather than its location. (v) Sequential mergers are not modeled: a later satellite can reach the nucleus while an earlier companion is still stalled, forming a triple system whose Kozai–Lidov cycles and chaotic exchanges drive a fraction of such pairs to coalescence [105, 106]. Part of the stalled population of Sec. VI B could be rescued through this channel. The quoted stalled fraction is therefore an upper limit on the surviving wandering population. Rescued pairs do coa-lesce, which raises the background rather than lowering it, and the effect works against the mass excess: a louder model needs less ∆ KH to match the same data. The size of that shift is bounded. Replacing the tidal delay by the fixed-mass one, which strands fewer satellites, raises Ayr 

by +12 .6%, and additionally setting τhard = 0, so that every pair coalesces as early as the model permits, raises it by 15.0%. With that caveat, neglecting realization variance is safe at current preci-sion [110, 111], although the full non-Gaussian treatment will be needed for IPTA-class data [112–114].  

> B. Relation to previous work

The framework occupies a deliberate middle ground, and it is worth separating what is new here from what is inherited. Three routes to a PTA population model are already in use. Tree- and catalog-based pipelines [13, 14, 19– 21] carry individual merger histories and are too slow to sample, reaching the data through a model bank or a Gaussian-process emulator instead. A second route parametrizes the merger rate directly from observed galaxy statistics and bypasses halos entirely [33, 44, 45, 71], which is fast and has supported emulator-free in-ference for a decade. A third evaluates an analytic EPS halo rate but compresses the whole halo-merger-to-coalescence stage into one constant efficiency [22, 23, 46, 115, 116]. Several groups have since placed a binary population directly inside the PTA likelihood [113, 117– 119], in each case carrying the halo-to-coalescence delay as a fitted parameter. What is new here is not emulator-free sampling, which the second and third routes already achieve, but that the sampled chain starts at an N -body-calibrated halo kernel and carries an integrated, mass-ratio dependent delay: against the raw LC93 kernel the calibration removes a 14% amplitude bias, and the con-stant merger probability is replaced by delay physics that predicts the non-merging fraction rather than assuming it. The individual ingredients are largely borrowed, de-liberately. Coupling tidal stripping to dynamical fric-tion along the orbit is standard in satellite dynam-ics [35, 36], and simulation-calibrated merger-time pre-scriptions aimed at pulsar timing exist [85]; what is new is importing that treatment into a nHz-background cal-culation, calibrated against the same simulations that fix 

sashimi ’s subhalo statistics. That the background am-plitude is far more sensitive to the hardening stage than to the outer delay is found independently in Ref. [120]. The collapse of the eccentric, environmentally hardened per-binary spectrum onto one rescaled reference curve is due to Ref. [71]. Wandering black holes have been pre-dicted before, both in hydrodynamic simulations [38, 39] and in semi-analytic models [40, 41]; what this census adds is that it is fixed by the same calibrated dynam-ics that set the background, with no free normaliza-tion. Among delay treatments the closest is Ref. [20], which sets a stripping-informed starting radius for a sub-sequent fixed-mass phase. Our eccentricity conclusions agree with Ref. [46]. 15  

> C. Outlook: one framework from PTA to LISA

Nothing in Secs. II–III is specific to the PTA band. Extending the mass grid downward and attaching a seed black-hole occupation function focc (M ) turns the same integral into a LISA-band merger-rate calculator [61, 121, 122], with the delay physics, including stalling (which matters more at the low masses where LISA sources live), carried over unchanged. Two consequences are worth stating. First, the oc-cupation parameters to which the PTA band is blind (Sec. II B) become the dominant observable at LISA, where the rate scales as f 2occ : LISA measures precisely the quantity that PTA data cannot. Second, the param-eters constrained here (∆ KH , σdex , τhard , and the delay physics) propagate directly into LISA-band predictions. Because ∆ KH and τhard enter both bands, and because a cached evaluation costs milliseconds in either, the two data sets can be sampled together , over shared param-eters, rather than one being conditioned on the other. Joint PTA–LISA population inference in this framework, together with individual-source and anisotropy statistics, will be presented elsewhere.  

> VIII. CONCLUSIONS

We have built and tested a population framework for the SMBH-binary GWB combining three properties that are not found together elsewhere, the second of which is the one that distinguishes it: it is merger-tree-free (a deterministic triple integral over an N -body-calibrated EPS accretion kernel, resolution-free by construction), its merger delays are regulated by subhalo tidal evolution coupled continuously to dynamical friction (predicting a stalled, wandering-black-hole population below a sharp critical mass ratio ξ ≃ 0.04), and it is fast enough (mil-liseconds per likelihood) to be sampled directly against PTA data without emulation. Direct sampling has since been achieved by several groups [113, 117–119]; what remains particular to this framework is that the delay is integrated from the stripping and sinking equations rather than fitted. Applied to the NANOGrav 15 yr Hellings–Downs free spectrum, excluding the one bin whose excess power the data provider has since attributed to pulsar noise, the fit yields a black-hole mass normal-ization ∆ KH = +0 .31 +0 .20  

> −0.23

Retain-ing the excluded bin would raise the normalization to +0 .42 +0 .23

Independently of the fit, the calibrated delay dy-namics predict a parameter-free stalled fraction of 38% of accreted satellites unmerged by z = 0 (the posterior-weighted non-coalesced fraction, adding still-hardening nuclear binaries, is 46 ±6%), whose wandering-black-hole mass budget matches cosmological simulations without tuning and exceeds that of a fixed-mass delay. This is an independent, electromagnetically testable prediction of the same framework. The identical machinery is de-signed to extend to the LISA band, where the occupation physics invisible to PTAs becomes the leading observable. The code implementing the framework, including the cached-likelihood evaluator and the analysis chains of this paper, will be made available with sashimi .
