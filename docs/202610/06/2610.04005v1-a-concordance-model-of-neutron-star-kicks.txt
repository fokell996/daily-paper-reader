ABSTRACT Neutron stars (NSs) receive natal kicks during supernovae that affect their survival in binaries and motion through the Galaxy. The global distribution of these kick velocities across the NS population remains uncertain. Here, we combine the orbital properties and birth rates of Gaia NS and Be+NS binaries with velocity constraints from young isolated pulsars to construct a concordance model of NS natal kicks. We find that all three populations can be jointly described by a shared bimodal kick distribution. The low-velocity component follows a lognormal distribution with μln v = 1 .87 +0 .65

The low-velocity component contributes only ∼ 10% of all NS births, but produces most Gaia NS binaries and roughly half of Be+NS binaries. The same model predicts systemic recoil velocities consistent with the observed kinematics of Gaia NSs, Be+NS binaries, and NS low-mass X-ray binaries. For Gaia NSs, whose formation history remains unknown, we use this kick distribution to constrain their progenitors. We favor pre-supernova binaries containing intermediate-mass stripped stars with ∼ 1 M ⊙ companions in au-scale orbits, and predict ∼ 300 such binaries in the Milky Way today, ∼ 40 in the LMC, and ∼ 30 in the SMC. Finally, we consider the implications of these kicks for NSs in hierarchical triples. We find that 2–10% of Gaia NS triples and 3–23% of Be+NS triples retain their tertiary after the supernova. Overall, our results suggest that a shared natal kick law can account for isolated pulsars and surviving NS binaries, where most NSs receive large kicks and low kicks are common among surviving binaries.

1. INTRODUCTION Nearly all neutron star (NS) progenitors are born with companions. Massive OB stars have binary or higher-order multiplicity fractions of 80–100% (Sana et al. 2012; Moe & Di Stefano 2017), yet most young Galactic NSs are observed as isolated pulsars (Lorimer 2008). This suggests that a large fraction of massive-star binaries ei-ther merge before core collapse or become unbound dur-ing the supernova (e.g., Brandt & Podsiadlowski 1995; Tauris & Takens 1998; Renzo et al. 2019). The impulse delivered to the compact object during a supernova (the ‘natal kick’) is a key ingredient for modeling the evo-lution of massive binaries and their downstream out-comes, including stripped-envelope supernovae, X-ray binaries, recycled pulsars, kilonovae, and gravitational-wave mergers (e.g., Bhattacharya & van den Heuvel 1991; Podsiadlowski et al. 2002; Tauris et al. 2012, 2017; Vigna-G´ omez et al. 2018; Abbott et al. 2017). Although natal kicks are thought to arise during the supernova (SN), the specific mechanisms and subsequent kick magnitudes remain uncertain. Large kicks are of-ten attributed to hydrodynamic recoil from asymmet-ric ejecta, with contributions from anisotropic neutrino emission (see Burrows et al. 1995; Arras & Lai 1999; Janka 2017; Lai 2004; Tamborra et al. 2014; Nagakura et al. 2019; Coleman & Burrows 2022; Burrows et al. 2024a,b; Janka & Kresse 2024). Small kicks are ex-pected from rapid, symmetric, or low-ejecta-mass ex-plosions, such as electron-capture and ultra-stripped su-pernovae (e.g., Nomoto 1984, 1987; Podsiadlowski et al. 2004; Tauris et al. 2013, 2015; Janka 2017; Gessner & Janka 2018; Tauris et al. 2017). Isolated pulsars show evidence for relatively large kicks, with inferred kicks ranging from ∼ 50 km s −1 to > 1000 km s −1 (Lyne & Lorimer 1994; Hobbs et al. 2005; Faucher-Gigu` ere & Kaspi 2006; Verbunt et al. 2017; Igoshev 2020; Disberg & Mandel 2025), although the shape of this distribution arXiv:2610.04005v1 [astro-ph.HE] 2 Oct 2026 2 Shariat et al. 

is still debated (e.g., Arzoumanian et al. 2002; Verbunt et al. 2017; Igoshev 2020; Disberg et al. 2025; Disberg & Mandel 2025). These measurements anchor the kick distribution of isolated NSs, but they do not directly probe the binaries that survive core collapse. Binaries that remain bound after NS formation pro-vide a complementary perspective. Their existence al-ready selects against very large kicks, and their post-SN orbits set the future evolution of the binary. One way to constrain natal kicks is by measuring the present-day motion of the surviving binaries through the Galaxy. This approach has been applied to a variety of bina-ries containing NSs and black holes (e.g., Repetto et al. 2012; Mandel 2016; Atri et al. 2019; Fortin et al. 2022; O’Doherty et al. 2023; Zhao et al. 2023; Disberg et al. 2024a; Nagarajan & El-Badry 2025). The systemic ve-locity of these systems encodes the center-of-mass recoil imparted on the binary due to the kick, but does not explicitly isolate the natal kick that the compact ob-ject received. Instead, it measures a combination of the natal kick on the compact object, acceleration due to symmetric mass loss, the pre-SN orbital velocity, and later evolution in the Galactic potential (Blaauw 1961; Hills 1983; Kalogera 1996; Kalogera & Webbink 1998; Atri et al. 2019; O’Doherty et al. 2023; Disberg et al. 2024a,b). This makes systemic velocities a useful but indirect constraint on the natal kick, given the uncer-tainties in Galactic phase, disk heating, ages, and pre-SN binary evolution, which can all blur the connection to the natal kick (Wielen 1977; Atri et al. 2019; Zhao et al. 2023; O’Doherty et al. 2023). A complementary constraint on natal kicks can be in-ferred from the orbits of surviving binaries, particularly those whose orbits have changed little since the SN. In that case, the present-day period and eccentricity can be modeled assuming an instantaneous explosion 1 to in-fer the natal kick and mass loss. This method allows inferences on the kicks received in wide Be+NS bina-ries and double NSs (e.g., Beniamini & Piran 2016; An-drews & Mandel 2019; Valli et al. 2025). Wide Be+NS systems are especially useful because the pre-SN evo-lution is comparatively well understood, there has only been one kick since then, tides are inefficient over the Be-star lifetime for orbital periods Porb ≳ 5 days, and pulse timing gives precise orbital periods and eccentric-ities (Reig 2011; Bashi et al. 2023). Valli et al. (2025) used a sample of 23 Be-XRBs to infer two kick families: an ultralow-kick component with vkick < 10 km s −1 for

the low-eccentricity systems and a ∼ 100 km s −1 compo-nent for the high-eccentricity sequence, with the latter favoring polar alignment within a few degrees of the pro-genitor spin axis. The recently discovered Gaia NS candidates extend orbit-based kick constraints to a new regime. The 27 systems identified in Gaia DR3 contain solar-type stars with dark companions in the NS mass range 2, on de-tached au-scale orbits (El-Badry et al. 2024b,c, 2026). Their observed periods are Porb = 189–1405 d and their eccentricities are e = 0 .12–0 .79. At these separations, tides, winds, gravitational radiation, and later stellar evolution should not have changed the orbit discernibly since NS formation. Unlike double NSs, the interpreta-tion involves one supernova rather than two, and unlike interacting X-ray binaries, the systems have not under-gone Roche-lobe overflow after NS formation. The Gaia NSs therefore provide one of the cleanest laboratories for constraining NS natal kicks. The Gaia NSs also offer two key advantages that make them unique among most other NS binary populations. First, they are nearby ( d ≲ 2 kpc) and have precise as-trometry with extensive radial velocity follow-up, pro-viding 6D kinematics for each binary (El-Badry et al. 2024b, 2026). Second, the Gaia astrometric binary se-lection function is well characterized (Halbwachs et al. 2023; El-Badry et al. 2024a; Lam et al. 2025), which is not the case for NS-LMXBs, for example (e.g., Mac-carone et al. 2022). Leveraging this, Shariat et al. (2026) modeled the Gaia selection function and used hierarchi-cal Bayesian inference to constrain the intrinsic demo-graphics of the Gaia NS population, including the dis-tributions of period, eccentricities, masses, and space densities. The resulting intrinsic population provides a benchmark for Gaia NS formation and kick models, where a successful model should match both the Gaia NS intrinsic period–eccentricity distribution and relative abundance in the Galaxy. In this paper, we use the intrinsic Gaia NS population to infer the progenitor masses, mass loss, and natal kicks required to form them. We then connect our results with the broader NS population to provide a global view of NS kicks. The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. Section 2 describes the methodology. The period–eccentricity distribution is still the main driver of the kick inference. 2.1.1. Contamination from white dwarfs 

Gaia NSs are NS candidates because the nature of the dark companion is inferred from its mass. Companions below the Chandrasekhar limit could be ultramassive WDs, and unresolved compact WD+WD binaries could also replicate a single NS-mass object (e.g., El-Badry et al. 2024b,c, 2026). Individual contaminants would not change the kick constraints for genuine Gaia NSs, but they would (a) reduce the true Gaia NS birth rate and (b) potentially bias the inferred kicks if they oc-cupy a different part of the Porb –e plane. The birth rate effect is likely subdominant, since the Gaia NS rate is already uncertain by a factor of ∼ 2 (Shariat et al. 2026). Single ultramassive-WD contaminants are also unlikely to dominate the kick inference because known 

Gaia WD+MS binaries tend to have relatively low ec-centricities, unlike the Gaia NS candidates (Shahaf et al. 2024; Yamaguchi et al. 2024a,b, 2025). Unresolved com-pact WD+WD contaminants are harder to assess be-cause their formation history and outer Porb –e distri-bution remain poorly constrained (Shariat et al. 2023; El-Badry et al. 2024b,c). Shariat et al. (2026) repeated the population in-ference using only the 19 Gaia NS candidates with 

MNS > 1.35 M ⊙, where ultramassive-WD contamina-tion is strongly reduced, and found similar period and eccentricity distributions 3. For the remainder of this study, we follow the discovery papers in modeling the full sample as NS–MS candidates. Joint fit: Gaia NS, Be+NS, and isolated pulsars 

The Gaia NS and Be+NS samples constrain the kicks of binaries that survive core collapse, while young iso-lated pulsars anchor the high-velocity branch of the parent NS kick distribution. We fit the 27 Gaia NS candidates and 23 Be+NS binaries with one shared two-component kick velocity distribution, while allow-ing their pre-supernova period and helium star mass dis-tributions to differ. The binary orbits and birth rates constrain the shape and weight of the low-kick compo-nent, and velocities of young isolated pulsars constrain the high-kick component (Disberg & Mandel 2025). Young pulsar velocities have been modeled with a double-peaked kick velocity distribution (e.g., Faucher-Gigu` ere & Kaspi 2006; Verbunt et al. 2017; Igoshev 2020), but Disberg & Mandel (2025) recently argued that a unimodal lognormal distribution is statistically consistent with the current young-pulsar sample. Gaia NSs and Be+NS binaries are rare compared to isolated NSs (e.g., Shariat et al. 2026), raising the question of whether they are simply drawn from the low velocity tail of such a single peaked isolated-pulsar kick law. If so, their orbits would not require a separate low-kick component to the kick distribution. We test this hypothesis by replacing the joint-fit kick law with single-lognormal kick distributions that remain consistent with isolated-pulsar constraints, and apply them to the same He+MS progenitor model used above (Appendix D). For each trial law, we calculate both the Gaia NS survivor fraction and the location of the se-lected survivors in the P –e plane. We find that single-lognormal models produce some Gaia NSs, but place too many of them at short periods and high eccentricities. In contrast, virtually no Gaia NSs have such properties. We quantify this by drawing the line shown in Figure 15 and computing, after apply-ing the DR3 selection function, the fraction of selected survivors above it as predicted by the kick law. Several pulsar-inferred kick laws are tested, including optimisti-cally broadened distributions. KINEMATICS The orbital fits constrain the natal kick through bi-nary survival and the observed Porb and e. However, an impulsive kick changes both the binary’s orbit and its center-of-mass velocity, altering its kinematic motion through the Galaxy. The present day space velocities of post-kick binaries thus provide a complementary con-straint on natal kicks. In this section, we use observed kinematics to independently test the unified NS kick model by comparing its predictions to the observed kine-matics of Gaia NSs (Section 4.1), NS-LMXBs (Section 4.2), and Be+NS binaries (Section 4.3). 4.1. SURVIVAL OF TERTIARIES The progenitors of NSs, massive OB stars ( ≳ 10 M ⊙), are often born in triple or higher-order multiples (50– 80%; Moe & Di Stefano 2017; Offner et al. 2023). This suggests that tertiary companions are prevalent around the progenitors of Gaia NSs. Triple evolution can also play a role in the formation of NSs and BHs orbited by low-mass companions (e.g., Eggleton & Verbunt 1986; Naoz et al. 2016; Rose et al. 2019; Generozov & Perets 2024; Li et al. 2024; Shariat et al. 2025b; Li et al. 2026; Naoz et al. 2025). A tertiary that remains bound after NS formation provides an independent constraint on the center-of-mass recoil, as illustrated by the wide compan-ion to the BH-LMXB V404 Cygni (Burdge et al. 2024; Shariat et al. 2025b) Motivated by these results, we in-vestigate how the inferred natal kicks impact tertiary companions. 5.1. Tertiary-survival calculation 

We test tertiary survival by adding outer companions to Gaia NS progenitors before the supernova and identi-fying which triples remain bound and dynamically sta-ble afterward. We add outer companions to simulated Gaia NS progenitors and determine whether the result-ing triples remain bound and dynamically stable after the supernova. The tertiary properties are drawn from the Shariat et al. (2025a) triple model, which was cali-brated on resolved triples in Gaia DR3. We select sys-tems with NS-forming primaries, 8 ≤ M1/M⊙ ≤ 20, low-mass inner companions, qin = M2/M 1 < 0.2, and inner separations 1 < a 1/au < 10. The tertiary mass, outer semimajor axis, eccentricity, and mutual inclina-tion define the immediate pre-supernova outer orbit. Our fiducial calculation uses the orbital period distri-bution of Duquennoy & Mayor (1991), as adopted by Shariat et al. (2025a). Although this distribution was measured primarily for lower-mass stars, it is broadly consistent with recent constraints on massive hierarchi-cal triples (Bordier et al. 2026); uniform and log-uniform outer-separation distributions give similar results. We assign one tertiary to each progenitor drawn from the inferred Gaia NS kick model and retain systems that are initially hierarchical (Naoz et al. 2013) and satisfy the Mardling & Aarseth (2001) stability criterion. We then apply the instantaneous-supernova calculation (Lu & Naoz 2019). A tertiary survives if the inner binary becomes Gaia-NS-like and the post-supernova triple re-mains bound, hierarchical, and dynamically stable. We simulate 5 × 10 6 initially stable triples. This calculation treats the assigned outer orbit as the immediate pre-supernova configuration and does not follow its earlier evolution. 5.2. Results

DISCUSSION 6.1. Comparison to Previous Kick Constraints 

Figure 11 compares different empirically inferred kick distributions, which fall into two broad categories: or-bital constraints and peculiar velocity constraints. For Gaia NSs, wide Be XRBs, and DNSs, the kick is in-ferred mainly from the post-supernova orbit (Beniamini & Piran 2016; Valli et al. 2025). For NS-LMXBs, NS-HMXBs, and isolated pulsars, the constraints are pri-marily inferred from present-day space velocities and Galactic kinematics (Hobbs et al. 2005; Faucher-Gigu` ere & Kaspi 2006; Verbunt et al. 2017; Igoshev 2020; Dis-berg & Mandel 2025). Our model fits the Gaia NS and Be+NS popula-tions simultaneously with young isolated pulsars to in-fer a unified bimodal kick law (top panel in Figure 11). Valli et al. (2025) interpret these constraints as a tri-modal kick law: an ultralow component with median ∼

6.5 km s −1, a narrow polar component near 100 km s −1,and a high-velocity component traced by isolated pul-sars. Our fit also recovers an ultralow component with median 6 .5 km s −1, while the low-velocity tail of the high-kick component supplies the eccentric Be+NS sys-tems near 100 km s −1. The present data therefore do not strictly require a separate intermediate-velocity par-ent component. 6.1.1. Be+NS binaries

(2025) modeled the Be-XRB period– eccentricity distribution with an explicitly bimodal kick prescription, finding an ultralow-kick branch for the low-eccentricity systems and a ∼ 100 km s −1 branch for the high-eccentricity Be-XRB sequence. Physical Interpretation 

6.2.1. Origin of low kicks 

Across NS populations, there is evidence for both low natal kicks (0–50 km s −1) and high natal kicks (100– 1000 km s −1). Which physical channels produce each regime remains an open question. Large kicks are pro-duced in 3D simulations of standard iron-core collapse when asymmetric ejecta gravitationally accelerate the newborn NS (e.g., Wongwathanarat et al. 2013; Janka 2017; M¨ uller 2020; Burrows et al. 2024a,b). By contrast, low kicks ( ≲ 50 km s −1) are expected when the explosion is rapid and/or the ejecta mass is small, leaving little time or leverage for large hydrodynamic asymmetries to grow. This includes electron-capture supernovae (EC-SNe) from ONeMg cores, for which multidimensional simulations predict kicks of only a few km s −1 (Nomoto 1984, 1987; Gessner & Janka 2018), ultra-stripped su-pernovae, whose ejecta masses can be ≲ 0.1 M ⊙ (Tau-ris et al. 2013, 2015), and possibly some low-mass iron-core collapses in stripped binaries (Podsiadlowski et al. 2004; Tauris et al. 2017; Janka 2017; O’Doherty et al. 2023). Recent 3D simulations of low-mass progenitors (∼ 9 M ⊙) produce total kick speeds of only a few tens of km s −1 for some rotation rates (Burrows et al. 2026). NS masses provide another test of these explosion channels. Recent simulation suites predict that more massive NSs receive larger kicks on average (Burrows et al. 2024b; Karim et al. 2026). Gaia NSs, which gen-erally formed through low kicks, should therefore harbor less-massive NSs than populations that received larger kicks, such as HMXBs, LMXBs, or high-e Be+NS bi-naries. Existing HMXB measurements may tentatively support this picture. The ten eclipsing HMXBs an-alyzed by Falanga et al. (2015) have relatively large NS masses (median 1 .57 M ⊙), and Fortin et al. (2022) infer large natal kicks for HMXBs (median vkick =116 +18  

> −15

km s −1). However, the mass estimates are heav-ily dependent on models of the donor RVs, donor masses, and eclipse geometry. More work is needed to establish an MNS –vkick correlation. Gaia NS1 makes it difficult to apply such an MNS –

vkick correlation uniformly. It has a mass of 1 .88 M ⊙, yet its modest eccentricity of ∼ 0.1 (El-Badry et al. 2024c) implies it received a low kick. A similar argument dis-favors an ECSN origin for the entire Gaia NS popula-tion. ECSNe explosions should produce a narrow range of MNS ≈ 1.25–1 .30 M ⊙, whereas 12 /21 Gaia NS candi-dates exceed 1 .4 M ⊙ (Kitaura et al. 2006; Schwab et al. 2010; Stevenson et al. 2022; El-Badry et al. 2024c, 2026). Also, the intrinsic Gaia NS mass distribution peaks near 1.3 M ⊙ and is broadly similar to those of accreting and recycled NS populations (Shariat et al. 2026). The low-kick component may therefore include several explosion mechanisms. Larger samples can test whether NS mass and natal kick are correlated. We also highlight that the inferred Gaia NS natal kicks (vkick = 3–30 km s −1) and the systemic velocities they impart (8–30 km s −1) are below the escape speeds of most dense clusters. Thus, such kicks would help re-tain NSs, consistent with their observations in clusters (Drukier 1996; Davies & Hansen 1998; Ivanova et al. 2008; Pfahl et al. 2002). For Gaia NSs specifically, glob-ular cluster models predict formation rates far below the inferred Galactic rate, indicating that cluster dynamics contribute only a small fraction of the observed popu-lation (e.g., Tanikawa et al. 2024; Schiebelbein-Zwack et al. 2026). 6.2.2. Living progenitors 

Our kick model directly predicts the living progeni-tors of Gaia NSs. In the isolated-binary picture consid-ered here, the immediate pre-supernova binaries contain stripped helium stars with characteristic mass MHe ∼

2.3 − 5 M ⊙ and ∼ 1 M ⊙ main-sequence companions in roughly au-scale orbits. These helium star masses lie in the intermediate-mass stripped star regime, Mstrip ∼ 1– 8 M ⊙, between ordinary hot subdwarfs and classical Wolf-Rayet stars (G¨ otberg et al. 2018; Drout et al. 2023; G¨ otberg et al. 2023; Hovis-Afflerbach et al. 2025).Such stars are predicted and observed to be hot, UV-bright, helium-rich, and hydrogen-poor, with typical Teff ∼ 50– 100 kK, log g ∼ 5, and radii of order 0 .5–1 R ⊙ (e.g., G¨ otberg et al. 2018; Drout et al. 2023; G¨ otberg et al. 2023). A young stripped star should outshine a ∼ 1 M ⊙

companion in the UV, making such systems easier to identify than analogous binaries with luminous B-type companions, where the stripped star signature is more strongly diluted (e.g., Drout et al. 2023; Blomberg et al. 2025; Ludwig et al. 2025). The most direct search for Gaia NS progenitors is therefore for hot MHe ≳ 2 M ⊙ helium stars with low-mass companions and periods of ∼ 50 to ∼ 10 3 days. Recent RV monitoring has revealed intermediate-mass stripped stars with optically faint companions and or-bital periods extending to ∼ 300 days (Laroche et al. 2026). These companions could be low-mass stars, consistent with the progenitors of Gaia NSs. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS 

Gaia DR3 revealed a new class of neutron stars in au-scale binaries with solar-type companions (El-Badry et al. 2024b,c, 2026). Their origin remains puzzling: the orbits are close enough that the NS progenitor likely in-teracted with the present companion, but too wide to be an obvious outcome of common-envelope evolution and too fragile to survive large natal kicks. Yet this de-tached orbital architecture with low-mass companions makes them uniquely sensitive probes of NS natal kicks. Once the NS formed, the orbit has remained largely the same, so the present-day periods and eccentricities pro-vide pristine records of the supernova mass loss and na-tal kick that occurred at NS formation. Using the intrinsic period–eccentricity distribution and space density of Gaia NSs (Shariat et al. 2026), we infer the kicks and progenitors required to form them. We then jointly model Gaia NSs, Be+NS binaries, and isolated pulsars to construct a concordance model of NS natal kicks. Our main conclusions are summarized as follows: 1. G. COMPARISON TO DISBERG ET AL. (2026) G.1. Approach 

Disberg et al. (2026) compare natal kick models across several classes of NS binaries using the rapid population-synthesis code COMPAS (Riley et al. 2022; Team Compas et al. 2025). They evolve 10 6 solar-metallicity binaries and compare 5 fixed kick prescriptions with observations of Gaia NS binaries, NS-LMXBs, NS–WD binaries, Be XRBs, and DNSs. Their calculation follows the full bi-nary evolution, including mass transfer and common-envelope evolution, eccentric pre-supernova orbits, two supernovae for DNSs, and delayed rocket kicks. We in-stead use the observed Gaia NS and Be+NS orbits to empirically infer the pre-supernova period and helium star mass distributions that best reproduce the data, without ties to a formation history. We then fit one shared two-component kick law, with the high-kick com-ponent constrained by young isolated pulsars. G.2. Gaia NSs 

The Gaia NS analyses differ in their progenitor mod-els, samples used, and treatment of the observed popu-lation. Disberg et al. (2026) analyze the 21 candidates from the original El-Badry et al. (2024b) sample and weight their simulated binaries using an approximate analytic Gaia selection function (Baibhav et al. 2026). We fit the updated 27 Gaia NS candidate sample from El-Badry et al. (2026) and fit to their intrinsic orbital properties, derived from modeling the full Gaia DR3 as-trometric binary selection function (see Shariat et al. 2026). This analysis provides the Galactic birth rate of Gaia NSs, allowing us to connect the selected bi-naries to the kick distribution of all NS births. Dis-berg et al. (2026) test fixed kick prescriptions within bi-nary populations. With their default COMPAS common-envelope model, they find that high pulsar-like natal kicks ( vkick ≳ 100 km s −1) followed by a fixed 30 km s −1

rocket kick (Hirai et al. 2024) 11 give the best agree-ment with the Gaia NS population. In their two-stage common-envelope model, low natal kicks can reproduce the observed orbits only when the rocket kick is in-cluded; without it, the predicted binaries are too eccen-tric. Even with the rocket kick, the predicted systemic recoils remain lower than their kinematic estimates un-less additional heating is invoked. Our empirical model favors progenitors that are wider (∼ 0.1–1 au), inferred directly from the observed or-bits. It reproduces the 27 Gaia NS binaries without a

Thus, within our model, the Gaia NS kinematics favor MHe = 4–6 M ⊙,and do not require large natal kick velocities. We do not follow the preceding mass transfer history and therefore cannot determine how these binaries remained wide or whether they experienced common-envelope evolution. However, Gaia DR3 has revealed WD+MS binaries in au-scale post-interaction orbits (e.g., Shahaf et al. 2024; Yamaguchi et al. 2024a,b, 2025), and van Son et al. (2026) find that Gaia NS orbits overlap those of other post-mass transfer binaries. These systems do not estab-lish how Gaia NS binaries formed, but they show that binary interaction can leave compact remnants in simi-larly wide orbits. The different kick inferences between the two works thus primarily depend on the assumed pre-supernova population and its binary evolution his-tory. G.3. Be+NSs 

Both studies analyze the same N = 23 Galactic Be+NS binaries compiled by Valli et al. (2025) and agree that the low-e systems are consistent with natal kicks 

≲ 10 km s −1. The main difference concerns the narrow high-e sequence. None of the five standard prescriptions considered by Disberg et al. (2026) reproduce this se-quence from their COMPAS progenitors. They argue that the observed period–eccentricity relation may already have been present before the supernova and then pre-served by a weak natal kick, with the eccentric progen-itors possibly formed through mergers in triples. Our fiducial model instead begins with circular progenitors (but see Appendix B) and produces most of the high-e

sequence using larger kicks from the pulsar-kick com-ponent (Figure 4). It therefore predicts that the high-

e binaries should have larger systemic velocities than the low-e binaries. Interestingly, the observed high-e

Be+NS binaries are indeed faster than the low-e bina-ries (Figure 8). While this provides tentative evidence for the high-e Be+NS binaries receiving larger systemic recoil kicks, the samples remain too small to determine whether the difference is caused by natal kicks, mass-loss recoil, or pre-supernova motion. G.4. NS-LMXBs 

Disberg et al. (2026) find that low-kick models un-derpredict NS-LMXB systemic velocities and therefore favor pulsar-like natal kicks for these systems. After ap-plying our inferred universal kick law to a simple popu-lation of compact LMXB progenitors, we similarly find that 80% of LMXBs arise from the pulsar-kick compo-nent. The predicted median recoil is 158 km s −1 (86– 258 km s −1), consistent with the observed 136 km s −1

79–233 km s −1 from O’Doherty et al. (2023) (Figure 7). The different Gaia NS and NS-LMXB kinematics can therefore arise from the same parent bimodal kick law. G.5. Double Neutron Stars 

For DNSs, Disberg et al. (2026) show that two eccen-tricity branches do not require two natal kick popula-tions. Weak second-supernova kicks combined with the nonmonotonic remnant-mass relation studied by Grich-ener et al. (2026) produce two ranges of ejecta mass, and therefore two ranges of Blaauw recoil and eccen-tricity. Our joint fit focuses on Gaia NS and Be+NS binaries, whose orbits provide a more direct record of a single supernova. However, given their conclusion, the DNS result would be consistent with the low-kick component.
