## 摘要
中子星（NS）在超新星爆发期间会获得诞生踢，这会影响它们在双星中的存活以及在银河系中的运动。这些踢速度在中子星群体中的总体分布仍不确定。在这里，我们将 Gaia NS 和 Be+NS 双星的轨道性质与诞生率，与来自年轻孤立脉冲星的速度约束结合起来，构建了一个中子星诞生踢的一致模型。我们发现，这三类群体可以共同用一个共享的双峰踢分布来描述。低速成分遵循对数正态分布，μ_ln v = 1.87^{+0.65}_{-0.53}，σ_ln v = 0.55^{+0.85}_{-0.44}，对应中位速度为 6.5^{+5.9}_{-2.7} km s^{-1}。高速成分与从年轻脉冲星推断出的分布一致，其峰值接近 200 km s^{-1}。低速成分仅贡献约 10% 的所有中子星诞生，但产生了大多数 Gaia NS 双星以及大约一半的 Be+NS 双星。同一模型预测的系统反冲速度与观测到的 Gaia NS、Be+NS 双星和中子星低质量 X 射线双星的运动学一致。对于形成历史仍未知的 Gaia NS，我们利用这一踢分布来约束其前身星。我们倾向于超新星爆发前含有中等质量剥离星、伴星质量约为 1 M⊙、轨道尺度为 au 的双星，并预测当今银河系中约有 300 个此类双星，LMC 中约有 40 个，SMC 中约有 30 个。最后，我们考虑这些踢对层级三体系统中中子星的影响。我们发现，2-10% 的 Gaia NS 三体系统和 3-23% 的 Be+NS 三体系统在超新星爆发后保留了其第三体。总体而言，我们的结果表明，一个共享的诞生踢规律可以解释孤立脉冲星和幸存的中子星双星，其中大多数中子星获得大踢速度，而低踢速度在幸存双星中很常见。

## Abstract
Neutron stars (NSs) receive natal kicks during supernovae that affect their survival in binaries and motion through the Galaxy. The global distribution of these kick velocities across the NS population remains uncertain. Here, we combine the orbital properties and birth rates of Gaia NS and Be+NS binaries with velocity constraints from young isolated pulsars to construct a concordance model of NS natal kicks. We find that all three populations can be jointly described by a shared bimodal kick distribution. The low-velocity component follows a lognormal distribution with $μ_{\ln v}=1.87^{+0.65}_{-0.53}$ and $σ_{\ln v}=0.55^{+0.85}_{-0.44}$, corresponding to a median speed of $6.5^{+5.9}_{-2.7}~{\rm km~s^{-1}}$. The high-velocity component is consistent with the distribution inferred from young pulsars, which peaks near $200~{\rm km~s^{-1}}$. The low-velocity component contributes only $\sim10\%$ of all NS births, but produces most Gaia NS binaries and roughly half of Be+NS binaries. The same model predicts systemic recoil velocities consistent with the observed kinematics of Gaia NSs, Be+NS binaries, and NS low-mass X-ray binaries. For Gaia NSs, whose formation history remains unknown, we use this kick distribution to constrain their progenitors. We favor pre-supernova binaries containing intermediate-mass stripped stars with $\sim1~{\rm M_\odot}$ companions in au-scale orbits, and predict $\sim300$ such binaries in the Milky Way today, $\sim40$ in the LMC, and $\sim30$ in the SMC. Finally, we consider the implications of these kicks for NSs in hierarchical triples. We find that 2-10$\%$ of Gaia NS triples and 3-23$\%$ of Be+NS triples retain their tertiary after the supernova. Overall, our results suggest that a shared natal kick law can account for isolated pulsars and surviving NS binaries, where most NSs receive large kicks and low kicks are common among surviving binaries.
