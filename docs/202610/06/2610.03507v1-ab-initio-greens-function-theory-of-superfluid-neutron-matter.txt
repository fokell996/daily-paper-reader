At the low densities encountered in the inner crust of neutron stars, neutron matter becomes superfluid, directly influencing macroscopic phenomena such as star cooling. However, a precise quantitative understanding of this system has been hindered by uncontrolled many-body approxi-mations. In this letter, we exploit the nonperturbative Gorkov algebraic diagrammatic construction method to provide a unified ab initio description of the equation of state and the single-particle spectral functions of homogeneous neutron matter, spanning from the dilute regime to saturation density. We identify clear microscopic signatures of superfluidity in the excitation spectrum and quantify the pairing gaps associated with realistic chiral Hamiltonians, estimating for the first time their sensitivity to the many-body truncation. Our robust handling of both dynamical and pairing correlations paves the way for exploring electroweak processes across the regimes relevant to neutron stars. 

Introduction. Homogeneous nuclear matter, an ex-tended system of nucleons interacting through the strong force, bridges the vastly different scales of finite nuclei and neutron stars [1–4]. A combination of laboratory ex-periments [5, 6], astrophysical observations [7, 8], and ac-curate nuclear ab initio computations [9–11], often based on Hamiltonians derived within chiral effective field the-ory [12, 13], provides constraints on the equation of state (EOS) of neutron matter at the intermediate densities characteristic of the outer core of neutron stars ( ρ ≈ 1– 2 ρ0, where ρ0 ≈ 0.16 fm −3). A different physical regime characterizes the inner crust, where neutron-rich nuclei coexist with a dilute neu-tron gas at nearly zero temperature [14, 15]. At such low densities, neutron-neutron interactions are dominated by the attractive 1S0 channel, rendering the system unstable to the formation of Cooper pairs and driving the onset of superfluidity [16–20]. Superfluidity modifies neutrino emission and absorption processes [19, 21–23] and the rotational dynamics (including pulsar glitches) of neu-tron stars [15, 24]. Vice versa, observations of the cool-ing of isolated neutron stars might help constrain mi-croscopic properties, for example, the magnitude of sin-glet gaps [25] or the critical temperature for triplet pair-ing [26, 27]. The standard description of nuclear superfluidity is based on BCS mean-field theory, which predicts pairing gaps, the energy required to break a Cooper pair, under the assumption of weak interactions [18, 19]. This as-sumption is however questionable in strongly correlated nuclear systems and has motivated beyond-mean-field computations (see e.g. [18, 28–36]). Although beyond-mean-field corrections are generally found to suppress the 1S0 gap relative to BCS predictions [3], superfluid pairing gaps remain substantially less constrained than the EOS. This owes to both their exponential sensitivity to medium effects [19] and the lack, in past calculations, of an assessment of theoretical uncertainties. The use of uncontrolled approximations, in particular, has hin-dered, so far, a rigorous understanding of the pairing properties of chiral Hamiltonians beyond the mean-field theory [32, 37]. In this letter, we determine the pairing gaps from full-fledged simulations of the Landau quasiparticle struc-ture of neutron matter. We estimate, for the first time, the uncertainties associated with the many-body trun-cation. These achievements are made possible by com-bining the nonperturbative algebraic diagrammatic con-struction (ADC) method with the Gorkov Green’s func-tion framework [10, 38]. By providing momentum- and energy-resolved spectral functions, we elucidate in Fig. 1 the normal-liquid and superfluid regimes of neutron mat-ter and identify microscopic signatures of pairing corre-lations.

While pre-vious studies have highlighted that subspace-projection techniques can capture crossovers between qualitatively different regimes [66, 67], our results indicate that super-fluid solutions do not emerge from the normal-state CCD extrapolation considered here (see also [68]). The discrepancy between ADC(3) and CCD is largest in the region kF ≃ 0.4–0 .6 fm −1, where it reaches nearly 20% of the total energy. At very low densities, kF ∼ 0.1– 0.2 fm −1, the neutron gas becomes sufficiently dilute that interaction effects are strongly suppressed. Consequently, all many-body calculations approach the free Fermi gas energy EF G . The enhancement of the difference between the paired and normal solutions - HFB and HF at the mean-field level, ADC(3) and CCD at the correlated level - around kF ≃ 0.4–0 .6 fm −1 is consistent with the den-sity region in which the pairing gap is maximal (shaded band; see also Fig. 3), and thus constitutes an EOS-level signature of the superfluid phase. 

Conclusions and perspectives. In this work, we have extended the ab initio Gorkov-ADC method to super-fluid neutron matter. We have shown that superfluidity induces a distinct qualitative change in the EOS con-cavity that normal-state frameworks, such as normal-state coupled-cluster, cannot capture. The pair for-mation mechanism has been elucidated by considering the energy- and momentum-resolved one-neutron spec-tral functions. From these, we have evaluated the pairing gaps in the low-density regime, estimating the sensitivity to the many-body scheme by comparing different trun-cations in the ADC hierarchy. 5Our findings demonstrate the role of dynamical self-energy effects in suppressing the pairing gaps in compar-ison to the mean-field (HFB) prediction from the same Hamiltonian. Once many-body correlations (in partic-ular, ladder diagrams) are properly included, gaps are relatively well constrained up to about kF = 0 .8 fm −1,showing a small residual method uncertainty and a re-markable consistency across different potentials. Ultimately, Gorkov-ADC provides a single, robust framework for understanding neutron matter from low to high densities, giving valuable insights for modeling the neutron-star crust matter. Our results pave the way for investigating the impact of pairing and many-body dynamical correlations on the electroweak processes that drive neutrino emission rates [23, 69–72] and strongly af-fect the thermal evolution of neutron stars [73, 74].
