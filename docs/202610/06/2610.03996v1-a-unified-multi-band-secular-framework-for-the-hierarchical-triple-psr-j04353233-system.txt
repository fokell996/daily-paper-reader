ABSTRACT The millisecond pulsar PSR J0435+3233, discovered by FAST, exhibits an unusually large apparent spin-down rate, potentially reflecting line-of-sight acceleration from a distant companion. Recent investigations reveal a hierarchical triple consisting of an inner pulsar–helium white dwarf binary with an orbital period of ∼ 8 days, orbited by a G-type subgiant tertiary on an eccentric ∼ 74 yr orbit. The current timing description admits two distinct geometrical solutions. We develop a singly averaged (SA) formulation that averages over the inner orbit while retaining the instantaneous phase of the eccentric outer orbit, thereby capturing the time-dependent quadrupolar perturbations induced by the tertiary. We show how this physical three-body description can be incorporated naturally into pulsar-timing analyses and use it to forward-propagate the two currently viable configurations, Tv1 and Tv2, to forecast when their timing signatures become distinguishable with FAST. The SA evolution predicts that the two configurations will become distinguishable in the coming years, with the effective projected semi-major axis, x1,eff , providing the earliest discriminating observable. We further extend the framework to predict the tertiary’s spectroscopic radial-velocity and astrometric evolution, linking FAST timing with LAMOST time-domain spectroscopy and Gaia astrometry. This multi-band framework has the potential to establish PSR J0435+3233 as an exceptionally well-constrained laboratory for three-body dynamics, compact-binary and stellar evolution, and gravitational physics.

Subsequent independent investigations by P. C. C. Freire et al. (2026) and Z. L. Yang et al. (2026) (hereafter Y26) successfully resolved this anomalous motion. Combining a reanalysis of the FAST archive with 

∼16.7 years of γ-ray pulsations from the Fermi Large Area Telescope (LAT; W. B. Atwood et al. 2009; S. Abdollahi et al. 2020, 2022) and astrometric infor-mation from Gaia (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2016), Y26 established the triple nature of PSR J0435+3233 and associated the bright optical/infrared source Gaia DR3 171833395176138240 with its tertiary com-panion. The latter is a Sun-like G-type subgiant with an inferred mass of M⋆ ≃ 0.98–1 .12 M⊙, orbiting the inner pulsar–white dwarf binary on a wide ( P2 ≃ 74 yr) and highly eccentric ( e2 ≃ 0.5983) outer orbit. This effort established PSR J0435+3233 as only the third known MSP residing in a hierarchical triple system, and only the second such system identified in the Galactic field (Y26). To map the three-dimensional architecture of the sys-tem, Y26 combined high-precision FAST radio pulse times of arrival (ToAs) with ∼16.7 years of Fermi -LAT gamma-ray pulsation timing. These timing data sets were further complemented by Gaia DR3 (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023) astrometric, distance, and mass constraints on the subgiant counterpart. By mod-eling the tertiary-induced non-Keplerian variations of the inner orbital parameters using these datasets, they constrained the outer orbital parameters. Specifically, Y26’s joint Bayesian analysis using nested-sampling yielded the following two degenerate three-dimensional geometries: 

• Solution Tv1 : Characterized by a nearly perpen-dicular mutual orbital inclination of iX ≃ 83 .8◦ be-tween the inner and outer orbits, with component masses of MPSR ≈ 1.15 M⊙, MWD ≈ 0.271 M⊙,and a subgiant tertiary mass of M⋆ ≈ 0.96 M⊙.

• Solution Tv2 : Characterized by a moderate mu-tual inclination of iX ≃ 55 .0◦, with corresponding component masses of MPSR ≈ 1.29 M⊙, MWD ≈

0.296 M⊙, and M⋆ ≈ 1.12 M⊙.It is important to note that while both solutions de-scribe the current baseline of FAST timing observations with similar timing residual statistics, they imply fun-damentally different orbital configurations and mass hi-erarchies (Y26). Because the tertiary star is currently moving toward its next periastron passage, scheduled for mid-2036 as evident from Table 4 of Y26, the rapidly strengthening tidal perturbation exerted by the outer orbit on the inner binary, and the resulting evolution of the inner-orbit geometry, provide a natural opportunity to break this degeneracy. Building directly upon this breakthrough by Y26, we employ a singly averaged (SA) formulation of HT dy-namics in which the rapid motion of the inner binary is orbit-averaged, while the instantaneous position of the tertiary along its eccentric outer orbit is retained. This allows us to incorporate naturally the time-dependent quadrupolar interaction between the inner and outer orbits, influenced by L. Luo et al. (2016); S. Toonen et al. (2016); A. S. Hamers (2020). The SA formulation turned out to be particularly well suited for describing the observationally relevant dynamics of the HT asso-ciated with PSR J0435+3233. Specifically, it predicts the continuous evolution of the inner orbital geometry, which can be mapped directly onto pulsar timing observ-ables such as the projected semi-major axis and its tem-poral variation. The resulting dynamical timing model can therefore be incorporated naturally into the anal-ysis of radio pulse ToAs measured using FAST. It re-places the phenomenological description of orbital evo-lution through higher-order time derivatives of the inner orbital elements, as pursued in Y26, with a physically motivated and self-consistent evolution governed by the underlying three-body dynamics. Thereafter, we employ our SA framework to forward propagate the competing Tv1 and Tv2 solutions, con-struct their corresponding FAST timing forecasts, and determine when their predicted timing signatures should become statistically distinguishable with continued ob-servations. Interestingly, our SA framework reveals that, although the Tv1 and Tv2 solutions remain nearly de-generate over the existing ∼4.6-yr FAST timing base-line, their predicted trajectories should become distin-guishable well before the 2036 outer periastron. The separation appears first in x1,eff , which is particularly sensitive to the different inclination evolution of the two three-dimensional configurations, while differences in the eccentricity-vector components ϵ1 and ϵ2 become Dynamical Forecasts for the Hierarchical Triple PSR J0435 +3233 System 3prominent subsequently. We forecast that extending FAST timing by only another 2 years, will produce a separation of ∆ x1,eff ∼ 100 μs between the two predicted trajectories. This also explains why the configurations could not be distinguished with the existing data: over the current baseline, both remain locally well described by the phenomenological Taylor expansions employed in Y26, whereas their subsequent nonlinear evolution in-creasingly departs from such local descriptions as the tertiary advances toward periastron. Continued FAST timing should therefore distinguish the two physical con-figurations in the near future, without requiring obser-vations extending to the 2036 periastron passage. Additionally, we demonstrate how our SA framework provides a natural route toward a unified multi-band description of PSR J0435+3233. The three-dimensional relative separation and velocity vectors of the outer or-bit, R(t) and V(t), connect the same dynamical model directly to the spectroscopic radial-velocity and astro-metric signatures of the optically identified tertiary, Gaia DR3 171833395176138240. This opens the possi-bility of a joint Bayesian analysis in which FAST timing, LAMOST (G. Zhao et al. 2012; X.-Q. Cui et al. 2012; H. Yan et al. 2022) time-domain spectroscopy, and Gaia 

astrometry are simultaneously fitted using a common set of masses and three-dimensional orbital parameters. Such a joint analysis should substantially reduce the ge-ometric and mass degeneracies present in any individ-ual data set and ultimately provide a more complete three-dimensional description of the hierarchical triple PSR J0435+3233 system. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2.2, we introduce the SA description of the PSR J0435+3233 hierarchical triple, following a brief overview of Y26. Section 2.3 incorporates the SA dy-namics into the pulsar-timing framework, while Sec-tion 2.4 presents forecasts for distinguishing the Tv1 and Tv2 solutions with continued FAST timing. Sec-tions 3.1 and 3.2 discuss the complementary spectro-scopic and astrometric prospects, respectively. Section 4 summarizes our main findings and outlines ongoing and planned follow-up efforts.

More importantly, the pre-dicted evolution of all four dynamical variables over the coming years is substantially larger than the current measurement uncertainties of their corresponding tim-ing observables. This provides a strong observational motivation for incorporating our SA formulation directly into the timing analysis of PSR J0435+3233, allowing the measured evolution to be described by the underly-ing three-body dynamics rather than through a succes-sion of phenomenological higher-order derivatives. It is useful to contrast the present SA formulation with the doubly averaged (DA) treatment commonly employed to investigate the long-term secular evolution of HTs (A. Gopakumar et al. 2009; A. Shukla et al. 2025). For example, one of us recently employed a quadrupolar-order DA description to constrain the pro-posed tertiary companion to the eccentric MSP binary PSR J1618 −3921 (A. Shukla et al. 2025). In the DA approximation, the perturbing Hamiltonian is averaged over the mean anomalies of both the inner and outer orbits. Consequently, the explicit dependence on the instantaneous outer orbital phase is removed, yielding equations that describe the long-term secular evolution of quantities such as the inner eccentricity and argument of periastron. Such an approach is well suited to probing the cumulative influence of a very wide tertiary when its orbital phase is poorly constrained and the observational quantities of interest are predominantly secular. The same approximation is not appropriate for our present purpose in PSR J0435+3233. We now investigate whether the inner-orbit mirror ambiguity can be resolved through SA three-body evo-lution. This is done by pursuing an additional SA ex-periment in which the three-dimensional configuration of the outer orbit is held fixed, as would be possible once its orientation is constrained astrometrically. We then evolve two inner-orbit configurations related by the reflection i1 → 180 ◦ − i1, which are indistinguishable through observables depending only on sin i1 at the ref-erence epoch. This numerical experiment is motivated by the explicitly orientation-dependent structure of the SA equations, which contain terms such as ( j1·ˆR)( j1×ˆR). Under inner-orbit reflection i1 → 180 ◦ − i1, while the three-dimensional outer orbit is kept fixed, these com-binations are not, in general, invariant. This suggests that the tertiary-induced torque may drive measurably different subsequent evolution of the two initially mirror-degenerate inner configurations. Influenced by these considerations, we propagate both inner binary con-figurations using the same SA equations and compare their predicted x1,eff (t) trajectories. As shown in Fig. 6, the two initially mirror-degenerate configurations follow substantially different x1,eff (t) trajectories when evolved under the same SA equations. The difference between the two configurations becomes particularly pronounced around the outer periastron passage, where the tertiary-induced interaction is strongest. This demonstrates that, once the three-dimensional orientation of the outer orbit is sufficiently constrained through the combina-tion of FAST timing, LAMOST spectroscopy, and Gaia 

astrometry, the subsequent time-dependent three-body dynamics can break the remaining inner-orbit mirror de-generacy. In particular, the two mirror configurations predict distinct evolutions of x1,eff (t), which can then be distinguished using FAST timing alone. We conclude by emphasizing that our SA frame-work provides a natural means of combining the sub-microsecond timing precision of FAST, the potential for long-term RV monitoring with LAMOST, and the high-precision astrometric and geometrical constraints pro-vided by Gaia . Together, these complementary observa-tional probes, interpreted within a common three-body dynamical framework, have the potential to transform PSR J0435+3233 into an exceptionally well-constrained laboratory for investigating hierarchical three-body dy-namics, compact-binary and stellar evolution, and grav-itational physics.

A particularly compelling application of PSR J0435+3233 is a strong-field test of the univer-sality of free fall (UFF), following the classic proposal of T. Damour & G. Schaefer (1991). If strongly self-gravitating bodies fall differently in an external gravi-tational field, the neutron star and its WD companion experience a differential acceleration that polarizes the inner orbit, generating a forced component of its ec-centricity vector (T. Damour & G. Schaefer 1991). We note that the coplanar HT in PSR J0337+1715 pro-vides a particularly clean realization of this strong-field Nordtvedt experiment, with the outer WD supplying the external gravitational field (K. Nordtvedt 1968; A. M. Archibald et al. 2018). No evidence for such a differential acceleration has been found. Most recently, G. Voisin et al. (2025) constrained the UFF-violation parameter to |∆| < 1.5 × 10 −6 or |∆| < 2.3 × 10 −6

at 95% confidence, depending on whether the observed long-period timing signal is modeled as a circum-triple planet or as achromatic red noise. This model depen-dence highlights the importance of an accurate dynam-ical description when searching for such subtle strong-field signatures. PSR J0435+3233 may eventually provide an even more sensitive realization of this experiment (P. C. C. Freire et al. 2026). Its inner orbit is timed with FAST at high precision; its projected pulsar semi-major axis is substantially larger than that of PSR J0337+1715, and its optically visible tertiary provides independent spectroscopic and astrometric probes of the outer or-bit. More importantly, its long and eccentric outer or-bit causes the external gravitational field to vary pre-dictably in both magnitude and direction, providing 18 Gopakumar et al. 

a time-dependent UFF signature rather than a purely static orbital polarization. Our SA formulation is partic-ularly well suited to exploiting this feature because it di-rectly evolves the eccentricity vector e1(t) while retain-ing the instantaneous position of the tertiary. A UFF-violating differential acceleration can therefore be incor-porated as an additional forcing term and constrained simultaneously with the standard Newtonian three-body and relativistic contributions. Continued FAST tim-ing, particularly toward the 2036 outer periastron, may therefore allow PSR J0435+3233 to improve upon exist-ing strong-field UF constraints. PSR J0435+3233 also provides an unusually well-constrained laboratory for studying compact-binary and stellar evolution. The ≃ 8-day MSP–helium-WD inner binary carries the imprint of the mass-transfer episode that recycled the pulsar, and its present orbital period and WD mass can be confronted directly with the ex-pected Porb –MWD relation for recycled pulsar binaries (T. M. Tauris & G. J. Savonije 1999; A. G. Istrate et al. 2014). The surviving G-type subgiant tertiary provides complementary information about the evolutionary his-tory of the primordial triple, whose present architecture can retain signatures of stellar mass loss, neutron-star formation and its associated natal kick, subsequent mass transfer, and long-term three-body evolution (J. T. Pi-jloo et al. 2012; S. Toonen et al. 2016; Y26). Measurements of the three component masses, the in-ner and outer orbital elements, and particularly the mu-tual orbital inclination can therefore be confronted with evolutionary calculations aimed at reconstructing the formation history of the entire system. Moreover, spec-troscopy of the tertiary can constrain its effective tem-perature, surface gravity, metallicity, mass, and evolu-tionary state, providing an independent stellar-evolution constraint on the age and history of the system. Com-bining these measurements with the exceptionally pre-cise dynamical information available from pulsar timing should make it possible to test whether a single evo-lutionary scenario can simultaneously account for the MSP–helium-WD binary, the surviving tertiary, and the present three-dimensional architecture of this remark-able HT (J. T. Pijloo et al. 2012; P. C. C. Freire et al. 2026; Y26). Our SA formulation, together with the multi-technique follow-up strategy advocated here, provides a natural framework for addressing several of these out-standing questions concerning the formation and evo-lution of the system, while simultaneously exploiting PSR J0435+3233 as a laboratory for precision tests of gravity. As a critical first step, the singly averaged 

ELL1H timing model presented in this work is currently being implemented in the pulsar-timing software pack-age PINT (J. Luo et al. 2021; A. Susobhanan et al. 2024). By combining the dynamical information from high-precision FAST timing with independent spectroscopic and astrometric constraints on the tertiary, it should become possible to reconstruct the evolutionary history and three-dimensional architecture of the system while searching for subtle departures from general-relativistic dynamics, including possible violations of the universal-ity of free fall.
