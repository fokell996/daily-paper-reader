Testing internal dynamics in neutron stars with radio pulsar timing 

Reginald Christian Bernardo 1

> 1Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics (Albert Einstein Institute), Leibniz Universität Hannover, Callinstrasse 38, D-30167 Hannover, Germany

ABSTRACT Rotational variations in pulsars reflect either internal angular momentum exchange via equal and opposite torques (stationary spin noise) or independent torques causing diffusive changes in total angular momentum (nonstationary spin noise). We compare two models of a radio-emitting crust and interior superfluid to test the stationarity of angular velocity fluctuations through observed pulsar phases. We present results for three isolated pulsars with measurable timing noise, demonstrating distinct inference outcomes. For PSR J1359-6038, the data mildly favor independent torques over equal-and-opposite torques, with an odds ratio of seven hundred to one. For PSR B1937+21, the analysis shows no clear preference between the models. For Vela in a pre-glitch interval, excess scatter and unexplained residual structure limit the interpretation. 

Keywords: Pulsars (1306) — Neutron stars (1108) — Astrostatistics (1882) — Gravitational waves (678) 

1. INTRODUCTION Pulsar timing measurements reveal stochastic rotational variations that reflect torques acting on neutron stars (E. J. Groth 1975; G. Hobbs et al. 2010). Radio pulses, emitted from the magnetosphere, track the rotation of the charged crust. However, neutron stars contain a superfluid interior that can rotate differentially and exchange angular momen-tum with the crust through mutual friction (R. Prix 1999, 2004; N. Andersson et al. 2006; B. Haskell & A. Sedrakian 2018). Thus, observed crustal fluctuations may arise from internal angular momentum redistribution within the star or from external torques that alter its total angular momentum. We compare two dynamical two-component models of pul-sar rotation that differ in their stochastic torque assumptions. In the singular model, independent torques act on the crust and superfluid, driving a diffusive total angular momentum mode and producing nonstationary spin noise, along with a damped crust-core spin lag (P. M. Meyers et al. 2021a,b; R. C. Bernardo 2026). In the minimal model, equal and opposite torques conserve total angular momentum, producing station-ary spin noise (M. Antonelli et al. 2023, 2025). Both models include mutual friction between the crust and superfluid, en-abling angular momentum exchange. The key difference lies in the torque correlations, which determine the stationarity of the spin noise: nonstationary for the singular model, station-ary for the minimal model. Our inference tests these torque statistics and their associated rotational dynamics.

Correlations between radio pulsar timing and continuous gravitational wave signals have been developed as probes of neutron star interiors (B. Haskell & A. Sedrakian 2018; B. Haskell & K. We apply the framework to three isolated pulsars, chosen to illustrate different inference outcomes and to represent diverse astro-physical contexts: J1359-6038, a reference timing-noise case (N. J. O’Neill et al. 2024); B1937+21, a millisecond pulsar central to nanohertz gravitational wave searches (G. The timing data include deterministic dispersion correc-tions from the published timing models. We additionally test whether stochastic dispersion measure (DM) variations affect the comparison. For J1359-6038 and Vela, previous studies motivate these checks (N. J. O’Neill et al. 2024; W. Dong et al. 2026; R. M. Shannon et al. 2016), but the lim-ited radio frequency coverage prevents these variations from being constrained independently. We therefore use specified DM amplitudes and temporal spectra. J1359-6038 retains its preference for singular dynamics across the three tested spectra using an amplitude measured from later MeerTime observations (M. J. Keith et al. 2024). Vela also retains a positive relative Bayes factor under the adopted DM stress test, with a spectrum dependent strength. For B1937+21, the wider frequency coverage allows a DM offset to be fitted within each session without a temporal DM spectrum, giv-ing ln 𝐵 𝑠,𝑚 = 1.37 ± 0.07. OUTLOOK Radio timing probes angular momentum exchange inside a neutron star because the correlations between the stochastic torques determine the fluctuations observed in the crust. Min-imal dynamics describe equal and opposite stochastic torques, conserve the total angular momentum and produce stationary angular velocity fluctuations (M. Antonelli et al. 2023). Sin-gular dynamics allow independent torques, fluctuations in the total angular momentum and nonstationary angular velocity fluctuations (P. M. Meyers et al. 2021a). Radio observations, however, directly trace only the crust, and different internal dynamics can produce similar crustal phase histories. In minimal dynamics (M. Antonelli et al. 2023), conser-vation of total angular momentum fixes 𝐼 𝑐 𝛿 Ω𝑐 + 𝐼 𝑠 𝛿 Ω𝑠 to a constant; or, at the level of the phase 

𝑥 𝑐 𝛿𝜙 𝑐 + 𝑥 𝑠 𝛿𝜙 𝑠 = 0 , (1) with 𝑥 𝑐 + 𝑥 𝑠 = 1. A constraint on the participating moment of inertia fraction fixes both the relative amplitude and the opposite sign of the two phase fluctuations. Radio timing alone does not determine this relative scale. An independent measurement of the superfluid phase enables testing the an-gular momentum exchange hypotheses (spin noise models) directly. An additional constraint on the superfluid moment of inertia fraction would overconstrain the minimal model. Singular dynamics make a different prediction (P. M. Mey-ers et al. 2021a). Even at a fixed superfluid moment of inertia fraction, independent stochastic torques leave a distribution of possible superfluid phase histories. An independent phase measurement could be compared with this distribution and provide information about the relaxation time and stochas-tic torques that cannot be obtained from the crust alone. The same observation could test either hypothesis: the exact phase relation predicted by minimal dynamics or the conditional phase distribution predicted by singular dynamics. The three applications separate relative model preference from physical interpretation. J1359-6038 mildly favors sin-gular dynamics, B1937+21 gives weak separation, and Vela remains limited by unresolved noise despite its large rela-tive Bayes factor. The framework also predicts the rotational behavior of the unobserved component. An independent rota-tional phase, together with information about the participating superfluid moment of inertia, would extend radio timing into a direct test of internal angular momentum exchange. Con-tinuous gravitational wave observations may provide such a measurement if the emission phase can be tied to the super-fluid rotation (B. Haskell & K. Schwenzer 2021; M. Antonelli et al. 2025). Joint radio timing and continuous gravitational wave observations could test whether pulsar spin noise arises from balanced internal torques or independent torques. PRIORS AND INFERENCE SUMMARIES Table 2 summarizes the inference results and priors for the minimal (stationary spin noise, internal angular momentum exchange) and singular (nonstationary spin noise, indepen-dent torques) models for each pulsar. Uniform priors apply to the logarithmic spin noise coordinates. The minimal radio likelihood is independent of the superfluid moment of inertia fraction, so its normalized prior integrates out and fixed- and free-inertia cases have identical evidences. The hidden phase predictions of both models, however, depend on this prior. B1937+21 and Vela use broad spin priors. For B1937+21, we also perform conditional fits over a grid of fixed superfluid moment of inertia fractions ( 𝑥 s = 0.01 , 0.02 , · · · 0.10) using the released DMX product (G. Agazie et al. 2023). The reported conditional comparisons pass their numerical checks (Appendix B); these fits should not be interpreted as an inertia posterior. Vela is also analyzed with a narrower relaxation time prior. For B1937+21 we also test how restricting the relaxation time and torque priors affects the evidence. The same nu-meric bounds are applied to each model’s torque coordinates, but because the parameters differ, these are not identical phys-ical priors. We estimate the evidence impact with the fraction of prior and posterior samples inside the restricted region, or the region that was kept after the original prior was trimmed down. Repeating the posterior sampling and evidence calcu-lations with restricted priors turned in consistent results.
