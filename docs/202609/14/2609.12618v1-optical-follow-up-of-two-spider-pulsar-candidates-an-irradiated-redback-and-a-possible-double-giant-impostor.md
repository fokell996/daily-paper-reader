## 摘要
致密双星毫秒脉冲星（绰号“蜘蛛”）是一类数量不断增长的中子星：银河系场中已知超过100个系统，其中包括30多个尚未探测到脉冲信号的候选体。在此，我们报告对两个蜘蛛脉冲星候选体的光学后续观测结果：2FGL J0846.0+2820（J0846，一个轨道周期为8.1天的猎人蛛候选体）和4FGL J0935.3+0901（J0935，轨道周期为2.4小时，曾被提议为过渡型、黑寡妇或红背蜘蛛亚类中的一种蜘蛛）。我们还分析了它们的多波段性质（红外、X射线和γ射线波段），以评估其本质和当前状态。J0846的光学轨道光变曲线从2023年的双峰变为2024年的单峰，但我们认为它们与辐照驱动或椭球轨道调制均不一致。其能谱分布显示出两个黑体状成分的证据，它们的温度（T）和半径（R）相近，分别主导光学（T2≃6000 K，R2≃1.8 R⊙）和红外（T1≃3400 K，R1≃5.2 R⊙）波段。基于此，我们认为J0846并非真正的脉冲星候选体，并提出另一种身份：一个分离的双红巨星。在J0935中，我们发现其光变曲线在伴星上合附近有一个流量极大，此时颜色和推断温度升高，表明脉冲星存在显著的辐照。我们对其伴星基底温度（4000-4600 K）给出了限制，这支持为J0935提出的三种分类之一：处于脉冲星状态的红背蜘蛛。

## Abstract
Compact binary millisecond pulsars (nicknamed "spiders") are a growing class of neutron stars: more than 100 systems are known in the Galactic field, including more than 30 candidates where pulsations have not been detected. Here we report the results of an optical follow-up of two spider pulsar candidates: 2FGL J0846.0+2820 (J0846, a huntsman candidate in an 8.1 d orbit) and 4FGL J0935.3+0901 (J0935, with a 2.4 h orbital period and proposed as a spider of either the transitional, black widow or redback sub-class). We also analyzed their multi-wavelength properties (infrared, X-ray and $γ$-ray bands), in order to assess their nature and current state. The optical orbital light curves of J0846 change from double-peaked in 2023 to single-peaked in 2024, but we argue they are not consistent with either irradiation-driven or ellipsoidal orbital modulation. Its spectral energy distribution shows evidence for two blackbody-like components with similar temperatures ($T$) and radii ($R$), which dominate the optical ($T_2\simeq6000$ K, $R_2\simeq1.8$ R$_\odot$) and infrared ($T_1\simeq3400$ K, $R_1\simeq5.2$ R$_\odot$) bands. Based on this, we argue that J0846 is not a bona fide pulsar candidate and propose an alternative identification: a detached double red giant. In J0935, we find a light curve with one flux maximum around superior conjunction of the companion, where the colors and inferred temperature increase, suggesting prominent irradiation from the pulsar. We place constraints on its companion base temperature ($4000\!-\!4600$ K), which support one of the three classifications proposed for J0935: a redback spider in the pulsar state.
