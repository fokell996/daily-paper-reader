(Dated: September 9, 2026) We show that phase-resolved ultra-high-energy emission from LS I +61 ◦303 defines a critical energetic boundary linking the compact-object engine, circumstellar interaction depth, and dense-matter structure. Combining the GeV–TeV–UHE spectral transition with an orbital acceleration– transparency sieve and branch-resolved stellar sequences for 116 certified EoS, we find that the measured 25–100 TeV tail primarily constrains the circumstellar environment, whereas a more de-manding pure-hadronic > 1 TeV interpretation reaches the physical moment-of-inertia distribution. At 2.0 kpc and 1 .4 M⊙, the latter gives I45 ,crit = 1 .5504; 82 of 116 EoS satisfy the corresponding energetic boundary at ˙Pref = 3 × 10 −15 s s −1. A separately applied tidal diagnostic, Λ 1.4 ≤ 580, is satisfied by 43 EoS, with nine satisfying both conditions. These models span several microscopic classes but occupy a narrow structural region, indicating structural overlap rather than selection of a unique EoS family. A separate GeV power-budget test requires ˙Prot ≳ 5.65 × 10 −14 fΩ s s −1

at 2.0 kpc. For fΩ ∼ 1, the source lies well above the UHE crossing and the EoS discrimination disappears. The UHE observation therefore identifies the engine–environment regime in which dif-ferences in neutron-star structure can become observationally discriminating, rather than defining a universal dense-matter selector. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

The equation of state (EoS) of cold supranuclear mat-ter remains a central problem in relativistic astrophysics. Massive pulsars require viable EoS models to support neutron stars near or above 2 M⊙ [1–4]; NICER con-strains the mass–radius relation [5–8]; and GW170817 constrains the tidal response [9, 10]. Yet substantially different high-density compositions remain compatible with current multimessenger data. We ask whether this residual structural diversity can be exposed by an independently constructed high-energy energetic constraint. We use a heterogeneous library assembled primarily from CompOSE [11–13] and exter-nal tables. After source, support, stability, and branch-resolution checks, 116 EoS are retained, spanning nu-cleonic, relativistic mean-field, crossover, and hybrid de-scriptions, without prior weighting by microscopic family. LS I +61 ◦303 provides such a laboratory. It exhibits orbitally modulated GeV emission from Fermi -LAT [14], TeV emission from MAGIC and VERITAS [15, 16], and UHE emission reported by LHAASO [17]. FAST also detected transient radio pulsations at P = 269 .15508 ±

0.00016 ms, providing direct evidence for a rotating neu-tron star [18]. At UHE energies, acceleration, photon es-cape, hadronic interaction efficiency, and compact-engine power must be satisfied simultaneously. In this Letter these requirements are kept causally sep-arate. Because the intrinsic phase-connected ˙Prot is unknown, we invert the UHE requirement into critical boundaries in spin-down, hadronic interaction depth, and I(M ). Thus the UHE observation iden-tifies an engine–environment regime in which neutron-star structure can become observationally discriminating, rather than a universal dense-matter selector.

> IV. CONCLUSION

We have shown that phase-resolved UHE emission from LS I +61 ◦303 defines an energetic boundary con-necting the compact-object engine, circumstellar inter-action depth, and dense-matter structure. The orbital acceleration–transparency constraint is independent of the EoS, while dense matter enters the energetic problem only through the stellar moment of inertia I(M ). Con-sequently, EoS differences become discriminating only when the system lies sufficiently close to energetic sat-uration. For the measured 25–100 TeV mixed tail, the required interaction depth is compatible with the adopted disk environment and the energetic condition remains viable throughout the certified canonical-mass cohort under the reference assumptions. > Appendix H: Statements intentionally not made

The present analysis does not interpret the UHE data as a universal EoS selector. In particular: 1. no TOV or Love observable modifies the orbital acceleration or γγ -transparency conditions; 2. no radius, tidal-deformability, maximum-mass, or sound-speed score is multiplied into the UHE ener-getic boundary; 3. EoS counts are bookkeeping results for the tested library and are not posterior probabilities; 4. common source systematics shift the energetic boundary but do not provide independent evidence for rank stability, because the fixed-mass ordering is algebraically the inverse-I ordering; 5. the nine-model energetic–tidal intersection is astructural overlap across multiple microscopic fam-ilies, not evidence for a unique preferred family; 6. the pure-hadronic > 1 TeV construction is a lim-iting stress test, not a preferred emission model or an observationally inferred EoS cut; 7. if the phase-averaged GeV component shares the same rotational reservoir with fΩ ≃ 1, the direct GeV closure condition removes the UHE EoS dis-crimination.
