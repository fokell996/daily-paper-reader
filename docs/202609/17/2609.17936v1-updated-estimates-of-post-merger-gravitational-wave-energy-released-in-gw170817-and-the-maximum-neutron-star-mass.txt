1. INTRODUCTION Neutron star (NS) mergers are sensitive probes of dense matter physics through both their gravitational waves (GWs) and remnant properties (Baiotti 2019; Shibata & Hotokezaka 2019; Radice et al. 2020; Dietrich et al. 2021). In particu-lar, the maximum mass of a cold, non-rotating NS ( MTOV ) is fixed by the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equations for a given equation of state (EoS) (Oppenheimer & Volkoff 1939), so a measurement of MTOV would strongly constrain the EoSs proposed currently (Lattimer 2012). The value of MTOV also sets the threshold between prompt collapse and a (possibly long-lived) NS remnant in a binary neutron star (BNS) merger, and therefore impacts the observable signals, e.g., GWs, gamma-ray bursts, and kilonovae (Baiotti & Rezzolla 2017; Bernuzzi 2020). Theoretically, causality arguments allow MTOV ≲ 3.2 M⊙ (Rhoades & Ruffini 1974), and nuclear models compatible with current astrophysical and nuclear-physics constraints suggest somewhat lower limits (e.g., ≲ 2.5 M⊙, Oertel et al. 2017). Observationally, several radio pulsars exceed 2 M⊙, placing a robust lower bound on MTOV (e.g., PSR J0740+6620, Fonseca et al. 2021). Likewise, the total energy emitted in post-merger GWs ( EGW ,p) can be a few percent of the binary’s mass (Bernuzzi et al. 2016; Zappa et al. 2018), carrying information on the remnant’s dynamics and EoS (Takami et al. 2014; Huang et al. 2022). Constraining EGW ,p is therefore crucial: for example, if more energy is radiated away in GWs, then less angular momentum remains in the remnant to support it against collapse, which in turn affects the inference of MTOV (Fan et al. 2013; Shibata et al. 2019; Shao et al. 2020). Although post-merger GW emission was not detected in GW170817 (Abbott et al. 2017a), theoretical models suggest that substantial energy could be radiated in the post-merger phase (Zappa et al. 2018; Torres-Rivas et al. 2019). Indeed, using a combination of the GW data of GW170817 and the NICER mass–radius measurement of PSR J0030+0451, Fan et al. (2020) constrained the EoS and found that the properties of the compact objects involved in GW170817 are likely in favor of a strong post-merger GW emission. Efficient post-merger emission would yield a relatively high 

MTOV , which is helpful in loosening the potential tension with the (indirectly-measured) high masses of some massive pulsars. arXiv:2609.17936v1 [astro-ph.HE] 15 Sep 2026 2Although no additional reliable BNS merger events have been detected in recent years, the NICER collaboration has made significant progress in measuring the radii of the NSs. Following PSR J0030+0451, NICER measured the massive pulsar PSR J0740+6620 (Riley et al. 2021; Miller et al. 2021), whose independently determined mass provides a valuable high-mass benchmark (Fonseca et al. 2021). Updated analyses using several years of NICER data combined with XMM-Newton observations found that the inferred radius of PSR J0740+6620 remains consistent with earlier results (Dittmann et al. 2024; Salmi et al. 2024). This is however not the case for PSR J0030+0451, for which the subsequent re-analysis, incorporating improved instrument calibration and expanded data sets, showed that while the compactness of PSR J0030+0451 is relatively well constrained, its radius estimate depends sensitively on assumptions about surface hot-spot geometry (Vinciguerra et al. 2024). Fortunately, Luo et al. (2024) (see also Biswas & Char 2025) found that the ST+PDT hot-spot configuration (in which one hot spot is modeled as a single-temperature circular region while the other consists of two overlapping components with different temperatures) is statistically preferred. More recently, NICER has released M –R measurements for additional pulsars, including for example PSR J0437–4715 with precise mass determinations (Choudhury et al. 2024). Such a growing set of well-observed pulsars, including PSR J0030+0451, J0740+6620, and PSR J0437–4715, collectively strengthens the empirical basis for EoS inference by reducing biases associated with any single source. Incorporating these new measurements would therefore enable a more precise EoS reconstruction, which in turn allows improved inference of the post-merger GW energy EGW ,p and the maximum nonrotating NS mass MTOV , when combined with the multi-messenger observations of GW170817 (Abbott et al. 2017b,c) and quasi-universal relations (QURs, which are EoS-insensitive). In particular, empirical fits relate the binary tidal parameter κT 

> 2

to the fraction of energy radiated during the post-merger phase (Zappa et al. 2018), and universal relations connect the critical collapse mass to MTOV (Breu & Rezzolla 2016; Shao et al. 2020). Updating these constraints using the latest observational data therefore constitutes the primary motivation of this work. Since the lifetime of the remnant of GW170817 is still under debate, in this work we also treat the collapse time as a free parameter and quantify its impact on the inferred 

MTOV .

(2024) performed a comprehensive Bayesian nonparametric inference of NS EoSs using three independent frameworks: a single-layer feed-forward neural network (Han et al. 2021), a piecewise-linear parametrization of the sound speed (Jiang et al. 2023; Annala et al. 2022), and a Gaussian process (GP) approach (Landry & Essick 2019; Essick et al. 2020). CONSTRAINTS ON MTOV WITHIN THE SUPRAMASSIVE NS SCENARIO OF GW170817 After the merger of a BNS, the outcome can be classified as follows (Baumgarte et al. 2000; Baiotti & Rezzolla 2017; Radice et al. 2020; Bernuzzi 2020): (i) prompt collapse, in which the collapse occurring during the first increasing of the NS central density; (ii) hypermassive NS (HMNS), with a mass exceeding the maximum allowed for uniform rotation and temporarily supported by differential rotation, collapsing within 10–100 ms; (iii) a supramassive NS (SMNS), supported by rigid rotation, surviving much longer (even seconds) until enough angular momentum is lost; and (iv) a stable NS. The first and last outcomes are not the focus of this work, as multiple lines of evidence suggest a delayed collapse for GW170817; in particular, the collapse time of the remnant has been inferred to be tc = 0 .98 +0 .31  

> −0.26

sby Gill et al. (2019). For a highly magnetized neutron star, the magnetic braking timescale of the differential rotation is just ∼ 0.1s (Shapiro 2000; Gao & Fan 2006), so it is reasonable to assume that the remnant of GW170817 was a SMNS rotating uniformly before its collapse if it survived for ≳ 0.1 s. We note, however, that the lifetime of the remnant is still quite uncertain: several recent analyses of the kilonova properties (e.g., the strength of specific spectral features and the inferred electron fraction of the ejecta) favor a considerably shorter lifetime of ∼ 0.1 s or even less (Domoto et al. 2022; Perego et al. 2022; Tarumi et al. 2023; Sneppen et al. 2026; Jacobi et al. 2026), and modern 3D general-relativistic radiation-hydrodynamics simulations reveal additional mass-ejection channels from a long-lived remnant, such as the spiral-wave wind and the neutrino-driven wind (Nedora et al. 2019, 2021; Just et al. 2023), that were not included in the prescription of Gill et al. (2019). Therefore, in addition to the fiducial case anchored to the Gill et al. (2019) estimate, we also treat tc as a free parameter and explore the limiting cases tc = 0 .1, 0 .3, and 1 .0 s.
