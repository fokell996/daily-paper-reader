ABSTRACT We report on the detection of polarized X-ray emission from the new Galactic magnetar EP J223759.5+531421, discovered by the Einstein Probe Wide-field X-ray Telescope on 2026 June 28. The Imaging X-ray Polarimetry Explorer ( IXPE ) follow-up observation started on 2026 July 6 and detected the source at a (absorbed) flux of ≈ 4.5 × 10 −11 erg cm 2 s−1 (2–7 .5 keV range), confirming the presence of two thermally emitting regions on the star surface, with temperatures ≈ 0.5 and ≈ 1 keV. A significant (at > 4σ confidence level) phase- and energy-integrated polarization degree of ≈ 7% was detected with the polarization angle ≈ − 2◦ East of the celestial North. The two thermal components exhibit quite different polarization properties, with the hotter one being more polarized. The difference is larger in the phase-folded data, with the polarization degree reaching ∼ 63% in the range 4–7 .5 keV, in correspondence with the secondary peak of the pulse profile, and never exceeding ∼ 30% at lower energies. The polarization angle continuously oscillates from −90 ◦ to +90 ◦ over one rotational cycle and is well fit by the rotating vector model, with an inclination of the line of sight and of the magnetic dipole axis relative to the star spin axis of ≈ 28 ◦ and ≈ 109 ◦, respectively. The data point to an emission geometry in which the cold thermal component likely originates from two antipodal caps, possibly in a magnetically condensed state, while the hotter one comes from a smaller region covered by an atmosphere.

1. INTRODUCTION On 28 June 2026, the Einstein Probe (EP ; W. Yuan et al. 2022) detected a previously unreported tran-sient X-ray source exhibiting the traits of a potential new magnetar in outburst: unabsorbed 0 .5–10 keV flux 

∼ 10 −10 erg cm −2 s−1, an absorbed power-law spectrum with photon index ∼ 2 and a clear periodic modulation, with P ∼ 3 s (H. N. Yang et al. 2026), later realized to be the first harmonic of a 6 s spin signal (N. Rea et al. 2026). Follow-up observations with EP and NuSTAR con-firmed the source, named EP J223759.5+531421, as a magnetar candidate (N. Rea et al. 2026). The magne-tar nature of EP J223759.5+531421 has been further strengthened by the detection of seven short (20–40 ms) bursts with the ART-XC telescope on board the SRG observatory (S. Molkov et al. 2026), as well as an addi-tional 40 ms burst with ECLAIRs on board SVOM (X. Feng et al. 2026). The source spectrum in the NuSTAR 

observation was well represented by the superposition of two thermal components, with kT w ∼ 0.4 keV and arXiv:2609.25958v1 [astro-ph.HE] 22 Sep 2026 2

kT h ∼ 1 keV, and a power-law tail with spectral index Γ ∼ 1.4 extending to ∼ 30 keV. The neutral hydrogen column density turned out to be NH ∼ 6.2 × 10 21 cm −2,with unabsorbed flux FX ∼ 1.6 × 10 −10 erg cm −2 s−1 in the range 0 .5–10 keV (H. N. Yang et al. 2026). Early searches at radio, optical, and near-infrared wavelengths did not reveal a counterpart (Y. Maan et al. 2026; P. Mo-hapatra et al. 2026; S. Q. Jiang et al. 2026; X. Liu et al. 2026; F. Coti Zelati et al. 2026a). Further monitoring with EP , XMM-Newton , SVOM 

and NuSTAR between June 29 and July 18 2026 provided a more accurate source position at RA =22 ◦37 ′59 ′′ .4 , DEC = +53 ◦14 ′22 ′′ .7 (J2000.0) and apreliminary phase-coherent timing solution with P =5.9958723(4) s and ˙P = 5(1) × 10 −12 s/s (F. Coti Zelati et al. 2026b; D. Gotz et al. 2026). The implied surface dipolar field at the equator is B ∼ 2×10 14 G, confidently qualifying EP J223759.5+531421 as a magnetar. A de-tailed analysis of these and the forthcoming observations will be reported in a separate paper (N. Rea et al. 2026). The IXPE observatory (M. C. Weisskopf et al. 2022) opened a new window in the X-ray sky, finally making it possible to measure the X-ray polarization in magnetar sources (see R. Taverna & R. Turolla 2024, for a sum-mary of IXPE results). In this Letter , we report on the first IXPE observation of EP J223759.5+531421 carried out in response to a ToO proposal (PI: R. Taverna).

OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION 2.1. IXPE 

The IXPE observation of EP J223759.5+531421 started on 2026 July 6 09:18:02.102 UTC and ended on 2026 July 8 16:53:23.425 UTC, for a total elapsed time of ≈ 200 ks. We retrieved level 1 and level 2 data files from the HEASARC IXPE public archive 1

and referred the event arrival times to the Solar Sys-tem barycenter using the ftool barycorr with JPL planetary ephemeris DE440 and the coordinates RA = 22 ◦37 ′59 ′′ .4 , DEC = +53 ◦14 ′22 ′′ .7 (F. Coti Zelati et al. 2026b). Background rejection was performed following the method presented in A. Di Marco et al. (2023), with the appropriate modifications to include the corrections for IXPE detector unit (DU) 2, following the failure experienced in 2025 (see e.g. A. Di Marco 2026). We checked that no solar flares occurred during the obser-vation of EP J223759.5+531421 by examining the back-ground lightcurve (so no excision of time intervals was necessary). The source and background counts were ex-tracted from the processed photon lists in a circle with

radius rsrc and an annulus with inner and outer radii 

rinn bkg and rout bkg , respectively, both centered on the source position. By maximizing the signal-to-noise ratio as dis-cussed in E. Piconcelli et al. (2004), we chose rsrc = 80 ′′ ,

rinn bkg = 120 ′′ and rout bkg = 240 ′′ . A total of ≈ 60 000 net source counts were collected over the three IXPE DUs in the entire 2–8 keV band. 2.2. EP-FXT 

We analysed two observations of EP J223759.5+531421 performed by the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) aboard EP during the IXPE observ-ing window (ObsIDs 08500000643 and 08500000644), to-talling 10 .3 ks of cleaned exposure. We used only FXT-A data, acquired in partial-window imaging mode, because its spectral calibration is better characterized than that of the corresponding FXT-B timing-mode data. Each dataset was processed with FXTDAS v1.30. Source and background events were extracted from a 60 ′′ -radius cir-cle and a 120 ′′ –240 ′′ annulus, respectively. Event times were referred to the Solar System barycenter using the DE440 ephemeris and the above-mentioned coordinates, and source, background spectra and response files were generated. For the phase-averaged analysis, the spec-tra from the two observations were combined. For the phase-resolved analysis, the events were folded using the timing solution described in Section 3 and divided into the same seven equally spaced phase intervals adopted for the IXPE analysis. Spectra were extracted sep-arately for each observation and phase interval and combined across observations within each interval. The resulting spectra were grouped over the 0 .5–10 keV en-ergy range to a minimum of 25 background-subtracted counts per bin.

4. SPECTRAL ANALYSIS Spectral analysis of the IXPE data was performed with the xspec suite (K. A. Arnaud 1996), after gen-erating the ancillary (ARFs) and modulation response (MRFs) files from the most recent version 3 of the IXPE 

response functions (v20260610) using the ixpecalcarf 

tool (C.-T. Chen & IXPE Science Operation Center 2025). Data grouping was performed to ensure a signal-to-noise ratio ≳ 3 in each energy bin. Since the source signal is background-dominated above ∼ 7.5 keV, in the spectro-polarimetric analysis we limited the IXPE 

dataset to the 2 .0–7 .5 keV range. We fit jointly the simultaneous EP (total exposure time of ≈ 10 ks) and IXPE observations, allowing us to extend the energy range down to 0 .5 keV and improve the statistical significance of our dataset. A fit with a single-component, absorbed model for the combined IXPE -EP dataset fails the 99% goodness-of-fit criterion ( χ2/dof = 1423 .86 /696 for a single black-body, BB, and 1147 .54 /696 for a single power-law, PL). We then attempted a fit with two-component mod-els, either two blackbodies ( bbodyrad +bbodyrad ) or a

To better investigate the variation of polar-ization with energy, we repeated the energy-dependent polarimetric fit across the entire 2 .0–7 .5 keV range, con-volving the spectral components with a linearly energy-dependent polarization model ( pollin in xspec ), and alternately leaving the slopes of PD and PA either as free parameters or fixed at 0 (thus reproducing a constant energy dependence). We found that additional degrees of freedom improve the χ2/dof ratio, which decreases from 492 .63 /496 for constant PD and PA, to 489 .46 /494 when both PD and PA linearly vary with energy, and to 490 .00 /495 for linearly dependent PD and constant PA 6.However, an F-test shows no statistically significant im-provement relative to the constant scenario: the null-hypothesis probability drops at ≈ 10% only when PA is the sole constant parameter (nhp = 10 .37%), whereas nhp ranges from ≈ 20% to ≈ 40% in the other cases. Nevertheless, the marginal improvement obtained for linearly dependent PD and constant PA is suggestive of a possible weak increase of the polarization degree with energy.

DISCUSSION Outbursts, events characterized by an enhancement of the persistent flux up to three orders of magnitude with respect to quiescence, have been the primary channel for discovering new magnetars, which are otherwise often too faint for detection with all-sky monitors. This was the case of EP J223759.5+531421, the first magnetar observed in a full-fledged outburst since the launch of 

IXPE in December 2021. Triggered by the initial EP 

detection, a prompt IXPE ToO observation was carried out, allowing for the first X-ray polarimetric study of a magnetar outburst. Although IXPE targeted EP J223759.5+531421 for a (relatively) short exposure time (≈ 200 ks), the observed flux in the 2–8 keV energy range (≈ 5 ×

10 −11 erg cm −2 s−1) was high enough to provide a ro-bust polarization measurement, with the signal remain-ing essentially unaffected by background contamina-tion nearly throughout the entire IXPE band (2 .0– 7.5 keV). Our phase-integrated spectral analysis con-firms the presence of at least two thermal components with different temperatures and radii, as first reported by N. Rea et al. (2026, see Table 1). As shown in Figure 1 (left panel), the contribution of the warm BB compo-nent significantly contributes to the total spectrum up to ≈ 4 keV where the hot BB takes over. We therefore investigated the spectral and polarization properties in the 2 .0–4 .0 keV and 4 .0–7 .5 keV bands separately. The temperatures and emission radii of both thermal components as derived from the phase-resolved spectral analysis remain consistent with those obtained from the phase-averaged spectrum and favor a scenario in which radiation originates from localized regions of the stellar surface, providing a natural geometric explanation for the moderately high pulsed fractions observed ( ≈ 25%, with a peak of ≈ 30% at 2 .0–4 .0 keV). This is further confirmed by the persistence of the double-peak struc-ture in the pulse profile at all energies (see Figure 2), with only minimal changes in the relative amplitude of the peaks. Such a localized emission geometry prevents treating high polarization degrees as unambiguous sig-natures of quantum electrodynamic (QED) effects like vacuum birefringence. The modest energy- and phase-averaged polarization degree ( ≈ 7%) seems to be essentially driven by low-energy photons (2 .0–4 .0 keV). The higher polarization (≈ 13%) measured at 4 .0–7 .5 keV, although still con-sistent with the low-energy value within 1 σ, suggests a possible increase with energy, while maintaining the same polarization direction in the sky (see section 5). This increasing trend is further supported by the phase-resolved analysis, which reveals a higher average polar-ization of ≈ 50% at high energies, compared to ≈ 20% at lower energies. The spectro-polarimetric analysis shows that the warm BB is indeed less polarized than the hot component, confirming that the low- and high-energy polarizations are essentially due to the different contri-butions of the two thermal components. Interestingly, and although the polarization of the warm blackbody is 11 below MDP 99 , the shape of the confidence contours (see Figure 4, bottom) suggests a clearly different polariza-tion direction between the two components (at least at 1σ confidence level). The phase-dependent behavior of the polarization an-gle is well fit by the RVM, while the polarization de-gree shows a clear phase dependence associated with the double-peaked pulse profile (although PD phase-dependent shape changes with energy), as found in other persistent magnetars observed by IXPE (R. Taverna et al. 2022; S. Zane et al. 2023; J. Heyl et al. 2024; R. Taverna et al. 2026). This finds a natural explanation in the presence of vacuum birefringence effects in the star magnetosphere, where the observed polarization degree reflects the polarization properties of radiation at the emission, whereas the polarization direction is frozen at the polarization-limiting radius, which is expected to be much larger than the stellar radius for magnetars (J. S. Heyl et al. 2003; R. Taverna et al. 2015). No 90 ◦ shift in the polarization angle across the IXPE band is visi-ble (see Table 4), suggesting that the same polarization mode (either ordinary, O, or extraordinary, X) domi-nates at all energies 7. For the geometry favored by the RVM model (inclination of the line of sight and of the magnetic dipole axis relative to the star spin axis of 

χ ≈ 25 ◦ and ξ ≈ 110 ◦, respectively) the polarization angle continuously swings across the entire −90 ◦, +90 ◦

range during a cycle. In this case, averaging the Stokes parameters over the rotational phase results in a low polarization degree, even accounting for vacuum bire-fringence (see R. Taverna et al. 2015, Figure 7, in par-ticular). This provides a self-consistent explanation for the low phase-averaged polarization degree at all ener-gies, in contrast to the much higher phase-resolved val-ues, and shows that, under these conditions, the intrinsic polarization degree is better tracked by phase-resolved polarimetry. The high temperature (exceeding 1 keV) and high po-larization ( ≳ 50%, with peaks of ≈ 65%) of the hot thermal component point to the presence of a mag-netized gaseous atmosphere, which, for the magnetic field strength inferred for EP J223759.5+531421 (≈

4 × 10 14 G at the poles), is expected to emit radia-tion predominantly polarized in the X-mode (R. Tav-erna et al. 2020; R. M. E. Kelly et al. 2024). Given the

Nevertheless, because the warm blackbody polar-ization direction derived from the spectro-polarimetric fitting appears misaligned relative to the hot component, and given that the hot blackbody already provides the larger contribution at the lower end of the IXPE band (≈ 2 keV), an O-mode contribution from the condensed surface cannot be ruled out, at least at specific rota-tional phases (where the viewing geometry may be more favorable). In order to qualitatively test this scenario without at-tempting a detailed fit to the data, we produced numer-ical simulations using the ray-tracing code described in R. Taverna et al. (2015). The emission is assumed to originate from two antipodal circular caps with semi-apertures ≈ 9◦, and from an elongated spot centered at magnetic colatitude θh = 27 ◦ with angular semi-apertures of ≈ 2◦ and ≈ 1◦. These values are broadly consistent with the emission radii inferred from the phase-resolved spectral analysis. The star mass and ra-dius are taken to be 1 .4 M⊙ and 13 km, respectively, with the surface sampled with a Θ ×Φ = 150 ×150 angu-lar mesh, and we assume a purely dipolar magnetic field (corrected for general relativistic effects, see D. Page & A. Sarmiento 1996), with a polar strength of 4 × 10 14 G. Radiation is assumed to follow pure blackbody distribu-tions, with a temperature of 0 .49 keV and 10% polar-ization in the X-mode for the antipodal regions and a temperature of 1 .1 keV, 80% polarized in the X-mode for the spot. The rest of the stellar surface emits unpolar-ized radiation at a lower temperature (0 .12 keV). Gen-eral relativistic effects (ray bending and gravitational redshift) are fully accounted for, and all temperatures and energies are those measured at infinity. Finally, the viewing geometry is set to the values derived from the RVM fit, i.e. χ = 28 ◦.21 and ξ = 108 ◦.90. The results of the numerical simulation, integrated throughout the 2 .0– 7 .5 keV energy band, are reported in Figure 9, where the IXPE data have been super-imposed for visual comparison. Even without aiming to reproduce quantitatively the observed behavior, the adopted emission geometry proves capable to reproduce the double-peaked profile of the light curve, together with a similarly double peaked polarization degree (in phase with the flux). Indeed, the high polarization com-ing from the isolated hot spot is primarily responsible for the phase-dependent behavior of the polarization de-gree, while the weakly polarized radiation coming from the larger antipodal regions reduces the overall polariza-tion from the intrinsic 80% to the observed 30–40%. In particular, a closer look at the middle panel of Figure 9 shows that the difference between the models obtained with (solid lines) and without (dashed lines) accounting for vacuum birefringence effects is marginal, the more so when compared with the 1 σ error bars of the data 8.This confirms that, as mentioned above, vacuum bire-fringence effects cannot be unambiguously probed sim-ply by assessing the polarization degree when radiation originates from such restricted regions. We note that this simulation was obtained assuming the warm and hot thermal components to be polarized in the same mode, and that the phase dependence of the polariza-tion degree does not yet track the observed dataset in full detail. Achieving higher precision is left to future work, with the implementation of self-consistent atmo-spheric and condensed surface models for the isolated and antipodal regions, thus incorporating a non-trivial angular dependence for both photon distributions and polarization, which is missing at present. The outburst of EP J223759.5+531421 highlighted once again the potential of X-ray polarimetry to provide crucial information of magnetar sources, complementary to those obtained from spectral and timing analyses alone. Unfortunately, in the case at hand the limited exposure time prevented us from tracking the temporal evolution of the polarization properties as the outburst decayed. Such monitoring would be particularly valu-able for investigating whether these events are accom-panied by changing magnetic topology (see e.g. H. Tong et al. 2021; M. Ge et al. 2025). In this respect, the de-velopment of next-generation X-ray polarimeters will be instrumental in enabling such investigations. Missions such as eXTP (S.-N. Zhang et al. 2025, with launch ex-pected in 2030), with its substantial improvement in soft X-ray sensitivity, and EXPO (P. Soffitta et al. 2026, pro-posed for ESA’s M8 mission), which offer the possibility of measuring polarization at higher energies, will make
