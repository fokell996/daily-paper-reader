## Bayesian Inference of fine-features of dense matter EOS from future high-precision data of neutron star radii

Abstract. Future high-precision X-ray and gravitational wave observatories are expected to measure the radii of neutron stars (NSs) with an accuracy better than about 0.1 km. However, it remains unclear what particular aspects of the Equation of State (EOS) and to what precision they will be better constrained. Within a Bayesian framework using a meta-model EOS and mock high-precision NS data, the posterior probability distribution functions (PDFs) of NS matter EOS parameters for both hadronic and quark phases and the transition between them were recently studied. We report here a few highlights of these studies. 

1 Introduction 

The current accuracy of measuring NS radii is about 1 km. For instance, LIGO /VIRGO inferred a radius of 

R1.4 = 11 .9 ± 0.875 km at 68% confidence level for two canonical NSs involved in GW170817 [1]. More recent NICER observations for NS radii generally have similar or larger errors, see, e.g., Refs. [2, 3]. These data have been used by the nuclear astrophysics community in extracting information about the nature and EOS of supradense NS matter in various ways, with many interesting results. In particular, they have helped us in better constraining the density dependence of nuclear symmetry energy Esym (ρ)at densities around (1 − 2) ρ0 where ρ0 is the saturation density of symmetric nuclear matter, see, e.g., Ref. [4] for a comprehensive review. As an example, shown in Fig. 1 is a comparison of the constraints on Esym (ρ) from NS observations (thick blue curves) with predictions of both phenomenological (left) and microscopic (right) nuclear many-body theories. Interestingly, while NS observations can already rule out many theoretical predictions around 

ρ0, there is a large open window of observational con-straints at ρ ≥ 2ρ0. More precise NS radius measure-ments may help close this open window. Moreover, to distinguish many possible EOSs and identify twin stars or strange stars, it is necessary to carry out the di ff eren-tial mass /radius measurement d M/dR in the mass region (1 .2 − 2.0) M ⊙ [5–11]. For this purpose, NS radii have to be measured much better than the current 1.0 km accuracy. It is very encouraging to note that high-precision NS radius measurements will be possible by using the next-generation X-ray pulse profile observatories and gravi-tational wave detectors. 2 Meta-model EOS for Bayesian analyses of NS observables 

First, we use the minimum model of NS matter consist-ing of neutrons, protons, electrons and muons in β equi-librium to construct the hadronic part of the NS EOS. In this model, the most basic input to construct the NS matter EOS P(ϵ), i.e., pressure P versus energy density ϵ, is the energy per nucleon E(ρ, δ ) in asymmetric nucleonic mat-ter of isospin asymmetry δ and density ρ. It can be written as E(ρ, δ ) = E0(ρ) + Esym (ρ)δ2 + O(δ4) in terms of the symmetric nuclear matter (SNM) EOS E0(ρ) and nuclear symmetry energy Esym (ρ). 3 Bayesian inferences of EOS parameters from high-precision NS radius data 

Shown in Fig. 4 is a comparison of the posterior PDFs of J0, Jsym , Ksym , and L from Bayesian analyses using the minimum model of NSs and the fiducial radius data of R1.4 = 11 .9 km for a canonical NS with an imagined precision of ∆R = 1.0, 0.5, 0.2, and 0 .1 km, respectively. Several interesting observations are worth emphasizing. First, while the most probable value of the skewness 

J0 measuring the sti ff ness of high-density SNM remains about the same, its precision gets appreciably improved as the precision ∆R changes from 1.0 km to 0.1 km, indicat-ing the potential of better constraining the EOS of high-density SNM. Second, the PDFs of L and Ksym have significant changes. In particular, the most probable L shifts to smaller values and the PDF( Ksym ) starts to show two peaks as the precision improves. It is known that these two pa-rameters are most important for determining the radii of canonical NSs [36]. The dual peaks in the PDF( Ksym ) is due to the correlations between L − Ksym and Ksym − Jsym 

[5]. It is not surprising that as the precision of probes im-proves, fine structures of the object get revealed, as seen frequently in many sub-fields of science. On the other hand, the PDF( Jsym ) peaks at the upper boundary of its uniform prior. These findings are qualitatively expected [14, 29]. It is known that the radii of canonical neutron stars are determined by the pressure around 2 ρ0 to which the symmetry energy makes a major contribution. Thus, using the same mean radius R1.4 = 11 .9 km with better precision from 1.0 to 0.1 km will lead mainly to a more precise inference of nuclear symmetry energy around 2 ρ0

characterized by L and Ksym . It will not improve much about the EOS at significantly higher densities character-ized by J0 and Jsym .Third, the response of the PDFs of symmetry energy parameters to the variation of the precision ∆R is rather asymmetric. This is rather di ff erent from the response of SNM skewness J0. It is seen that the PDF( J0) mostly narrows its width symmetrically around approximately the same most probable value. It is known that the J0 is mainly determined by the maximum mass and causality, while the radii are mainly controlled by the symmetry energy. Thus, the PDF( J0) is not a ff ected much by the variation of ∆R.On the other hand, a small change in radius can cause a significant change in the density profile or average density of NSs. Since the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volko ff equa-tions governing NS structures are highly nonlinear, the mapping between the mass-radius sequence and the EOS is rather nonlinear. We now turn to the e ff ects of precision σ = ∆ R on inferring the EOS of QM and properties of hadron-quark first-order phase transition within the CSS model. Shown in Fig. 5 are the posterior PDFs of the three quark mat-ter EOS parameters inferred from R2.0 = 11 .9 km with a precision of σ = 1.0 and 0 .1 km, respectively. In this example, the prior range for the hadron-quark transition density is set 3 .0 ≤ ρt/ρ 0 ≤ 6.0. The reason we now use the mock radius data for the radii of NSs with 2.0M ⊙ in-stead of canonical NSs with 1.4M ⊙ is because the radii of more massive NSs are less a ff ected by the still uncertain crust EOSs, thus more reliable for probing the properties of QM and hadron-quark phase transition. It is interesting to see that the PDF and the most prob-able value of ρt are very sensitive to the precision of NS radius measurement. This is understandable because the average density ρa of an NS scales with M/R3; a small variation of its radius can lead to a big change in its ρa

and the entire density profile. Moreover, as we mentioned earlier, the correspondence between the NS mass-radius relation and the underlying EOS, especially in the high-density region, is highly nonlinear because of the highly nonlinear nature of the TOV equations. It is also very interesting to see that the precision σ has essentially no e ff ect on the posterior PDFs of QM proper-ties quantified by ∆ϵ and C2qm . Moreover, the PDF( C2qm )is rather flat in its whole range. It indicates that the NS radius data, regardless of its precision, does not constrain the quark matter sti ff ness. This is physical, although it may sound very disappointing to some people. In fact, it has been well known that the radii of canonical NSs are de-termined by the pressure at densities around 2 ρ0 [37]. For massive NSs, the relevant density is expected to be higher [38]. But with the most probable hadron-quark transition density ρt as high as 3 .5ρ0 with σ = 1.0 km and 4 .7ρ0 with 

σ = 0.1 km, the QM sti ff ness is not expected to a ff ect the radii even for massive NSs.
