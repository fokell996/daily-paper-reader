## 摘要
探索来自中子星(NS)的轴子类似粒子(ALP)在\emph{Fermi}-LAT能量范围内的特征在很大程度上仍未得到研究。具有异常强磁场的中子星，例如磁星和具有磁星级磁场的脉冲星，为ALP-光子转换提供了特别有前景的环境。磁星的特征是表面磁场高达$B_0\sim(10^{14}$--$10^{15})\,\mathrm{G}$；然而，尽管其磁场极端，但在\emph{Fermi}-LAT能量范围内尚未牢固探测到稳定的磁星发射，高能活动通常与罕见的耀发事件相关。在这项工作中，我们研究了不同类别中子星内部ALP的产生以及随后在其磁层中ALP转换为光子的过程。ALP发射率由恒星密度和温度$T$决定，而转换概率则因恒星周围的强磁场而增强。我们进一步考虑了光子穿过银河系磁场的传播，这可能对可观测光子通量提供额外贡献。我们研究了由此产生的伽马射线特征，并评估了来自中子星磁层的ALP诱导发射是否在\emph{Fermi}-LAT波段能量$E\gtrsim100\,\mathrm{MeV}$下可被探测。此外，我们考虑了通过逆康普顿散射对磁层光子进行再处理是否可以将部分发射移至更高能量并提供额外的观测特征。我们使用由此产生的通量从现有伽马射线观测中推导约束，并以COSI为代表示例来评估未来MeV-GeV观测的灵敏度。

## Abstract
Exploring axion-like particle (ALP) signatures from neutron stars (NSs) in the \emph{Fermi}-LAT energy range remains largely unexplored. Neutron stars with exceptionally strong magnetic fields, such as magnetars and pulsars with magnetar-like magnetic fields, provide particularly promising environments for ALP--photon conversion. Magnetars are characterized by surface magnetic fields as large as $B_0\sim(10^{14}$--$10^{15})\,\mathrm{G}$; however, despite their extreme magnetic fields, no steady magnetar emission has been firmly detected in the \emph{Fermi}-LAT energy range, with high-energy activity generally associated with rare flaring episodes.   In this work, we investigate ALP production in the interiors of different classes of NSs and the subsequent conversion of ALPs into photons in their magnetospheres. The ALP emissivity is determined by the stellar density and temperature $T$, while the conversion probability is enhanced by the strong magnetic fields surrounding the star. We further account for photon propagation through the Galactic magnetic field, which can provide an additional contribution to the observable photon flux. We investigate the resulting gamma-ray signatures and assess whether ALP-induced emission from NS magnetospheres could be detectable at energies $E\gtrsim100,\mathrm{MeV}$ in the \emph{Fermi}-LAT band. In addition, we consider if the reprocessing of the magnetospheric photons through inverse Compton scattering can shift part of the emission to higher energies and provide an additional observational signature. We use the resulting fluxes to derive constraints from existing gamma-ray observations and to estimate the sensitivity of future MeV--GeV observations, taking COSI as a representative example.
