## 摘要
GW190425的高总质量对我们理解双中子星（DNS）的形成构成了挑战，因为在银河系盘面中尚未观测到如此重（$\ge 3$\,M$_\odot$）的DNS系统。人们提出了许多形成场景来解释其形成。我们在一个经过校准到银河系DNS种群的自洽双星演化框架内对这些场景进行了测试。在本系列论文I中，我们研究了太阳金属丰度（$Z = Z_\odot$）下，一颗$1.4$\,M$_\odot$中子星（NS）与一颗$2.5$--$10$\,M$_\odot$氦星组成的双星系统的演化，并假设NS上的吸积受爱丁顿极限限制，使用了\texttt{MESA}。在本文中，我们考虑了NS-氦星双星在NS质量范围为$1.1$--$1.9$\,M$_\odot$，且$Z=Z_\odot$、0.1\,$Z_\odot$和0.01\,$Z_\odot$条件下的演化。我们发现重DNS的形成是罕见的，仅占DNS的$\sim 0.5$\%。其中大多数通过“标准形成”通道形成，该通道被广泛认为形成了GW170817和观测到的银河系DNS。在太阳金属丰度下，我们没有发现通过“快速并合”通道的贡献，但在低金属丰度下，系统主要通过不稳定质量传输形成。此外，我们发现在我们所考虑的金属丰度范围内，重DNS形成比例对金属丰度没有显著依赖性。我们得出结论，重DNS并不构成一个独立的子群，而仅仅是标准DNS种群的高质量尾部。

## Abstract
The high total mass of GW190425 challenges our understanding of double neutron star (DNS) formation, as no such heavy ($\ge 3$\, M$_\odot$) DNS system has been observed in the Milky Way disk. Numerous formation scenarios have been proposed to explain its formation. We test these within a self-consistent binary evolution framework calibrated to the Galactic DNS population. In Paper~I of this series, we studied the evolution of a $1.4$\,M$_\odot$ neutron star (NS) in a binary with a $2.5$--$10$\,M$_\odot$ helium star at solar metallicity ($Z = Z_\odot$), assuming Eddington-limited accretion onto the NS using \texttt{MESA}. In this paper, we consider the evolution of NS-He star binaries with a broad range of NS masses from $1.1$--$1.9$\,M$_\odot$ at $Z=Z_\odot$, 0.1\,$Z_\odot$ and 0.01\,$Z_\odot$. We find that the formation of heavy DNSs is rare, accounting for only $\sim 0.5$ per cent of DNSs. The majority of these are formed through the `standard formation' channel, widely believed to form GW170817 and observed Galactic DNSs. We find no contribution through the `fast-merger' channel at solar metallicity, but systems at low metallicity predominantly form through unstable mass transfer. Furthermore, we find no significant dependence of the heavy DNS formation fraction on metallicity over the range considered here. We conclude that heavy DNSs do not form a separate subpopulation and merely represent the high-mass tail of the standard DNS population.
