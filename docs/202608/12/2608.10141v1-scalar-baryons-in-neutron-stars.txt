## Scalar baryons in neutron stars 

Julian Heeck 1, ∗ and Yu Zhi 1, †

> 1

Department of Physics, University of Virginia, Charlottesville, Virginia 22904, USA 

Neutron stars have baryon chemical potentials that can exceed the neutron mass, providing favor-able conditions to convert neutrons to new particles carrying baryon number, even if those processes are kinematically forbidden in vacuum. We study the effect of GeV-scale scalars with baryon number on neutron stars’ equation of state and show that non-perturbatively large repulsive self-couplings are required to support the observed two-solar-mass neutron stars. We also study potentials with attractive self-interactions, which can trigger scalar production even for masses above the chemical potential and resemble Coleman’s Q-matter. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

Particles beyond the Standard Model that carry baryon number can have interesting phenomenology, no-tably as (asymmetric) dark matter candidates or in con-nection to the matter–antimatter asymmetry of our uni-verse. Simple models can be found in Refs. [1–3], the most popular being a new neutral fermion χ with baryon number B(χ) = 1 and effective interactions such as the dimension-six portal ¯χudd/ Λ2, which leads to a mass mixing of χ with the neutron n ∼ udd at low energies. For mχ < m n, neutrons can then decay into this lighter neutral baryon, e.g. via n → χγ , which is subject to strong constraints [4, 5] that become even stronger for lighter χ once the channels n → χπ 0 and p → χπ + open up [3, 6]. Aside from the phase-space suppressed region 

mχ ≃ mn [5, 7–9] – where bound neutrons are stable – neutron decay rates into χ are suppressed to at least 10 −30 yr −1 via invisible-neutron searches [10, 11], to be further improved by JUNO [12]. For mχ ≥ mn, nucleons are stable and the model is much less constrained, but as shown in Refs. [13, 14], 

χ could still be produced in neutron stars as long as 

mχ is smaller than the chemical potential μB of neu-trons/baryons, which can be of order 1 .5 GeV depend-ing on the Equation of State (EoS). The conversion 

n → χ into a non-interacting χ then relieves Fermi pressure, drastically softening the neutron star’s EoS and typically lowering the maximal mass a neutron star can have. Our observations of old neutron stars with masses around 2 M⊙, e.g. PSR J1614-2230 [15] and PSR J0348+0432 [16], then put constraints on this new-physics model. Similar analyses have been performed in Refs. [17–21], usually in the context of the neutron-lifetime anomaly [22, 23], which requires a sizable free-neutron conversion branching ratio BR( n → χ) ≃ 1%. In this letter, we will apply a similar analysis to 

scalar baryons, i.e. complex scalar fields ϕ with B(ϕ) = 1 [1, 3, 8, 24]. The simplest low-energy couplings are of the form nνϕ ∗ and peϕ ∗, which induce n → ϕ¯ν or

p → ϕe + if the new scalar is light, again constrained to rates below 10 −30 yr −1 [3]. For mϕ ≥ mn, the conversion rates and underlying couplings could be large enough to affect neutron stars, as long as mϕ < μ B . Once again, the neutron’s Fermi pressure can be relieved by trans-forming a neutron into the new particle, but now the new particle will not fill up its own Fermi sphere but just condense in the core since it follows Bose statistics. The ϕ would not generate any pressure in this case and soften the neutron star’s EoS even more dramatically than the fermion χ! However, scalars generically have repulsive self-interaction terms to stabilize the scalar po-tential, e.g. λ|ϕ|4, so ϕ will not end up in a point-like core after all but instead form a sphere due to the self-interaction pressure, rendering the EoS stiffer. Just like in the fermion case, the observation of 2 M⊙ neutron stars then puts constraints on the mϕ–λ parameter space. Our setup differs from the often discussed scenario of dark-matter-admixed neutron stars, which are usually treated in a two-fluid approximation [25, 26]. In our case, new particles are not captured by neutron stars but pro-duced inside, and hence insensitive to the neutron star’s environment. Since we assume chemical equilibrium of all baryons, the equations are also much simpler. Lastly, the particles under discussion are not dark matter, as they can decay into nucleons in the region of interest.

V. CONCLUSIONS 

Increasingly precise observations of neutron stars allow us to use them as probes of physics beyond the Standard Model. Light new particles in particular better not mod-ify the equation of state too much, lest it fail to allow for the 2 M⊙ neutron stars we have detected. This ba-sic constraint has been used in the past on GeV-scale dark fermions that carry baryon number and can be produced via neutron transitions if energetically favor-able. In this letter, we have adapted this discussion to dark bosons , i.e. scalar baryons. The absence of Fermi pressure makes these light particles even more danger-ous to neutron stars, only ameliorated if the repulsive self-interactions have non-perturbatively large couplings. We provided quantitative constraints on repulsive self-interactions for scalar masses up to the maximal chemical potential inside the neutron star, up to 1 .5 GeV for typ-ical equations of state. In addition, we also considered the case of attractive interactions, with potentials and models familiar from Q-ball solitons; here, the new bind-ing energy allows for production of even heavier scalars, which form a Q-matter condensate. While we focused on a scalar with baryon number B = 1, an analogous dis-cussion holds for other cases, e.g. a scalar ξ with B = 2 and coupling to two neutrons, ξ∗nn [1, 55], which can be produced in neutron stars if mξ < 2μmax
