## All You Need is not Ωgw : Beyond the Mean of the Cross-Correlation Estimator when Searching for an Astrophysical Gravitational-Wave Background

(Dated: August 12, 2026) Searches for the stochastic gravitational-wave background (SGWB) using ground-based detectors rely heavily on the cross-correlation estimator ¯Cf , whose statistical properties determine the theo-retical foundation for inference frameworks built on it. Past analyses usually assume Gaussianity of ¯Cf , but this assumption has not been systematically tested for a background of astrophysical origin like the one produced by compact binary coalescences. In this work, we decompose ¯Cf into three physically motivated components: (1) the mean intensity, (2) the geometrical shot noise, and (3) the polarization leakage, and we compute 90% credible intervals for its cumulants up to fourth order for the binary black hole, binary neutron star, and neutron star-black hole populations by propagating local merger rate uncertainties. We find that for the upcoming LIGO O5 sensitivity, ¯Cf remains effectively Gaussian for all three populations, validating current Gaussian likelihood frameworks. For Cosmic Explorer (CE), however, the ¯Cf produced by binary black holes becomes non-Gaussian, with skewness and excess kurtosis of 0 .31 +0 .04

These results indicate that the Edgeworth expansion pro-vides an adequate leading-order description of the non-Gaussianity expected in the next-generation era, while also motivating the development of more sophisticated statistical methods for a more accurate treatment. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

The direct detection of gravitational waves (GWs) by the LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA (LVK) network has opened a new observational window onto the Universe [1–6]. Beyond resolved compact binary coalescences (CBCs), an as-yet undetected stochastic gravitational wave back-ground (SGWB) — the incoherent superposition of unre-solved astrophysical and cosmological sources — encodes integrated information about the GW emission history of the Universe [7–9]. The astrophysical SGWB sourced by unresolved CBCs is expected to dominate in the LVK band and is projected to be detected within the next decade [10, 11]. The standard approach to searching for the SGWB using ground-based detectors is the cross-correlation method [9, 12], in which the time-domain data from a detector pair is divided into segments and combined into the frequency-domain estimator ¯Cf . Most of the exist-ing analyses, including the past LVK searches [10, 11], assume that ¯Cf follows a Gaussian distribution; the like-lihood then factorizes as a Gaussian in each frequency bin, and inference on the SGWB amplitude reduces to standard matched-filter statistics [13, 14]. This Gaus-sian assumption is well motivated for a truly stochastic background composed of a large number of incoherently

The signal is composed of a finite number of discrete, transient events with in-trinsic chirp-like time–frequency structure, definite po-larization, and source-specific sky locations and intrin-sic parameters. Whether the resulting ¯Cf is Gaussian depends on how many such events contribute per anal-ysis segment and how detector responses average over them. Several previous works have addressed aspects of this question: the popcorn / non-stationary regime has been studied analytically in Refs. [15–22], weak depar-tures from Gaussianity have been incorporated via the Edgeworth expansion in Refs. [23, 24], and the deviation of the detector response to individual events from the sky-averaged overlap reduction function (ORF) has been studied in Ref. [25]. Nevertheless, a systematic, quan-titative characterization of the higher-order statistics of ¯Cf — across the full frequency band, the relevant CBC populations, and different detector sensitivities — has so far been missing. In this paper, we provide such a characterization. We decompose ¯Cf into three physically distinct contribu-tions: (1) the mean intensity, (2) the geometrical shot noise, and (3) the polarization leakage. We compute the cumulants κn( ¯Cf ) up to fourth order for the binary black hole (BBH), binary neutron star (BNS), and neu-tron star-black hole (NSBH) populations. We find that ¯Cf remains effectively Gaussian for all three populations for the LIGO sensitivity in the upcoming fifth observa-tion run (O5), justifying the continued use of Gaussian-arXiv:2608.11119v1 [gr-qc] 11 Aug 2026 2likelihood frameworks for the foreseeable future. For next-generation (XG) GW detectors, Einstein Telescope (ET) [26–28] and Cosmic Explorer (CE) [29–31], the sit-uation changes qualitatively. For a network comprising two CE instruments, the ¯Cf from the BBH background develops appreciable non-Gaussianity, with the skewness and excess kurtosis of ¯Cf reaching O(0 .3) and O(1 .3), re-spectively, in the most sensitive band of the detector net-work. While the Edgeworth expansion — the standard tool for incorporating mild non-Gaussianity — remains adequate as a leading-order description in this regime, its limited range of validity motivates a more accurate treat-ment (or, alternatively, a notching strategy that removes resolved BBH signals from the data stream [32–34]). The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II reviews the cross-correlation estimator and de-rives the cumulant expansion of ¯Cf for a CBC-sourced SGWB. Section III presents the numerical results for the BBH, BNS, and NSBH backgrounds across the LIGO O5 and CE detector configurations. Section IV discusses the implications of our findings, the breakdown of the di-agonal approximation for BNSs at low frequencies, and frequency correlations as an additional caveat for future work.  

> II. CROSS-CORRELATION

This section outlines the cross-correlation search for a SGWB. The first part assumes the target SGWB is of cosmological origin, with the standard theoretical proper-ties expected in the literature, i.e., persistent, Gaussian, stationary, isotropic, and unpolarized. In the second half, we consider a SGWB of astrophysical origin, such as the one generated by CBC mergers.

To quantify this, the following section presents numerical results for the cumulants κn( ¯Cf ) up to n = 4, explicitly detailing the contribution of each term in Eq. (19). Before discussing our simulations, we clarify an im-portant conceptual distinction. In searches for a CGWB using the cross-correlation method, one commonly as-sumes the weak-signal regime , which requires the SGWB signal to be a genuinely stochastic process–one that can-not be characterized by a deterministic waveform h(t; f ). The scenario considered here is fundamentally different. CBC signals interact with our detectors in a determin-istic fashion; the only source of randomness is our in-ability to predict the exact number of sources and their precise source parameters within a given observation pe-riod. Consequently, comparing the PSD of CBC events directly against the detector noise PSD does not straight-forwardly indicate whether we are in the weak-signal regime, as that regime is defined in the context of truly stochastic signals, such as a SGWB of cosmological ori-gin. 6

> B. Region in which the low-overlap probability approximation holds

From the chosen CBC populations we can evaluate the expected number of events per time-frequency bin Rτ as a function of frequency. At high frequencies, this quan-tity reduces to what is commonly referred to as the duty cycle in the literature [16–19]. At low frequencies, how-ever, a single chirp can extend across multiple segments, so Rτ is no longer determined by the merger rate alone but also depends on how rapidly the chirp frequency evolves with time. We start by evaluating the distribution of the number of time segments occupied by an event from a given fre-quency to merger and within a single frequency bin. The latter is obtained by computing the difference in time to merger at the edges of adjacent frequency bins, and con-verting this to a number of time segments with the chosen 

τ . We recall that in our analysis τ = 4 s = 1 /δf . The results are shown in Fig. 1: here, for each of the three CBC populations, we report the median and 68%, 95%, and 99% highest density intervals (HDIs) for these distri-butions, obtained by sampling 5 × 10 5 events from each of them. The values are obtained using the expression for the time to coalescence as a function of frequency from Ref. [64], accurate up to 3.5 post-Newtonian (PN) order for non-spinning binaries. For BBHs, the vast majority of the events only occupy one time segment until merger already at ∼ 10 Hz, while BNS and NSBH systems can occupy several time segments until ∼ 40 Hz. The lower the frequency, the higher the number of segments oc-cupied, which can increase to more than 10 3 for BNSs at 3 Hz. Below ∼ 10 Hz the stationary-phase duration 7of an event at frequency f exceeds a single segment for BNSs and NSBHs, so single events contribute coherently to multiple adjacent segments. > IV. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION

In this paper, we have characterized the statistical properties of the segment-averaged cross-correlation es-timator ¯Cf for an astrophysical SGWB sourced by CBC populations. By decomposing ¯Cf into three physically distinct components — the mean intensity, geometrical shot noise, and polarization leakage — and computing the cumulants κn( ¯Cf ) up to fourth order, we have quan-tified for the first time the non-Gaussianity of ¯Cf across a wide range of frequencies and detector sensitivities. The principal finding is that the non-Gaussianity of ¯Cf depends strongly on both the source population and the detector configuration. For all three CBC popula-tions, ¯Cf remains effectively Gaussian under the upcom-ing LIGO O5 sensitivity, so existing Bayesian frameworks based on Gaussian likelihoods can be applied without modification. For a two-detector CE network, however, the situation changes qualitatively: ¯Cf from the BBH background develops non-negligible non-Gaussianity at 

f = 20 Hz ( |γ1| = 0 .31 +0 .04 

> −0.03

, γ 2 = 1 .3+0 .4

> −0.3

), while ¯Cf from the BNS and NSBH populations remain effectively Gaus-sian. See Sec. III for caveats to this calculation for the BNS background. A separate but equally important caveat concerns fre-quency correlations. Current analyses typically assume ¯Cf to be statistically independent across frequency bins, so that the likelihood factorizes into a product of per-bin contributions. This assumption holds for a CGWB, but for a SGWB generated by CBC events the power emit-ted in different frequency bins is strongly positively corre-lated , owing to the chirp-like evolution of CBC signals in the time-frequency domain. Neglecting these correlations is unlikely to bias the posterior means of the parameters of interest, but it leads to overly wide posteriors, since the analysis no longer makes optimal use of the available information. We defer to future work a detailed investigation of the impact of ˆC(I); off −diag

on the statistical properties of ¯Cf , the development of more refined statistical meth-ods for handling strong non-Gaussianity, and the inclu-sion of correlations between ¯Cf and ¯Cf ′ in the Bayesian inference likelihood.
