## 摘要
千新星为双中子星并合提供了关键的电磁窗口，揭示了并合系统的性质，同时探测宇宙的r过程核合成。然而，迄今为止仅探测到少数千新星候选体，其中AT2017gfo是与引力波事件GW170817相关联的唯一一例。在本工作中，我们提出了\textsc{Genova}，一个概率框架，可直接从双中子星并合的引力波后验样本预测千新星光谱和光变曲线。该方法使用条件归一化流来学习静止系光谱的分布，条件为源坐标系分量质量、潮汐形变率、观测视角和并合后时间。其他千新星模型参数，如抛射物不透明度，在训练过程中被边缘化，因此其效应作为预测不确定性传播到预测光谱中。在自洽性测试中，流模型再现了中位光变曲线，残差通常低于$\sim 0.1$星等，预测的中心68%区间宽度比在$\sim 0.4$--$8.0$天内主要保持在$0.8$到$1.4$之间。与物理上不同的千新星模型的比较表明，概率预测在训练所用模型之外仍能提供信息。我们将\textsc{Genova}应用于GW170817/AT2017gfo，使用多波段观测数据，包括来自可见光和红外巡天望远镜的天文学数据的重新缩减的$Y$、$J$、$K_s$波段测光，这些数据在本工作中给出。所得的预测区间大致涵盖了观测结果，同时捕获了引力波后验不确定性和边缘化千新星模型参数引起的差异。

## Abstract
Kilonovae provide a key electromagnetic window into binary neutron star mergers, revealing the properties of the merging system while probing r-process nucleosynthesis of the Universe. However, only a few kilonova candidates have been detected to date, with AT2017gfo being the only one associated with a gravitational wave event, GW170817. In this work, we present \textsc{Genova}, a probabilistic framework for predicting kilonova spectra and light curves directly from gravitational wave posterior samples of binary neutron star mergers. This method uses a conditional normalising flow to learn the distribution of rest-frame spectra conditioned on the source-frame component masses, tidal deformabilities, viewing angle and time since merger. Other kilonova model parameters, such as ejecta opacities, are marginalised over during training, so that their effects are propagated into the predicted spectra as predictive uncertainty. In the self-consistency test, the flow model reproduces the median light curves with residuals typically below $\sim 0.1$ mag, and the ratio of the predicted central 68\% interval widths remains predominantly between $0.8$ and $1.4$ over $\sim 0.4$--$8.0$ days. Comparisons with a physically distinct kilonova model show that the probabilistic prediction can remain informative beyond the model used for training. We apply \textsc{Genova} to GW170817/AT2017gfo using multi-band observations, including newly re-reduced $Y$-, $J$-, $K_s$-band photometry from the Visible and Infrared Survey Telescope for Astronomy, which we present in this work. The resulting predictive intervals broadly encompass the observations while capturing both gravitational wave posterior uncertainty and the variation induced by marginalised kilonova model parameters.
