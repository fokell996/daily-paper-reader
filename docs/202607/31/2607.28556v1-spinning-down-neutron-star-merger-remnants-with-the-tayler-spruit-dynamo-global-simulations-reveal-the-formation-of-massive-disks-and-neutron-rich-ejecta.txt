Spinning down neutron-star merger remnants with the Tayler–Spruit dynamo: Global simulations reveal the formation of massive disks and neutron-rich ejecta 

Harry Ho-Yin Ng 1 and Elias R. Most 1, 2 

> 1TAPIR, Mailcode 350-17, California Institute of Technology, Pasadena, CA 91125, USA
> 2Walter Burke Institute for Theoretical Physics, California Institute of Technology, Pasadena, CA 91125, USA

ABSTRACT Magnetic-field amplification and angular momentum (AM) transport critically shape the secular evolution, lifetime, and electromagnetic signatures of binary neutron-star merger remnants. While the magnetorotational instability can operate in the outer negative-shear regions of the neutron-star remnant and accretion disk, the positive-shear, stably stratified core may instead be susceptible to the Tayler–Spruit dynamo. We present the first global, long-term general-relativistic neutrino-radiation magnetohydrodynamics simulations of a neutron-star merger remnant incorporating the unresolved Tayler–Spruit dynamo through a new mean-field dynamo subgrid prescription. Our axisymmetric simulations starting from a realistic merger remnant show that the Tayler–Spruit dynamo is primarily active in high-latitude regions of the remnant core. The resulting Maxwell stresses redistribute AM on a spin-down timescale of a few hundred milliseconds, substantially flattening the core rotation profile and transferring mass and AM from the outer remnant into the disk. This produces a more massive, extended, and strongly magnetized disk with a low electron fraction, leading to substantially more neutron-rich ejecta. Our results demonstrate that currently unmodelled Tayler–Spruit dynamo action can qualitatively alter the rotational evolution, collapse prospects, disk formation, and multi-messenger signatures of long-lived neutron-star merger remnants.

1. INTRODUCTION Binary neutron-star (BNS) mergers are key multi-messenger sources (J. M. Lattimer & D. N. Schramm 1974; D. Eichler et al. 1989; B. D. Metzger et al. 2010), producing gravitational waves (GWs) (B. P. Abbott et al. 2017), kilonovae powered by the radioactive decay of ejecta undergoing r-process nucleosynthesis (M. M. Kasliwal et al. 2017; C. D. Kilpatrick et al. 2017; M. R. Drout et al. 2017; D. Kasen et al. 2017; V. A. Villar et al. 2017), X-ray and radio emissions (R. Margutti et al. 2017; K. P. Mooley et al. 2018a,b) from a struc-tured jet and cocoon (O. Gottlieb et al. 2018; Y. Wu & A. MacFadyen 2019), and short gamma-ray bursts (sGRBs) (Fermi-LAT Collaboration 2017; LIGO Scien-tific Collaboration et al. 2017).

The lifetime of the remnant determines the onset of sGRBs, the secular ejecta mass loss, and the kilo-nova properties (K. Hotokezaka et al. 2013; T. Diet-rich et al. 2015; D. Radice et al. 2016; L. Bovard et al. 2017; L. Lehner et al. 2016; D. Radice et al. 2018; S. Bernuzzi et al. 2020; V. Nedora et al. 2019, 2020; F. Zappa et al. 2023). While various parameters and ini-tial conditions control the remnant lifetime (R. Gill et al. 2019; A. Murguia-Berthier et al. 2021; P. Beniamini & W. Lu 2021), among the many factors, magnetic fields play a crucial role (B. Margalit et al. 2022). General-relativistic magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) simula-tions have shown that the Kelvin–Helmholtz instabil-ity (KHI) (D. J. Price & S. Rosswog 2006; K. Kiuchi et al. 2015), magnetic winding (S. L. Shapiro 2000), the magnetorotational instability (MRI) (S. A. Balbus & J. F. Hawley 1991), and large-scale dynamo processes can amplify the magnetic field, modify the field topol-ogy, and affect the remnant dynamics (F. A. Rasio & arXiv:2607.28556v1 [astro-ph.HE] 30 Jul 2026 2S. L. Shapiro 1999; D. J. Price & S. Rosswog 2006; B. Giacomazzo et al. 2015; K. Kiuchi et al. 2015; M. Ruiz et al. 2016; R. Ciolfi et al. 2019; R. Aguilera-Miret et al. 2020; C. Palenzuela et al. 2022; M. Chabanov et al. 2023; T. Celora et al. 2025; J. Fields et al. 2026; E. M. Guti´ errez et al. 2025; J. V. Kalinani et al. 2026; A. Neuweiler et al. 2026; I. Rainho et al. 2025; H. H.-Y. Ng et al. 2026; M. Pais et al. 2026; E. M. Guti´ errez et al. 2026; K. Kiuchi et al. 2026). A persistent difficulty in constraining the remnant lifetime is that the relevant magnetic-amplification mechanisms and the correspond-ing angular momentum (AM) transport are often unre-solved or not fully captured in global simulations. This is because effective (primarily neutrino-driven) viscous scales are often orders of magnitude smaller than the dy-namical length-scales of the merger processs (J. Guilet et al. 2015; V. A. Skoutnev & A. M. Beloborodov 2024). This has motivated large-eddy, subgrid, and mean-field approaches for unresolved turbulent amplification, dy-namo action, and AM transport (A. S¸ adowski et al. 2015; M. Shibata & K. Kiuchi 2017; D. Radice 2017; M. Miravet-Ten´ es et al. 2022; C. Palenzuela et al. 2022; M. D. Duez et al. 2025; H. Zhou et al. 2026; W. Cook & S. Bernuzzi 2026), or viscous-hydrodynamic models, particularly for long-term simulations (M. D. Duez et al. 2004; R. Fern´ andez et al. 2019; S. Fujibayashi et al. 2020; P. Chawhan et al. 2026). For a long-lived post-merger remnant, the rotation profile remains differential regardless of the initial conditions and equation of state (EOS) (M. Hanauske et al. 2017; K. Ury¯ u et al. 2017; M. Cassing & L. Rezzolla 2024). One channel responsi-ble for AM transport in the remnant is the MRI-driven 

αΩ dynamo (K. Kiuchi et al. 2024), but it operates only in negative-shear regions, i.e., regions where the angular velocity decreases outward, such as the outer remnant and disk (S. A. Balbus & J. F. Hawley 1991). In regions where the shear profile is increasing, i.e., where the MRI cannot operate, another potential AM transport channel has been proposed: the Tayler–Spruit (TS) dynamo. In this mechanism, non-axisymmetric modes of the Tayler instability (TI) (R. J. Tayler 1973), a pinch-type instability of toroidal magnetic fields, re-generate the poloidal magnetic field, while differential winding amplifies the toroidal component, forming a self-sustained dynamo cycle (H. C. Spruit 2002) in which the winding naturally transports AM (S. L. Shapiro 2000). Inside stars, the TS dynamo is expected to op-erate in differentially rotating, stably stratified interiors and contribute to the spin-down of stellar cores (H. C. Spruit 2002; J. Fuller & L. Ma 2019; L. Ma & J. Fuller 2019). In proto-neutron stars spun up by fallback accre-tion, idealized models suggest that the TS dynamo could amplify magnetar-strength interior magnetic fields (P. Barr` ere et al. 2025). Idealized global MHD simulations of stably stratified spherical shells have begun to demon-strate self-sustained TS dynamo action and the asso-ciated AM transport (L. Petitdemange et al. 2023; P. Barr` ere et al. 2026b). Because resolving this process re-mains computationally infeasible, stellar-evolution mod-els generally represent it as an effective viscosity in the AM equation (B. Paxton et al. 2015, 2018; J. Fuller et al. 2019; V. A. Skoutnev & A. M. Beloborodov 2025). The TS dynamo has also been proposed to oper-ate in the positive-shear core of BNS merger remnants, where it may efficiently amplify the magnetic field and transport AM, potentially shortening the remnant life-time (B. Margalit et al. 2022; A. Reboul-Salze et al. 2025). As a result, novel approaches to effectively mod-eling the TS dynamo in neutron-star merger calculations are needed. We therefore present such an approach: the first global GRMHD simulations of a long-lived BNS merger rem-nant using a subgrid mean-field prescription to model the unresolved TS dynamo (Sec. 2) and quantify its impact on magnetic-field amplification, AM transport, secular evolution, and the emitted signals of long-lived remnants (Sec. 3).

We numerically solve the 2.5D axisymmetric general-relativistic neutrino-dynamo-magnetohydrodynamics equations in a conformally flat spacetime. The initial remnant is modeled using the fully nonlinear outcome of a numerical relativity calculation. For this, we first use the outcome of a three-neutrino-species simulation of an irrotational binary neutron-star merger from H. H.-Y. Ng et al. (2025) (see the setup therein), adopting the SFHo EOS (A. W. Steiner et al. 2013) and a total bi-nary gravitational mass of 2 .5 M⊙. Spin-down of this BNS merger remnant will lead to a stable, uniformly rotating neutron star. The simulation was performed with the full-GR ideal-GRMHD neutrino-radiation code 

FIL-M1 in three-dimensional (3D) Cartesian geome-try (E. R. Most et al. 2019; C. Musolino & L. Rezzolla 2024). FIL is derived from the IllinoisGRMHD code (Z. B. Etienne et al. 2015a). In FIL-M1 , we addition-ally impose a purely poloidal magnetic seed 2 ms be-fore merger, generated by the toroidal vector potential 

Aϕ = A0 max(0 , p − 0.01 pmax ), where p is the pressure, 

pmax is the maximum pressure inside the binary stars, and A0 is chosen such that the maximum poloidal-field strength is Bpol max ∼ 10 14 G at the time of insertion. We evolve the merger until 15 ms postmerger (¯ t0 = 15 ms, where ¯ t = t−tmer is the postmerger time measured from the moment of merger tmer ) and then perform a coordi-nate transformation and ϕ-averaging of the 3D data. At this point, the mass-averaged magnetic-field strength is around 10 15 G. The hydrodynamic, magnetic-field, radi-ation, and metric quantities are transferred to another ideal-GRMHD neutrino-radiation code, Gmunu , which employs the conformal-flatness condition in cylindrical geometry ( R, z, ϕ ) (P. C.-K. Cheong et al. 2021), to continue the long-term axisymmetric simulation (H. H.-Y. Ng et al. 2024b). The spurious oscillatory feature in the temperature profile caused by the ϕ-averaging and gauge difference, as reported in H. H.-Y. Ng et al. (2024b), is removed by remapping the temperature pro-file after metric initialization. We leverage the high efficiency of the axisymmetric approximation (A. T.-L. Lam & M. Shibata 2025), motivated by the nearly ax-isymmetric postmerger remnant after the damping of GW emission (M. Hanauske et al. 2017), together with the conformally flat gravity scheme, which further im-proves efficiency while remaining highly accurate with respect to full general relativity (H. H.-Y. Ng et al. 2024b). The metric variables are updated at intervals of ∆ tmet ≃ 5 × 10 −1 M⊙ (J.-L. Jiang et al. 2025). For the Gmunu continuation, we extend the computa-tional domain to ( R, z ) ∈ [0 , 2500] × [−2500 , 2500] km 2.The simulation employs eight refinement levels, with a finest resolution of 150 m; the finest refinement box cov-ers R ≤ 33 km and |z| ≤ 18 km. The ϕ-averaging can partially disrupt the magnetic-field topology and under-estimate the toroidal magnetic energy because of cancel-lations. We therefore ϕ-average the toroidal field using its root-mean-squared value, with the sign set by the winding-induced equatorial parity. 

Gmunu controls the magnetic-field divergence using a combination of hyperbolic divergence cleaning and multigrid elliptic cleaning (A. Dedner et al. 2002). The subgrid dynamo model is implemented using the scheme of (E. R. Most 2023). Simulations adopt the Harten, Lax, and van Leer approximate Riemann solver (A. Harten et al. 1983), the third-order piecewise-parabolic reconstruction method (P. Colella & P. R. Wood-ward 1984), and the IMEXCB3a time integrator D. Cavaglieri & T. Bewley (2015). For radiation, we em-ploy the energy-averaged (grey) moment scheme (P. C.-K. Cheong et al. 2023, 2024), coupled to the neutrino microphysics library Weakhub (H. H.-Y. Ng et al. 2024a) (see App. B for details).

RESULTS We now provide a detailed discussion of the nonlinear evolution of the TS dynamo inside a neutron-star merger remnant. The preceding evolution of this system is de-scribed in detail in H. H.-Y. Ng et al. (2025), and we focus only on aspects of magnetic-field evolution, AM transport, and the launching of winds from the system. We primarily describe our fiducial ξTS = 1 model but also comment on differences from other configurations. 3.1. Dynamo activation and field amplification

v∞ and entropy, and more neutron-rich composition pri-marily arise from the more diffuse and thicker disk pro-duced by the TS dynamo (Fig. 10). As discussed and shown in Fig. 6, the TS dynamo re-distributes AM and mass from the outer core to the disk (with the disk migrating to higher Mg, disk and Jdisk ). The readjustment of centrifugal support and the out-ward transport of mass expand the disk toward larger cylindrical radii and vertical heights (Fig. 10). At ¯t = 385 ms, the ρ = 10 11 and 10 10 g cm −3 contours in the ξTS = 1 model extend farther in ( R, |z|) by approxi-mately (12 , 8) km and (20 , 18) km, respectively, than in the ξTS = 0 model (see Fig. 10). In the ξTS = 0 case, the moment-based radiation transport produces overes-timated high-Ye matter surrounding the smaller disk, consistent with the results of F. Zappa et al. (2023). For comparison, in the ξTS = 1 case, neutron-rich mat-ter from the outer core is also injected into the disk and quickly attains β-equilibrium through weak interactions, forming a more neutron-rich disk than in the ξTS = 0 case (see Fig. 10). The combined effects in the TS mod-els transport matter outward to form a larger, more weakly bound, magnetized, and centrifugally supported disk, thus producing a larger amount of neutron-rich, fast, and energetic ejecta. This could enhance the post-merger ejecta component of red kilonovae from massive remnants, potentially affecting the conventional picture of predominantly neutron-poor outflows (B. D. Metzger & R. Fern´ andez 2014). Future work will be required to systematically investigate the impact of the TS dynamo on ejecta composition.

CONCLUSIONS We have presented the first global general-relativistic neutrino-radiation magnetohydrodynamics simulations of a binary neutron-star merger remnant incorporat-ing a subgrid mean-field prescription for the unresolved Tayler–Spruit (TS) dynamo. Motivated by recent theo-retical work suggesting that the TS dynamo may operate in the stably stratified, positive-shear cores of merger remnants (B. Margalit et al. 2022; V. A. Skoutnev & A. M. Beloborodov 2024; A. Reboul-Salze et al. 2025), we developed a relativistic dynamo closure (N. Buc-ciantini & L. Del Zanna 2013) that incorporates buoy-ancy, neutrino-viscous suppression of the Tayler instabil-ity, and local nonlinear saturation following our earlier approach (E. R. Most 2023). Combined with an MRI-driven dynamo operating in the negative-shear outer remnant and accretion disk (K. Kiuchi et al. 2024), this provides a unified framework for modeling unresolved magnetic-field amplification throughout the postmerger system, which cannot be captured at Pm ≃ 1 (or in sim-ulations with numerical dissipation (V. A. Skoutnev & A. M. Beloborodov 2024)). Despite being active only in a localized region of the remnant, the inclusion of the TS dynamo has drastic consequences for the long-term evolution of the rem-nant. The dynamo efficiently generates Maxwell stresses that redistribute AM throughout the remnant core. This transport progressively removes differential rota-tion, drives the remnant toward quasi-uniform rotation on secular timescales, and transfers both mass and AM from the outer core into the surrounding accretion disk. As a consequence, the disk becomes more massive, more extended, and more strongly magnetized than in mod-els without TS-dynamo action, providing a substantially larger reservoir for late-time outflows. These changes naturally modify the observable evo-lution of the system. The amplified magnetic field launches a magnetically dominated polar outflow, while the larger and more weakly bound disk produces sub-stantially more neutron-rich ejecta, which could po-tentially drive a stronger red postmerger component of a kilonova. For remnants closer to their stability limit, this additional transport may shorten the rem-nant lifetime and alter the threshold for delayed gravi-14 tational collapse. Based on our simulation for the SFHo EOS (A. W. Steiner et al. 2013) with an initial core mass of 2 .27 M⊙, we estimate that cores with initial masses of 2.42 M⊙ will collapse within a few hundred milliseconds. While our simulations highlight the importance of modeling the TS dynamo in postmerger evolutions, sev-eral caveats remain. The nonlinear saturation of the Tayler instability under neutron-star merger conditions is not yet understood from first principles (V. A. Sk-outnev & A. M. Beloborodov 2024), and better models are needed to accurately characterize the timescale, the instability regions inside the remnant, the nonlinear ge-ometric dependence, and the polar TI-driven branch (P. Barr` ere et al. 2026a,b). Additionally, trapped neu-trino could contribute to the buoyancy calculation, and neutrino-mediated thermal diffusion may weaken the effective thermal restoring force at short radial wave-lengths, enlarging the range of TI-unstable scales (H. C. Spruit 2002; J. Fuller et al. 2019). Ultimately, estab-lishing self-consistent simulations that capture the TS dynamo in merger remnants will require 3D dissipative GRMHD simulations (E. R. Most & J. Noronha 2021) coupled to Boltzmann transport (F. Foucart et al. 2018; A. Offermans et al. 2026) that provides physical neutrino viscosity and diffusivity. Finally, the approach to the TS dynamo and its impact on post-merger remnant evolu-tion might not be unique to neutron stars, as the merger of white dwarfs has also been argued to provide condi-tions susceptible to the TS dynamo (K. J. Shen et al. 2012; J. Schwab et al. 2012; R. Pakmor et al. 2024).
