(Dated: July 31, 2026) Quark stars — hypothetical compact stars made entirely of deconfined quark matter — offer a clean testing ground for gravity beyond general relativity. We study their structure in f (T, T ) grav-ity, a teleparallel theory in which torsion is coupled directly to the trace of the energy–momentum tensor through a single constant coupling. Using the standard MIT bag description of quark matter, we solve the modified stellar structure equations and follow how the mass, radius, compactness, and surface redshift respond as the coupling is varied across its full admissible range. The maximum mass turns out to depend on the coupling in a non-monotonic way: it rises above the general relativ-ity value, peaks near 2.02 solar masses at a moderate positive coupling, and then falls steeply as the coupling approaches a critical value at which the structure equations become singular. The two-solar-mass pulsar constraint is satisfied within a finite window of positive couplings. All configurations on the candidate stable branch satisfy causality and remain below the standard general-relativistic compactness and surface-redshift benchmarks.

I. INTRODUCTION 

Compact stars are the densest objects that we can ac-tually observe, and for this reason they serve a double purpose: they probe the behaviour of matter at densi-ties that no laboratory can reach, and they probe grav-ity in its strong-field regime. Over the past decade the observational situation has improved dramatically. The gravitational-wave event GW170817 provided the first di-rect measurement of the tidal deformability of neutron-star matter [1, 2], radio timing of PSR J0740+6620 es-tablished that any acceptable model must support at least two solar masses [3], and NICER X-ray observations added a simultaneous mass–radius measurement for the same pulsar [4, 5]. At the extremes of the mass distribu-tion sit the “black widow” pulsar PSR J0952 −0607 with 

M ≈ 2.35 M⊙ [6], the 2 .6 M⊙ secondary of GW190814, whose nature is still debated [7], and the unusually light central compact object in HESS J1731 −347 with

M ≈ 0.77 M⊙ [8]. Together these observations form a set of targets that any theory of dense matter, or of gravity, must confront. One long-standing possibility for the interior of such objects is quark matter. If strange quark matter is the true ground state of hadronic matter, as conjectured by Witten and by Farhi and Jaffe [9, 10], then self-bound quark stars can exist [11, 12]. The simplest quantitative description of such stars is the MIT bag model [13], in which confinement is represented by a constant vacuum pressure B. More refined treatments start from pertur-bative QCD at high density, a program that goes back to the pioneering calculations of Freedman and McLerran, Baluni, and Toimela [14–17] and has now reached partial N3LO accuracy [18–23]. Interpolations between the per-turbative regime and stellar densities give quark-matter equations of state that can be confronted directly with pulsar and gravitational-wave data [24–27]. At the den-sities relevant for stellar cores, quark matter is also ex-pected to pair, most symmetrically in the colour-flavour-locked phase [28–32], and quark or hybrid configurations have been proposed as explanations of both GW190814 and HESS J1731 −347 [33–36]. Quark stars have like-wise been used as test beds for modified gravity, for in-stance in 4D Einstein–Gauss–Bonnet gravity [37] and in Rastall–Rainbow gravity [38]. Related programmes have arXiv:2607.27365v1 [gr-qc] 29 Jul 2026 2treated quark stars in the curvature-based counterpart 

f (R, T ) gravity [39] and in its matter-Lagrangian exten-sion f (R, L m, T ) gravity [40]; the present work extends this line of trace-coupled constructions to the torsional sector. On the gravity side, teleparallel theories offer an at-tractive alternative starting point. In the teleparallel equivalent of general relativity the dynamical variable is the tetrad and gravity is encoded in torsion rather than curvature; promoting the torsion scalar T to an arbitrary function f (T ) gives a modified theory whose field equa-tions remain second order [41–44]. Relativistic stars are known to exist in f (T ) gravity [45], although the choice of tetrad requires care: for nonlinear f (T ) a diagonal tetrad in spherical coordinates generates spurious constraints, and one must instead work with a “good” (rotated) tetrad or, equivalently, in the covariant formulation with a nontrivial spin connection [46, 47]. A further extension couples the geometry directly to matter. On the curva-ture side this leads to f (R, T ) gravity [48]; its torsional counterpart, f (T, T ) gravity, was introduced by Harko, Lobo, Ot´ alora, and Saridakis [49], where T is the trace of the energy–momentum tensor. The trace coupling makes the theory non-conservative, ∇μΘμν̸ = 0, which opens qualitatively new phenomenology. The f (T, T )framework has since been studied in many settings: late-time cosmology and dynamical-systems analyses [50–54], gravitational baryogenesis and Big Bang nucleosynthesis constraints [55, 56], thick branes [57], black-hole ther-modynamics [58], wormholes [59–61], gravastars [62], charged fluids with gravitational decoupling [63], and anisotropic strange-star models built on prescribed met-ric ansatze [64–66]. For isotropic compact stars obtained by actually in-tegrating the stellar structure equations, however, the available results are limited. Pace and Levi Said de-rived a working TOV model for quark stars in f (T, T )gravity and solved it with the MIT bag model [67], and later treated neutron stars in a perturbative scheme [68]. Their quark-star study, however, retained a nonzero cosmological constant, adopted the strange-quark-mass-corrected bag parameter ω = 0 .28, and explored only a few negative values of the coupling through single-star mass profiles. What is still missing is a nonperturbative treatment with the conformal bag equation of state, a systematic maximum-mass scan over the full admissible range of the coupling — including its positive branch — and a direct confrontation with the modern observational bounds listed above. That is what we do here. In this paper we derive the modified Tolman– Oppenheimer–Volkoff (TOV) equations for the linear model f (T, T ) = T + βT with matter Lagrangian Lm =

p, solve them numerically for the conformal MIT bag equation of state, and scan the coupling over the full admissible range β ∈ [−10 , 12 .25]. Our field equations were cross-checked with independent computer-algebra software. The central result is that the maximum grav-itational mass Mmax (β) is non-monotonic: it peaks at 2.021 M⊙ near β ≈ 3.10 and collapses as β → 4π,where the pressure equation develops a singular denomi-nator. The 2 M⊙ pulsar constraint then selects the win-dow β ∈ (0 , ∼ 5.5). The paper is organised as follows. Section II sets up the action and the field equations. Section III derives the modified TOV system and states the equation of state. Section IV collects the stability criteria, and Sec. V de-scribes the numerical method and its validation. The re-sults are presented in Sec. VI and discussed in Sec. VII. Throughout we use geometrised units G = c = 1 and the metric signature ( −, +, +, +).

> IV. STABILITY ANALYSIS

Within the one-parameter equilibrium sequence, the onset of instability is identified by the first maximum of M (ρc) [71]. Configurations with dM/dρ c > 0 define the candidate stable branch identified by the turning-point criterion; those with dM/dρ c < 0 beyond Mmax 

are unstable. The full radial perturbation equations in 

f (T, T ) gravity have not yet been derived, and in a non-conservative matter–geometry coupling neither the pulsation equations nor the applicability of the general-relativistic turning-point theorem can be assumed to carry over unchanged. The criterion is therefore used here as a diagnostic rather than as a proof of dynami-cal stability, and a complete Sturm–Liouville pulsation analysis is reserved for future work. VII. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS 

We have studied quark stars in f (T, T ) = T + βT

gravity with the conformal MIT bag EOS at B =60 MeV fm −3. The maximum mass Mmax (β) is non-monotonic in β: it peaks at 2 .021 M⊙ for β ≈ 3.10, ex-ceeding the GR value by ∆ M = +0 .057 M⊙ (+2 .9%), and is suppressed toward the singular surface β = 4 π.The conventional 2 M⊙ pulsar requirement [3] is satisfied 7for β ∈ (0 , ∼ 5.5), so a moderate positive trace coupling is compatible with this observational threshold within a finite interval. The peak mass Mmax = 2 .021 M⊙,however, does not reach the central mass estimate of PSR J0952 −0607, M ≃ 2.35 M⊙ [6]. Accommodating such a high mass would require either a stiffer quark-matter equation of state, additional microphysical effects, or an extension of the present one-parameter model; we note this as a direction for future work rather than a re-sult established here. All configurations on the candidate stable branch are causal, satisfy Γ > 4/3 analytically, and remain below the standard GR compactness and surface-redshift benchmarks. The non-monotonic behaviour can-not be traced to a sign change of the factor (16 π−7β): for 

ω = 1 /3 the denominator of Eq. (19) reduces to 43 (4 π−β), and the pressure-gradient coefficient (30) grows mono-tonically in magnitude with β > 0. The turnover instead reflects the competition between the coupling’s effect on the pressure gradient, on the metric potential, and on the algebraic closure, and is established here numerically rather than analytically. Some limitations should be kept in mind. The full ra-dial pulsation equations in f (T, T ) gravity have not been derived; our stability statements rest on the turning-point criterion [71] and on the analytic bound Γ >

4/3 [72], and a complete Sturm–Liouville analysis would put them on a firmer footing. The tidal Love number k2

and the dimensionless deformability Λ, which are directly constrained by GW170817 [1, 2], remain to be computed in this framework. As in the original compact-star treat-ment of Pace and Levi Said [67], the terminal value of the integrated mass function has been identified with the gravitational mass of the star. An explicit analysis of the surface junction conditions would provide a useful inde-pendent test of this prescription, and is left for future work. Finally, more realistic quark-matter descriptions — including perturbative-QCD corrections [24–26] and colour-superconducting phases [28, 30, 31] — together with a scan over the bag constant B, would broaden the parameter space and sharpen the comparison with ob-jects such as HESS J1731 −347 and the GW190814 sec-ondary [7, 8]. In summary, quark stars in f (T, T ) = T + βT grav-ity with the conformal MIT bag EOS display a non-monotonic maximum-mass profile bounded above by the singular surface at β = 4 π, with a peak of Mmax =2.021 M⊙ at β ≈ 3.10 and observational viability for 

β ∈ (0 , ∼ 5.5). These results establish f (T, T ) gravity as a viable framework for massive quark stars and moti-vate further studies of tidal deformability, radial stability, and extended EOS families.
